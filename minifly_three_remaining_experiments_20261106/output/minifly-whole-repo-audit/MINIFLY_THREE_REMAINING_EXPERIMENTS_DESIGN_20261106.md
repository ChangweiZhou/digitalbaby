# MiniFly designs for the three remaining experiments

**Proposed development protocols · November 6, 2026**

These experiments test three separate claims. A frozen pretrained encoder may make experience more reusable. Downscaling during rest may improve late transfer in modular addition. A ring-shaped working state may help a learner use a counting curriculum on untaught sums. Each can run independently. The latest recurrent experiment motivates the design choices here. All 24 histories acquired literal taught prompts and all missed the transfer floor when only the edge-row order changed. The attractor and arithmetic curriculum below are new conditions. [Claude’s review](<../../../../Downloads/MiniFly past-work review for Mr. Zhou.docx>) · [Recurrent worked-examples report](../minifly-recurrent-worked-examples/OFFICIAL_REPORT.md)

| Experiment | Intervention | Decisive comparison |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Frozen pretrained encoder | Learn a shared byte encoder before a local-learning life | Correctly pretrained vs target-shuffled pretraining and random initialization, with the same downstream local update |
| Downscaling during rest | Periodically shrink the shared model weights after taught acquisition plateaus | Pulsed shrinkage vs zero shrinkage and smooth shrinkage with the same nominal cumulative factor |
| Attractor with worked counting | Add a working state with ring geometry and teach counting through the byte stream | Ring vs degree-matched rewired topology, each under answer-only, shuffled and aligned worked teaching |

## Experiment 1 Frozen pretraining and local learning

**Question.** Does a learned frozen representation improve transfer to a new world when only a local readout can adapt? V207 established a prose prediction effect in another learner. The proposed experiment tests whether a developmental representation changes subsequent learning behavior. [V207 results](../minifly-pretraining/OFFICIAL_RESULTS.json)

**Development before birth.** Use a 64-unit causal GRU with a learned 16-dimensional byte embedding. Give it 1,048,576 next-byte targets in fixed 128-byte windows, half from the locally cached V207 training prose and half from independently generated synthetic sequences with repeated motifs and variable distractors. The synthetic generator uses a different grammar from the graph episodes. Exclude A and B graph records, graph answers, reachability traces and any renderer-derived features from pretraining. Hash the text windows, synthetic corpus, generators and splits. Train on the true next byte in one arm and permute targets across distinct windows within the same source and target position in the matched sham. The sham keeps the original inputs, byte marginals and optimizer budget. Fix the checkpoint at the target cap.

| Birth condition | Encoder during life |
| --- | --- |
| Aligned pretraining | Frozen pretrained GRU and embedding. Discard the developmental prediction head. |
| Shuffled-target pretraining | Identical architecture and development budget, with shuffled targets. Freeze and discard its head in the same way. |
| Random recurrent | The paired initial GRU and embedding, frozen without development. |
| Stock sensor anchor | Archived fixed FE/KC encoder with a seed-fixed projection to 64 dimensions. This anchors the comparison against the historical sensory route. |

All arms use a new zero-initialized 257-way affine readout. Apply the same online output-error rule to each delivered byte and EOS. For each output neuron, its update depends on that neuron's prediction error and its presynaptic feature vector. Use the same per-vector unit-norm cap and a fixed learning rate of 0.03 in every arm. Reset context at record boundaries and preserve the readout across worlds. Hash frozen tensors before and after life. Gradients are used in the separate developmental reference only.

**Life and controls.** Use three paired development seeds and both opposite opcode histories, with the revised raw graph fixtures. In A, expose 2,048 answer episodes. For each birth, create a matched A control with identical prefixes and timing, assigning opposite laws to balanced halves of the semantic keys and swapping the assignment at each epoch. This removes a stable prior law. In B, both copies receive the same 2,048 episodes with fresh actors and location bindings, using the same opcode vocabulary and consistent law as the main A history. Score fresh edge-row renderings of B keys after 0, 128, 512 and 2,048 B episodes. Use a separate diagnostic panel of A keys after B and record outputs with native EOS. The readout gets feedback only in the teaching streams.

**Decision.** The primary endpoint is the normalized trapezoidal area of held-presentation exact accuracy over B exposures. In each seed and law, require aligned pretraining to beat both recurrent birth controls and the stock sensor anchor by five percentage points on that area. For a forward-transfer claim, also require its consistent-A life to beat its inconsistent-A life by five points. Report the stock anchor, first-answer accuracy at B zero, and A retention separately. If the local head fails to reach 52/64 exact on literal A keys, report that acquisition failure before interpreting a weak B curve. Use the same 64 B probe keys at every checkpoint and record which have received feedback so far. The zero-dose probe tests unseen B keys. Later scores mix first use and retrieval across presentation changes, and the curve measures acquisition speed. Establish E3 separately on withheld graph topologies with a causal prior-teaching control.

**Budget and preflight.** At most 6,291,456 pretraining targets across the three paired seeds and two trained births. The local roster has 48 histories and at most 196,608 episodes, or 10,813,440 delivered symbols at 55 per episode. Before launch, test byte and EOS parity, frozen-weight invariance, normalization, local updates, exact/canonical exposure checks and no-feedback generation. Confirm the matched A streams have the intended law frequencies. Count forward computation and training separately for development and life.

## Experiment 2 Downscaling after acquisition on mod 97

**Question.** Does periodic downscaling produce a late rise in untaught modular sums, and does the timing of shrinkage matter? This tests the proposed weight intervention. Sleep is the motivating analogy.

**Task and model.** Train on raw two-digit byte records such as `24+81=08`, followed by native EOS. The fixed-width grammar is shared across all arms. Supply no parsed operands, magnitude features, arithmetic intermediate states or residue labels other than the observed answer bytes. Split the 4,753 unordered operand groups into 2,376 taught and 2,377 held groups. Keep both operand orders together. Balance by residue and by carry, and include 48 diagonal groups in the taught split, giving 4,704 taught and 4,705 held ordered prompts. Reserve half of the held groups as the sealed endpoint. Use a learned eight-dimensional byte embedding in an eight-slot ordered window, followed by two 128-unit tanh layers and a linear 257-way head. Add the same archived FE/KC features and trainable linear residual readout to every arm. Use ordinary synaptic summation in this branch. No key search or value retrieval is required.

**Common acquisition and fork.** Use three seeds with independent splits. Each seed trains once with Adam and zero weight decay until two consecutive fixed probes show at least 95% exact on taught prompts. Probe every 512 updates, and stop acquisition at 4,096 updates. If a seed already exceeds 50% held exact on the monitoring half at the fork, record early transfer and exclude it from the delayed-rise claim. At the shared checkpoint fork model, optimizer and data cursor into these arms.

| Arm | Treatment during 4,096 further updates |
| --- | --- |
| No downscaling | Advance the same stream without shrinkage. |
| Periodic downscaling | Every 128 updates, multiply the shared weight matrices by 0.98. |
| Smooth downscaling | Multiply those same matrices by \(0.98^{1/128}\) after each update. |

Keep the optimizer moments, biases and sparse residual weights outside the intervention. The smooth and periodic arms match nominal multiplicative shrinkage over each interval. Because learning continues between multiplications, their learned states can differ. No replay or extra examples occur during a pulse. Report each branch's trainable weight norms and evaluate the shared and sparse paths separately by masking the other path at inference. Those masks are diagnostics.

**Decision.** A delayed-rise result requires the early taught plateau, at least a 20-point improvement over the fork on sealed held exact, a final advantage of at least ten points over no downscaling, and taught exact still at or above 95%. For a specific timing claim, the periodic arm must also beat the smooth arm by five points. Require the direction and thresholds in all three qualifying seeds for a positive development claim. Save the fork checkpoint and score it on the sealed half only after all final branch checkpoints are frozen. If fewer than three reach the fork, report the partial results without promotion. Evaluate both-order exact, held first byte, wrap and carry subsets, and own EOS. Audit ordered and canonical lookup, nearest taught operand pairs, frequency and carry-free heuristics. Use an exact modular oracle to check scoring. Conditional on a positive result, rerun the same winning schedule with a fixed within-split permutation of the taught labels and the same prompt sequence. This preserves output frequencies and tests dependence on coherent arithmetic teaching. Require a ten-point advantage over that teaching control in every qualifying seed for the narrow E3 claim. Call a late rise grokking only when the trained-versus-held curves show the preregistered separation. The experiment ends with immediate transfer on the finite modular task.

**Budget and preflight.** Main maximum is 49,152 updates at batch 64, or 3,145,728 presentations and 28,311,552 delivered symbols. A conditional teaching-control replicate can add up to 1,572,864 presentations. Check split leakage, matched batch order, correct shrinkage sites, native EOS and fork equivalence. Time complete updates and full scoring before allocating the main run. Record when a seed stops before the fork and retain its acquisition curve.

## Experiment 3 Attractor topology with worked counting

**Question.** Does a ring-shaped working state improve exact addition from raw digits after counting and worked teaching, compared with an equally sized recurrent control? This is the remaining part of the attractor proposal. The previous graph run used a generic GRU and a different teaching signal. [Completed graph comparison](../minifly-recurrent-worked-examples/OFFICIAL_REPORT.md)

**Topology comparison.** Give both reference models the same fixed byte embedding, 32 working units, sparse sensory branch and 257-way head. The working state uses a gated local recurrent transition with self-connections, four excitatory neighbor inputs and one shared activity-inhibition term. The ring connects each unit to the two neighbors on each side. Generate a connected, non-isomorphic, degree-four control by edge swaps and keep equal edge counts, signs and initial gains. Use a learned per-unit sigmoid gate to interpolate between the previous state and bounded rectification of the byte input plus recurrent drive. The drive sums self-excitation and the mean of the four neighbors, then subtracts the same global mean-activity term. Match the transition equation and parameter count and freeze the graph assignments. Neither model receives an origin, digit-to-location map, a prescribed shift per count mark or a numeric decode. The ring supplies the spatial topology as a declared inductive bias. Reset working state at episode boundaries. Use the same byte-gradient training procedure in both references. If frozen ring states lack distinct localized states that persist under zero input, report the architecture as a local recurrent ring rather than attributing behavior to an attractor.

**Counting and worked teaching.** In every arm, include the same counting episodes mapping tally streams of length 0 through 18 to ordinary decimal answer bytes. Addition records use the digits 0–9 in two fixed-width fields, with an ordinary decimal answer and EOS. Teach all ordered pairs with at least one operand at most four. Hold every pair with both operands at least five, including both orders. This gives 75 taught and 25 held ordered prompts. Reuse counts and digit tokens across groups. Report separately the held sums 10–13, which occur as taught addition answers, and 14–18, which appear as count labels but not as taught addition answers.

Cross both model topologies with three teaching arms. The worked arm learns a separate fixed-width auxiliary trace showing the two operand tally sequences in padded fields with a separator. The trace contains no intermediate or final decimal results. The shuffled arm re-pairs the same traces across different semantic keys. The answer-only arm sees the same auxiliary frames with trace-response loss masked. Use one count, one addition and one auxiliary record per training group. Match the order of count and addition examples, answer loss and update counts. At evaluation, provide the ordinary digit prompt only. The learner must emit the answer and EOS on its own.

**Exposure and mechanism audit.** Canonicalize commuted pairs and scan all answer, count and trace records for held equations. Audit whether a table keyed by a held operand pair can extract its answer from a recorded trace or count episode without executing a learned operation. Count-to-digit associations are intentionally available. Log explicitly which sums are seen in that form. Run exact/canonical lookup, trace-replay coverage, nearest-pair, most frequent and carry-free comparators. The exact arithmetic oracle checks rendering and scoring. Also record whether the working state maintains distinct localized activity, whether a tally byte moves that activity in a repeatable direction, and whether temporarily suppressing the working-state output selectively removes a held advantage. Those checks are required to credit an attractor mechanism. The behavioral comparison remains interpretable as a topology comparison if the checks fail.

**Decision.** Use three paired seeds and score after 2,048, 8,192 and 16,384 groups, with a fixed cap for every arm. Require at least 60/75 taught exact and 20/25 held exact in each seed for a positive behavior screen. A topology advantage requires at least five more held items than the rewired arm with the same teaching. A worked-teaching advantage requires at least five more than both teaching controls on the same topology. Report both-order exact on distinct operands. If a condition clears the behavioral screen, run a count-only attribution control with the same chronological inputs and loss masking on addition and auxiliary responses. If that control also succeeds, the common counting and input experience sufficed under the control. Compare its score with its own frozen birth and report the topology effect within that curriculum. Otherwise require the passing arm to beat the control by five held answers in each seed before attributing reuse to the addition teaching. Continue the passing histories through 2,048 matched non-arithmetic byte episodes, then re-score the original held prompts without feedback and score fresh wrappers separately. Require the held floor to survive on the original prompts. Report fresh-wrapper accuracy as a separate presentation result. An E3 claim is confined to this bounded task and curriculum. The gradient-trained reference establishes feasibility for a later local-rule test.

**Budget and preflight.** Main maximum is 294,912 training groups and at most 18,874,368 delivered symbols with a 64-symbol limit per group. The attribution and continuation for one passing condition add at most 55,296 groups. Lock count frequencies, mask checks, trace target marginals, input exposure and the topology control before training. Require deterministic frozen scoring, native EOS, and comparable numerical stability in the two recurrent graphs. Measure real update and inference time before releasing the roster.

## Run discipline

Each proposal starts with a separate engineering block. Freeze sources, seeds, task splits, decision thresholds and a measured resource cap before development. Keep all scheduled controls and record abstentions, failures and raw output sequences. Launch order can be independent. The downscaling experiment is the most self-contained. Frozen pretraining addresses transfer with local adaptation, and the attractor experiment has a distinct mechanism check that should pass engineering qualification before a full training run.
