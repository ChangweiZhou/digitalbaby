**Authorization.** Mr. Xu's message of 11 November 2026 asked for three steps:

1. Finish the every-byte births (E18b).
2. Run a clean control of local prediction heads in which only the gradient between banks changes (E18c).
3. Test targets on different timescales (E23).

**Model.** The `bank2` learner of `arch17.py`: two stacked fixed-rate banks, embeddings out of decay, sign-feedback rule T. It has 1,782,418 parameters, or 2,176,915 with the far head of N1/F4/F4s. That is roughly 48–58 times V181's 37,313-parameter design, so this is **not a mini learner**. Births are 24,576 updates on the four mod-89 development operations, with 0–12 distractor letters. Scores are whole answers including EOS, on sealed panels at k = 12. Hard = (add89 + sub89)/2; max/min = (max89 + min89)/2.

## Answers

1. **E18b. Every-byte targets do not rescue B3, and they break B2.**
   - With a loss on every byte, B3 does learn its input code: operator and distractor-letter embeddings move 0.50–0.71 of a row norm, against 0.00 in plain B3.
   - Answers stay at the floor: hard 0.2–1.7, max/min 1.2–2.8.
   - The same loss takes a working B2 from hard 66–75 to 0.3–0.7, and from max/min 94–97 to 21–23. That max/min is below the 49–52 that copying an operand would score.
   - So under sign feedback at this dose, every-byte targets swamp the answer signal.
2. **E18c. Passing error from the second bank to the first is worth points, and the gain survives a life.**
   - Relay (R2) differs from B2 only by opening the single read from bank 1 into bank 2's gradient path. Heads, losses, parameters and the credit-through-time rule are all identical.
   - R2 births beat B2 by 7.4, 18.8 and 12.5 hard points, and are ahead at every checkpoint.
   - After the life's interference and later teaching (block 6), R2 leads by 12.5, 13.6 and 14.8 F1 points. Retention (F1 after block 6 divided by F1 after block 2) is 0.96–0.98, against 0.89–0.96.
   - Cost is about 9% more CPU at birth and 7% more in the life.
   - Bank 1's own head does not get better (PC.5 misses). The gain shows up in the main answer.
3. **E23. The timescale question was not answered.**
   - The matched base H0 trains bank 1's head on every next byte. That alone collapses the birth: hard 5.5–10.0 against B2's 66–75.
   - Adding the bank-2 head (N1, F4, F4s) takes hard to about 1.
   - Under the decision table committed in advance, P23.3 and P23.4 missing means "extra every-byte heads under sign feedback damage the bank they train", so the contrasts cannot be read as timescale tests.
   - F4, N1 and F4s are equal within one point. The far heads stay below the structural baseline: they never learn their own targets.

## Checks made after the runs

- **The same-host partners reproduce E18 exactly.** Every sealed score at every checkpoint, and every weight tensor, is identical to the E18 births for B2 and for B3. Only the `.pt` file bytes differ, from serialization. CPU per birth is 28% lower here (4,109 against 5,700) for B2 and 23% lower for B3.
- **Isolation between banks holds in the run products.**
  - In B2-all, H0, N1, F4 and F4s of the same seed, the embedding, bank 1 and bank 1's head are bit-identical. Only bank 2 and the main head differ.
  - So bank 1 is trained by its own head alone, as the `local` design says. Embedding movement and head-1 scores are identical across these cells for that reason.
  - It also locates H0's collapse. H0 differs from B2 only in bank 1's head target. So the damage enters through the representation bank 1 hands upward, not through anything trained in bank 2.

## What went wrong

- **E23's matched base was not checked before E23 was committed.** H0 (head 1 on every next byte) had no dry or pilot evidence of being harmless at full length. The 512-update dry runs tested only that the machinery ran. E18b's B2-all collapse, known after wave 2, already pointed to the danger, but the plan was run as registered.
  - The waves after H0 cost about 48,700 CPU-s (N1, F4, F4s) and produced only a damaged-base result.
  - Lesson: a cell that serves as the reference for a contrast needs its own full-length sanity result before the contrast is launched.
- **P18b.3 is formally "misses" for an uninformative reason.** B2-all fell to B3-all's level instead of B3-all staying below a working B2-all. The prediction assumed B2-all would work.
- **Monitoring.** Two of my wait loops hit the 2-hour tool limit during the lives waves. That stopped the watcher only; the runs and the driver were unaffected.

## What this settles, and the next decision

- **Settled at development level (three seeds, one host, internal review only):** opening only the bank 1 → bank 2 error path improves both births and lives, on new problems and after interference, for about 8% more compute.
  - This is the first clean isolation of the mechanism behind E18's local-head gain. It is evidence that the gain comes from error passing between banks, not from extra heads.
- **Not settled:** whether a later target helps. To ask that question, the near/far contrast needs a base that works.
  - One option: keep both head 1 and the main head on answer bytes (as in B2 or R2), and add only a bank-2 head on prompt bytes at a small loss weight, checked first at full length for no damage.
  - That would be a new design. It needs Mr. Xu's authorization and its own draft-audit-test cycles.
