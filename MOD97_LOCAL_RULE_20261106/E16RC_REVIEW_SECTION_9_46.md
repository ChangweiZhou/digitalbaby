**9.46 E16-RC (cloud session, 9 November 2026): the raw-byte reservoir's capacity curve is flat from 16,384 slots up.** This section covers `E16RC_REPORT.md`, `exp45/SUMMARY_E16RC.md` and nine lives on the standing seeds. The same label-free, verbatim-byte reservoir as E16-R3 was run at 16,384, 32,768 and 131,072 slots (0.26, 0.52 and 2.1 MB at 16.0 bytes per slot), against E16-R3's 65,536 slots, the every-entry arm and the twin. After block 6, the ratio to the twin on held compositions was 0.82 / 0.76 / 0.81 at 16,384, 0.85 / 0.85 / 0.78 at 32,768, 0.86 / 0.81 / 0.85 at 65,536 and 0.87 / 0.85 / 0.85 at 131,072. The mod-97 and mod-73 ratios stay at 0.83–0.98 and 0.92–0.99 at every capacity. Of the predictions, P16RC.4 (bytes per slot) holds, and P16RC.3's bound of about 20,500 distinct composition strings under 24,000 holds. P16RC.1 (monotone) misses in every seed, with reversals of up to 3.7 points, within the seed-to-seed spread. P16RC.2 misses because 16,384 slots already meets the composition gate in two seeds. P16RC.3's saturation clause misses in seed 116089, where 131,072 slots sits 3.6 points under the every-entry arm; the label-free share of compositions in replay (about 16% of slots) is a candidate for that difference, though this job did not test it. On these seeds, a quarter of a megabyte of observed bytes carries most of the retention, and 1.05 MB is the smallest budget here that met the gate in all three. The curve is single-run per seed and not resolved beyond that. A log incident caused by the session's own git autostash cost one log its final line; all receipts are intact.
