# Fly teaching circuits: what can be reduced, and what the physiology identifies

**13 September 2026 · Follow-up to the V55 anatomical audit**

**A much smaller sample can recover the anatomical teaching-address operator. The available physiology supports type-specific, feedback-dependent responses, but does not identify a complete teaching rule.** The strongest new compression result is that **128 proportionally stratified training KCs retain 97.5% of the full training set's predictive improvement** on 391 held-out KCs. This is evidence for reducing the data needed to estimate an anatomical operator—not evidence that a 128-KC brain can learn and behave like a fly.

I refined the suggested assignments around measurements that are actually identifiable. Zenodo was unavailable; retries stopped at the user's instruction. The publisher's source-data workbooks and the authors' model repository provided a productive workaround. BANC spatial replication remains **unassessed**, rather than a negative result. [Manual links and exact data request](MANUAL_DOWNLOADS.md).

## Results at a glance

| Question | Result | Interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| Can subsampling recover the compact address table? | 128 stratified KCs: 1.108 bits/site; all 1,524 training KCs: 1.069; KC-wide baseline: 2.632 | Strong within-hemibrain compression of anatomical prediction |
| Is stratification useful? | Retains all 13 KC subtypes; uniform sampling at 128 retains 9.0 on average | Protect rare types; no large universal accuracy advantage over uniform sampling |
| Does learned MBON feedback affect DAN responses? | Early PPL1-α3 CS+ response increase is 7.26 Hz larger in control than MBON-blocked flies at 5 min | Feedback contributes to the early response change |
| Is simple addition adequate? | Mean odor+shock residual intervals include zero in all four tested cohorts | Compatible with addition; equivalence and universal sufficiency are not established |
| Is multiplication required? | Product correction helps γ1pedc/OCT, but results vary across odors/types; calibrated linear gains also help | No transferable event×feedback mechanism identified |
| Is direct DAN recurrence necessary? | Available windowed responses do not identify it; the published model's rate network has no direct DAN→DAN term | Slow history dependence must be distinguished from instantaneous recurrence |
| Has BANC local addressing replicated? | No coordinate subset obtained; authenticated API access unavailable, public file needs a large scan | Prior type-level replication stands; spatial replication is pending |
| Does a constant common teacher test local addressing? | All normalized mixtures give exactly the same result | The proposed common-scalar “cheapest gate” is mathematically uninformative |

## 1. Recovering an address operator from a small KC sample

I froze the earlier hemibrain analysis and its original KC folds. The new analysis used fold 0 as a fixed test set: **391 KCs and 32,988 KC→MBON site-partner rows**. The remaining **1,524 KCs**, spanning 13 supplied subtypes, formed the sampling pool. All sampled KCs retain their observed output-site relationships; synapses were not independently thinned.

The anatomical prediction is still

\[
q(d\mid k,o)\propto f(k,d)R(d,o),
\]

with a 20-DAN-type × 33-MBON-type table. The target is the **nearest recorded DAN terminal type on the same KC**, not measured dopamine release or plasticity. Each test KC's full recorded DAN input mixture remains available. Thus this experiment measures how well a small training sample estimates the shared table; it does not delete the remaining neurons from a dynamical circuit.

For each of six budgets, I ran 40 uniform, 40 proportional-by-subtype, and 40 balanced-by-subtype samples: **720 experiments**. Stratified samples reserve at least one KC per subtype, then allocate the remaining budget proportionally or approximately equally, subject to population caps. Inverse-inclusion weights correct stratified counts back to the population target, rescaled to the KC budget. The pseudocount remains one per table entry. No test labels select the samples.

| Training KCs | Proportional stratification: mean loss | Sampling 2.5–97.5 percentile | Full-training improvement retained |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 32 | 1.240 | 1.177–1.349 | 89.1% |
| 64 | 1.159 | 1.103–1.253 | 94.3% |
| 128 | 1.108 | 1.064–1.189 | 97.5% |
| 256 | 1.090 | 1.045–1.136 | 98.7% |
| 512 | 1.079 | 1.032–1.124 | 99.4% |
| All 1,524 | 1.069 | — | 100% |

Retained improvement is `(baseline loss − sampled loss)/(baseline loss − full-training loss)`. These intervals describe random sampling on **one fixed test set**; they are not uncertainty across specimens. Occasional samples outperform full training on this particular test set, so 100% is not an upper bound on an individual draw. This follow-up was chosen after the earlier anatomical effect was discovered and is not a preregistered independent confirmation.

![KC sampling accuracy and subtype coverage](figures/sampling.png)

At 128 KCs, uniform sampling is close: **1.120 bits/site and 96.8% retained improvement**. Its sampled subtype count ranges down to seven, whereas both stratified designs always cover all thirteen. Equal allocation to rare and common subtypes costs some population-weighted accuracy at the smallest budgets, even after weighting. **Proportional sampling with a rare-type minimum is the most defensible starting design here; its main advantage is coverage.** The accuracy distributions overlap substantially.

For a smaller fly-inspired circuit, this supports preserving the small DAN/MBON interface and using representative whole-KC profiles. It does not yet justify subsampling every neuron class at the same fraction. Sensory coding, external inputs, feedback stability, spike variability, plasticity and behavior still need separate conservation tests. The 128-KC figure is an estimation budget for this operator, not a discovered minimum brain size. [All draws](results/address_sampling_draws.csv), [sampling design](results/address_sampling_design.json), [script](address_sampling.py).

## 2. Physiology: what the downloaded data can answer

The package now includes **15 publisher source workbooks plus `Imaging_24hr_data.xlsx`**, covering 57 sheets. I extracted **2,112 numerical measurement cells** with exact workbook/sheet/cell references. These are not 2,112 independent flies. I checked 161 reconstructed column means against the publisher's means; all agree within numerical precision. The original model workbook contains **86 nonmissing mean response values**, with SEMs, rather than fly-level voltage traces. [Source data and article](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-024-07819-w), [authors' repository](https://github.com/schnitzer-lab/Luo_Huang_2024_MB_model).

The source sheets support repeated-session contrasts within a fly, separate treatment cohorts, and same-fly trials of odor, shock and their combination. They do **not** supply a verified joint time-series table of the five DAN types and six MBON types. Some experimental preparations recorded more than one type, but no universal shared animal/trial key is established in these extracted spreadsheets. “Fly #1” in one type's sheet cannot be joined to “Fly #1” in another type's sheet.

Consequently, fitting the suggested M0–M4 equations as if every `m(t)` and `d(t)` were measured together would produce unjustified coefficients. I instead performed identifiable response-combination tests, intervention contrasts, and validation of frozen published predictions. `DAN_DYNAMIC_SPEC.json` records missing feedback/recurrent coefficients as `null`, with reasons; it does not replace missing data with a guessed matrix.

### 2.1 Type-specific event responses and history dependence

Shock-induced response changes differ markedly across the five recorded PPL1 types: mean changes are approximately **27.85, 29.47, 0.25, 11.38 and 0.78 Hz**, respectively, for γ1pedc, γ2α′1, α′2α2, α3 and α′3. Sucrose suppresses several types and has a different profile. Conditioning changes are also uneven across types and sessions. These measurements require a representation that permits heterogeneous response changes and suppression below baseline; they do not make negative firing rates biologically meaningful. [Event response table](results/unconditioned_event_responses.csv), [all conditioning contrasts](results/conditioning_changes.csv).

A descriptive compression check on the five-type × five-odor response matrix used held-out flies **within each type's cohort**, with independent fold assignments across types. Rank-one prediction has RMSE **5.92 Hz**, rank two **5.10 Hz**, and the full rank-five mean table **5.18 Hz**. This suggests limited-dimensional response structure for these odors. It does not establish two universal teaching channels: the selected stimulus set is small, the rank comparison is exploratory, and it measures activity prediction rather than anatomy or learning. [Rank comparison](results/odor_rank_cv.csv).

### 2.2 Feedback blockade supplies stronger evidence than a cross-cohort regression

For PPL1-α3 responses to attractive CS+ odors, I calculated each fly's change from its own pre-training response, then compared the control and MBON-γ1pedc-output-blocked cohorts (12 flies each):

| Session | Control change | Blockade change | Control − blockade, 95% bootstrap interval |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Mid-training | +10.52 Hz | −0.11 Hz | **+10.62 [5.74, 15.64] Hz** |
| 5 min | +9.86 Hz | +2.59 Hz | **+7.26 [3.08, 11.34] Hz** |
| 1 h | +1.22 Hz | +1.37 Hz | −0.15 [−4.77, 4.48] Hz |

The two early CS+ contrasts survive Holm correction across the nine reported treatment contrasts (adjusted Welch-test p≈0.0064 and 0.0274). CS+ minus CS− change contrasts are positive early but do **not** survive this broader nine-test correction at 0.05. Bootstrap intervals are pointwise. This is evidence for a feedback contribution under the intervention, not a numerical estimate of a universal MBON→DAN gain or proof that the manipulated pathway is the only source of history dependence. [Treatment contrasts](results/feedback_between_group.csv).

![Feedback and additivity estimates](figures/physiology.png)

### 2.3 Addition is plausible; a universal multiplicative gate is not established

The same-fly combination data contain 16 flies per α3 odor cohort and 14 per γ1pedc odor cohort. For each fly, I computed `combined − odor − shock`:

| DAN / odor | Mean residual | 95% bootstrap interval |
|---|---:|---:|
| α3 / ACV | −0.20 Hz | −3.67 to +2.99 |
| α3 / OCT | +0.99 Hz | −1.50 to +3.64 |
| γ1pedc / ACV | −1.99 Hz | −5.31 to +1.70 |
| γ1pedc / OCT | −1.13 Hz | −5.68 to +3.08 |

All include zero. However, none lies wholly inside either an exploratory ±2-Hz band or ±20% of its mean shock response. **Failure to reject non-additivity is not evidence of close equivalence.** The two bands are descriptive sensitivity thresholds, not established biological equivalence margins. [Residuals](results/additivity_residuals.csv).

I compared seven predictors of the combined response: odor only; shock only; their fixed sum; sum plus intercept; sum plus an `odor×shock/100` correction; sum plus both corrections; and calibrated additive gains `bO·odor+bS·shock`.

| Predictor | α3 within-cohort leave-fly-out RMSE | γ1pedc within-cohort leave-fly-out RMSE |
|---|---:|---:|
| Odor only | 11.74 | 14.63 |
| Shock only | 7.19 | 6.71 |
| Fixed sum | 6.21 | 7.84 |
| Sum + intercept | 6.58 | 8.26 |
| Sum + product | 6.53 | 6.58 |
| Sum + intercept + product | 6.59 | 5.90 |
| Calibrated additive gains | 5.77 | 5.84 |

Units are Hz. Each fitted model's primary validation trains entirely within one odor cohort and excludes the test fly. Thus the two cohorts per type are scored together but **do not share fitted coefficients** in this table. Cross-odor animal overlap is not documented; the additional leave-one-odor-cohort-out analysis is a distribution-transfer check, not certified independent-fly validation.

The product correction helps γ1pedc chiefly for OCT; it worsens the ACV cohort and does not consistently improve α3. Calibrated additive gains improve within-cohort prediction but transfer poorly across odors: leave-odor-cohort-out RMSE is 8.44 Hz for α3 and 12.63 Hz for γ1pedc, versus 6.21 and 7.84 for the fixed sum. Added flexibility can fit within-cohort variability without recovering a stable rule. Per-fly loss-difference intervals are exploratory because cross-validation fits overlap. Component responses themselves are noisy measurements from separate trials; attenuation or calibration effects can mimic an interaction.

Crucially, **odor response plus shock response is not the proposed external-event-plus-MBON-feedback decomposition**. These fits cannot establish `u×Fm` gating, conditional necessity of external input after all feedback is known, or a prediction-error interpretation. [Every prediction](results/combination_predictions.csv), [all seven models and both validations](results/combination_cv.csv).

## 3. What the original model contributes—and what it does not

I inspected the published MATLAB equations and decoded both fitted parameter sets. The three-module model has **23 free fitted parameters**; the two-module version has **16**. Their rate-network matrices contain no direct DAN→DAN connections: history enters through plastic KC→MBON weights and odor adaptation, with MBON feedback affecting DAN responses. “Recurrent learning circuit” should therefore not automatically become a fitted `R·d(t−1)` term. The steady-state simulator does not identify millisecond DAN recurrence from these source means. [Decoded parameters](results/published_model_parameters.csv), [rate-network coefficients](results/published_model_rate_network.csv), [original model code](sources/model_repo/matlab_code/model_functions/Dx_steady_state_MBON_0301_2023.m).

The decoded two-module fit retains an MBON-γ1→DAN-α3 coefficient of about −0.350, while two other retained rate-network links have fitted magnitudes near 2×10⁻⁹. This makes selective reduction plausible **inside that fitted model**. These are the authors' effective fitted coefficients, not newly measured synaptic efficacies or a proof of minimality. Neither model was re-fitted here.

For an external-condition check, I scored the authors' already-published median predictions against the experimental values in Fig. 5h/j/k—data from ISI and extinction protocols outside the 86-mean fitting table. I kept model-output columns separate from biological measurements. There are six ISI/time-point means and eight extinction means, with 14 or 10 flies per cohort. ISI time points remain paired within their own cohorts; treatment columns are not joined by row number.

The model captures useful patterns, including the lower 3-h attractive-odor response-bias ratio after late extinction: observed **−0.054**, prediction **0**. But the attractive control ratio is **0.641**, versus prediction **0.307**; the 24-h/900-s-ISI mean is **−4.27 Hz**, versus **−9.26 Hz** predicted. Two of fourteen published predictions fall outside pointwise 95% bootstrap intervals for the observed means. That count is descriptive, not a formal calibration test: predictions share fitted parameters, some outcomes are repeated within fly, and model-parameter uncertainty is not included in these mean intervals. The authors' plotted simulation intervals are 16–84%, not 95%.

![Frozen author predictions versus extinction measurements](figures/published_validation.png)

This validates neither our local-address extension nor its ablations. It establishes an existing biological modeling baseline with successes and quantitative mismatches. [Prediction audit](results/published_predictions_summary.csv).

## 4. Joining WHERE with activity: an identifiable limit

The five recorded PPL1 types can be aligned to hemibrain types using explicit compartment annotations in the supplied instances: PPL101, PPL103, PPL105, PPL106 and PPL104. Their nearest terminals account for **72,810/156,651 = 46.48%** of the earlier strict anatomical site-partner rows. This is coverage of recorded nearest-terminal labels—not measured coverage of dopamine release. The other fifteen terminal types remain physiologically unmeasured in this analysis. [Mapping and evidence](results/physiology_hemibrain_type_mapping.csv).

As a limited sensitivity calculation, I projected measured conditioning means through local and KC-wide mixtures **conditional on these five types**, explicitly renormalizing within that subset. On 15,659 held-out rows whose nearest terminal belongs to this subset, the resulting projections differ by RMS **1.51–2.82 Hz** across the eight CS/session conditions. This is a difference between two constructed projections, not observed local dopamine or predicted behavior; no missing PAM activity was set to zero in a purported whole-brain field. [Conditional sensitivity](results/conditional_field_sensitivity.csv).

The suggested constant-teacher ablation needs correction. For any normalized address,

\[
a_d(t)=c(t)\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\ell_{k,o}(t)=\sum_d q(d\mid k,o)c(t)=c(t).
\]

Local, KC-wide and shuffled addresses will be identical under a common scalar teacher. The numerical check differs only by floating-point error, below 3×10⁻¹⁵. **Identical behavior in that setup cannot show that anatomical locality is unnecessary.** If “same teacher” instead means the same *heterogeneous vector across experimental arms*, the test can be informative; channel heterogeneity must be specified and held fixed.

More generally, `(q_local−q_KCwide)·a` is blind to the common-mode component of `a`. The relevant compression target is therefore **activity differences among DAN types that interact with output-address differences**, rather than neuron count or anatomical rank alone. This is an algebraic design constraint, not a newly verified learning mechanism.

Handler's experiments constrain timing-dependent plasticity sign and distinct receptor contributions; Cohn's experiments constrain output-pathway locality within a KC. Neither establishes numerical parameters for every compartment in our two connectomes. I used these as qualitative constraints and did not digitize curves or claim a held-out quantitative timing test. A local mixture of response means lacks the timing information required to predict those results. [Handler 2019](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31230716/), [Cohn 2015](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26687359/).

## 5. BANC spatial replication: pending data, not failed biology

I inspected the current public v888 enriched-v2 Parquet footer. It describes **155,247,357 rows, 1,553 row groups and a 17.15-GB object**. Every row group's ID ranges overlap the requested KC→MBON or DAN→KC populations. The selected coordinate/ID/ROI columns alone total about **7.64 GB compressed** under ordinary row-group projection. A “lazy filtered table” would not mean a small network transfer here. The public CAVE information request redirected to sign-in, so no authenticated targeted query was run.

I prepared frozen lists of **4,552 KCs, 104 MBONs and 293 PAM/PPL1 DANs**, a precise filtered-export request, and an optional offline extractor. The enriched table also lacks the earlier hemibrain-style pair of `pre_label` and `post_label` fields; one generic `label` column cannot be assumed equivalent. Coordinates, receiving territory and release/size filtering require auditing before comparison. [Access/size audit](results/banc_subset_feasibility.json), [manual instructions](MANUAL_DOWNLOADS.md).

The verdict is **`NOT_ASSESSED_DATA_ACCESS` for new BANC spatial replication**. The prior audit's **type-level replication remains valid**. Reporting `NOT_REPLICATED` would wrongly turn missing data into evidence against the effect. No neuron IDs, coordinates or edges have been merged across specimens.

## 6. Refined assignments and decision

The work supports a compact **representation** with sampled whole-KC profiles, a retained DAN/MBON type interface, signed changes around baseline, history-bearing state and output-specific addresses. It does not identify the full evolution equation or its translation into byte-level events. In particular, the artificial BYTE outcome codebook still provides no biological interpretation of event drive.

The next assignments should be narrower than “fit five teaching models and try V56”:

1. **Complete BANC spatial replication from a filtered, audited v888 subset.** Reuse the original null and held-out-KC protocol only where territory/coordinate definitions match. This determines whether the address compression generalizes beyond hemibrain.
2. **Audit recovered trace metadata before fitting dynamics.** Establish exact fly/trial/type pairings and stimulus times. If simultaneous feedback recordings are absent, use intervention-constrained latent-state models with explicit identifiability limits; do not manufacture a population time series.
3. **Test a reduced anatomical representation with a fixed heterogeneous teacher vector.** Preserve the DAN/MBON interface and compare whole-KC sampling budgets, local/KC-wide/shuffled allocation, rare-type coverage and uncertainty. A generic probe can test routing sensitivity, but physiological or task semantics require independent validation.
4. **Require quantitative plasticity timing data before fitting the final synaptic rule.** History in weights, adaptation and recurrent activity are alternative explanations to compare, not interchangeable assumptions.

**Proceed with reduction and measurement-driven model identification. Do not yet promote the combined proposal to a validated persistent-learning mechanism.** The substantive advance is that the address operator appears inexpensive to estimate, early learned feedback effects are measurable, and several proposed tests can now be ruled out as non-identifying before another long sequence of synthetic experiments.
