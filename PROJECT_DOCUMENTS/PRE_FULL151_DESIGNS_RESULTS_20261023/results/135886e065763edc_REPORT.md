**MiniFly iteration 7 — testing an LH reference for memory readout**

13 September 2026. **The separate experiment is complete. Real LH wiring improves naive-baseline prediction in the declared comparison, but the resulting static reference fails the runtime memory-readout criteria. Keep iteration 5 as the learning parent.** This iteration adds an executable, stateless comparator and a reproducible negative functional result; it does not identify a persistent learning core or repair retention.

The positive finding is specific: the eight-feature LH model predicts held-out naive alpha3 output with **3.346 Hz RMSE**, versus **4.023 Hz** for the same-capacity PN control and **3.535–3.754 Hz** for 20 matched randomized LH graphs. The failure is consequential: after thresholds are calibrated on separate no-learning histories, correct isolated choices fall from **85/96 at five minutes to 12/96 at 24 hours**. The unchanged parent's offline memory criterion still passes **95/96** at 24 hours. Most missed choices are abstentions caused by the threshold needed to suppress baseline error.

![Readout experiment evidence](figures/readout_decision.png)

**What was actually tested.** The [protocol](PROTOCOL.md) and [configuration](config.json) were frozen before fitting. The proposal was treated as a hypothesis, not as evidence of a functioning circuit. We kept the iteration-5 `bi` learner, its learning parameters, 5,177 KCs, 302 input PNs and 88 PN channels unchanged. Every anatomical operation uses the existing FlyWire v630 specimen. No BANC or hemibrain identities were joined, and no download or large synapse-table rescan was needed.

The primary anatomical population is the metadata class selection `LHLN` or `LHCENT`: 479 local and 42 centrifugal cells, of which **520 receive selected-PN input**. There are **13,338 PN→LH pairs and 107,453 synapses**, supplied by 295 of the 302 PNs. The graph stores signed count × 0.275; undoing that factor corrects the proposal's interpretation of weighted edge sums as synapse counts. The broad `olfactory` class contains 2,274 afferent sensory cells and cannot stand in for LH output cells. See the [anatomy summary](results/anatomy_summary.json) and [connection audit](results/anatomy_connections.csv).

**An annotation limit materially narrows this result.** Final verification found **620 additional LH-named cells outside the declared class filter; 600 receive selected-PN input**. Of the 620, 616 lack a class annotation, two are labeled DN and two ALIN. A name-based inventory is itself provisional, but it establishes that the primary set is incomplete. We retained the declared population after seeing the outcomes and recorded the [scope audit](results/annotation_scope_audit.json). This is a test of a local/centrifugal subset and one static linear family, not a rejection of all LH output circuits. The additional identity table remains in the parent project and is referenced in the bundle manifest.

The selected population reaches alpha3 MBON14 directly through only **five GABA-labeled cells and 95 synapses**: four LHCENT8 cells and one LHCENT9. There are also **155 common downstream targets** receiving at least five synapses each from selected LH cells and alpha3 MBON14. The [direct-source table](results/direct_LH_MBON14_sources.csv) and [top convergence candidates](results/convergence_candidates.csv) retain identities and transmitter information. These routes motivate possible convergence experiments; they do not establish a subtraction circuit. The main fitted comparator is virtual, and its fitted signs are not asserted to be realized by those anatomical cells.

This qualification also follows the literature. Dolan and colleagues demonstrated learned MBON-alpha2sc influence on LH neurons carrying innate olfactory information; their retrieval circuit concerns alpha2, not this model's alpha3 output. LH cannot generally be assumed to remain an untouched naive copy. [Dolan et al., 2018](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6226615/). Connectome work distinguishes PN and MBON input territories and recurrent communication across olfactory processing levels; anatomical integration does not itself specify a neutral-baseline subtraction. [Schlegel et al., 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8298098/).

**A small, fixed experiment.** Primary `LH8` takes incoming-count-normalized linear PN drive at each selected LH cell, standardizes it on development inputs, retains eight principal components, and fits two ridge outputs to naive left/right alpha3 responses. The fixed penalty is 0.1; predicted references are clipped to the parent's known output bounds. `PN8` uses the same eight-feature fit directly on PN channels. `PN88` uses all 88 channels and has greater fitting capacity. `LH_direct2` uses two features weighted by actual LH→left/right MBON14 counts and therefore has a smaller bottleneck. Constant-reference and raw-output controls complete the comparison. There was no hyperparameter search.

The feature transform and linear decoder collapse exactly to 88×2 PN coefficients plus two intercepts before clipping. Thus LH provides a constrained feature basis, not information absent from PN input. LH8 and PN8 each fit 18 regression coefficients after feature extraction; PN88 fits 178. The [fit accounting](results/fit_accounting.csv) reports this distinction. Neither real LH recurrence nor LH adaptation is simulated.

We generated **800 new, mutually disjoint patterns**: 256 development, 128 threshold calibration, 128 baseline test and 12 test banks of 24. They were also excluded from old iteration-5 inputs and iteration-6 reserved banks. Development targets contain naive parent responses, not training-choice labels. Threshold calibration uses separate no-learning histories: naive, cue-only exposure followed by five minutes, and exposure followed by 24 hours. All models share the same parent trajectories; there are 1,920 offline probes in total. The [split inventory](results/split_inventory.csv) preserves seeds and hashes. Generated patterns and repeated probes are not independent flies or measured odors.

For each of 20 nulls, equal-weight double-edge swaps preserve every selected PN/LH node's degree and synaptic strength, the edge-weight multiset, and PN-side × LH-side/class/transmitter blocks exactly. Crossed edges must be absent. The procedure changes 63.4–64.5% of edge identities and at least 45.0% of synaptic mass; all invariants pass. Exact PN-type-to-LH-type associations are intentionally disturbed. See [null invariants](results/matched_null_audit.csv) and [mixing audit](results/null_mixing_audit.csv). This is a constrained randomization ensemble, not a demonstration of uniform sampling over all admissible graphs.

**A structural gain survives the declared null comparison.** LH8's naive RMSE is 16.8% below PN8 and 19.7% below the constant predictor. It beats all 20 matched graphs, giving the declared one-sided empirical rank **p = 1/21 = 0.0476**, the finest attainable value with this ensemble. The [final null metrics](results/matched_null_final_metrics.csv) therefore pass the narrow LH-specific prediction screen. This modest, single-specimen result does not establish a conserved computation, correct downstream physiology, or a useful autonomous choice circuit. Larger-capacity PN88 predicts better still, at 2.487 Hz. None of these baselines meets the declared ≤1 Hz feasibility gate.

**The runtime reference fails.** The score is observed mean alpha3 output minus predicted reference. Thresholds are fixed from the calibration set's 95th percentile of absolute no-learning scores, with a 1 Hz minimum. LH8 needs 8.937 Hz for a single cue and 8.161 Hz for a pair contrast. A pair chooses the predicted preferred option only outside that interval and otherwise abstains. The following table uses those calibrated thresholds; the required isolated-choice count is at least 84/96 at each delay, revision at least 10/12, and each no-learning false-alarm rate at most 5%.

The [complete summary](results/readout_summary.csv), [phase-specific decisions](results/pair_decision_metrics.csv), and [acceptance checks](results/acceptance_checks.json) retain the failures. LH8 passes only two of nine usefulness gates. Its correct-choice advantage over PN8 is small: +1.0 percentage point at five minutes and +2.1 at 24 hours; paired bootstrap intervals over the 12 banks include zero. See [paired effects](results/paired_choice_effects.csv). No behavioral improvement over PN8 is established by this panel.

The threshold tradeoff explains why the proposal's promising sign result was insufficient. Among **647 changed probes**, LH8 with a fixed 1 Hz threshold detects **571/647 (88.3%)**, but declares a change on **300/384 (78.1%)** dedicated no-learning probes. Calibration lowers those false alarms to **35/384 (9.1%)**, while detecting only **142/647 (21.9%)** changes. Sign agreement is **503/647 (77.7%)**, evaluated on all changed probes rather than only easy detected cases. These miss the 80% detection, 90% sign and 5% false-alarm gates. [Single-cue metrics](results/single_cue_metrics.csv).

At 24 hours, a fixed 1 Hz pair threshold gives 77 correct choices, seven abstentions and 12 wrong choices. The calibrated threshold gives 12 correct and **84 abstentions**, with no wrong choices. Raising the threshold therefore conceals most weaker memories; it does not demonstrate their erasure. Calibration itself yields 5.208% false alarms because of the finite-sample percentile and inclusive threshold rule; this small deviation is retained. Sequential test histories are richer than calibration histories, and their false alarms increase further. We did not tune on those failures.

**History is a concrete missing input.** On held-out sensory-only histories, LH8 baseline RMSE rises from **3.346 Hz naive to 6.818 Hz after cue exposure and five minutes of rest**, returning to the naive value after 24 hours. The mean reference overestimate after familiarization is 6.395 Hz. PN88 also fails here, with 6.404 Hz RMSE despite its better naive fit. A current-cue-only map cannot reproduce the changing adaptation-dependent baseline of the same cue. [History-dependent errors](results/baseline_prediction_metrics.csv).

The historical iteration-5 panel makes this limitation explicit: even an arbitrary cue-specific constant, optimally chosen after seeing all those histories, has an in-sample RMSE floor of **1.549 Hz**. The median same-cue baseline range is 2.408 Hz and the maximum is 9.957 Hz. This is a descriptive floor on that fixed retrospective panel, not a lower bound on every possible decision circuit. [Static-reference limit](results/static_reference_limit.json).

We also reproduced the substance of Claude's retrospective observation: subtracting each cue's own initial response gives **104/113 (92.0%)** correct signs on changed historical probes, compared with 50/113 for raw output. But it produces false alarms on **17/87 (19.5%)** unchanged probes and requires the very cue-specific reference we wanted to avoid. The new LH8 gives only 67.3% sign agreement on that historical panel. These old probes were excluded from fitting and acceptance; the 92% result is a useful diagnostic, not a demonstrated autonomous decoder. [Historical audit](results/historical_reference_audit.csv).

**What this teaches us about the persistent core.** A static reference cancels algebraically when measuring learning-induced score change against the same adaptation-preserving plastic-reset evaluation: `(raw − reference) − (reset − reference) = raw − reset`. Verification confirms this numerically. It can align a decision baseline but cannot repair stored-trace decay by itself. With every readout attached, the unchanged parent passes 96/96 five-minute and 95/96 24-hour isolated memory criteria, 17/48 half-retention tests after interference, and 11/12 reversals on these new banks. Those counts use new inputs and should not replace the earlier iteration-5/6 bank results. [Parent memory metrics](results/unchanged_parent_memory_metrics.csv).

Our false-alarm requirement tests a memory-specific neutral-baseline readout. Untrained animals can have valid innate preferences; a no-learning preference is not inherently a biological error. Failure here rejects the proposed reference function under this contract, not every biologically plausible LH/MB valence computation. A next experiment would need a better-defined LH output/convergence inventory and an explicit account of which history-dependent physiological signals the readout can actually access. It should compare a shared-history reference with a generic control of equal state and capacity. More PCA settings, another arbitrary memory lifetime, or redefining retention would not answer that question. No extra fit was launched after failure.

**Executable result and verification.** The standalone [readout module](src/readout.py) accepts only current PN activity and two observed alpha3 responses. It has **zero added mutable bytes**, 1,456 bytes of fixed numerical reference constants, and no cue lookup, clock, adaptation access or reset operation. Its algebraic collapse matches the explicit feature decoder within 7.2×10⁻¹⁵ Hz. The parent remains **165,688 mutable bytes**, constant through the 1,024-pattern capacity check; checkpoint continuation and passive attachment preserve rates, arrays and counters exactly. All **43 implementation checks pass**, while scientific acceptance remains false. [Implementation verification](results/implementation_verification.json), [capacity checks](results/capacity_checks.csv).

The [campaign entrypoint](run.py) reproduced the complete declared preparation, fitting, evaluation and checks in about 13 seconds on this Mac. The largest measured process RSS was approximately 728 MB during sparse-graph extraction; parent panel generation used about 221 MB. These are process peaks, not total system RAM. The separate online demo executes without reset-clone calls and does not alter the learning model. [Reproduction receipt](results/campaign_reproduction.json), [online interface receipt](results/online_demo/summary.json). Instructions are in the [README](README.md).

Datasets, raw probe/per-bank collections, caches, historical archives and unchanged third-party code remain in the parent project.
