# MiniFly V9.10C — a small online shared head improves unseen-pair learning

**Prospectively locked comparator, not a native REF mechanism.** A single 5,177-weight KC value head, trained *online* on the same presented byte records and feedback as REF, answered **77.54%** of never-reinforced rank comparisons correctly at the first old-teaching-end probe across 64 new worlds. Its no-old-head-write control tied at 50%, yielding a world-paired **+27.54 percentage-point** teaching effect [24.33, 30.74]. Contemporaneous native REF reached **62.11%**; the head-minus-REF paired difference was **+15.43 pp** [10.72, 20.14]. The prospectively specified immediate candidate gate passed. After a day, 72 new-cohort teaching records, and a further day, the same head scored **75.39%** on the old never-taught pairs while learning the new directly taught cyclic pairs to 100%. This is a concrete **bounded online E3 comparator within the rank family**, not a general persistent learning core or proof that REF's own writer/readout has been repaired.

## Exposure and actual learner

The fixture randomizes eight uppercase bytes to ranks in each world. Twenty of 28 unordered pairs are taught in both orders for eight balanced bouts (320 records). Both orders of the other eight pairs receive **no feedback**, including after their first scored choice. The head's update receives only the 5,177-dimensional `pending_x` already captured for the *currently presented* 12-byte ordered cue before its outcome byte, plus that record's neutral/aversive feedback. It does not query the reverse cue during training, parse symbol identities, receive hidden ranks, or keep per-pair slots. The head uses one zero-initialized `float64` vector (41,416 learned bytes), no bias or optimizer state, and the preregistered normalized update `w += 0.5 (y−w·x)x/(x·x+1)` with `y=+1` neutral and `−1` aversive. Both test orientations are scored read-only by the same vector. Encoder weights and the V9.2 parent checkpoint were unchanged.

The head-only `H_W` and `H_N` arms share the no-native-value-write sensory model and differ only in whether **old** feedback updates `w`; `H_N` stays exactly zero at old end. Native `REF_W` and `REF_Z` share the same stream but differ in old native value writes. All branches use identical clocks and byte-predictor teaching. The old-day state then forks to rest (`R`), later sensory stream without value/head write (`S`), and the same stream with new writes (`L`). The later cohort uses three disjoint symbols and six directly taught ordered cues forming a cycle. Its score checks new acquisition (E1); it is **not** a held-out second-relation test.

## Confirmed results

Choice gives 1 for a correctly signed pair margin, 0.5 for a tie, and 0 for an incorrect sign. All intervals below are across **64 worlds**, which are the statistical units. The only confirmatory primary is old-end `H_W−H_N`; other intervals are descriptive.

| Probe | Shared head with old teaching | Its no-old-write control | Native REF with old teaching | Native no-old-write |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Old end, eight never-taught pairs | **77.54%** [74.33, 80.74] | 50.00% | 62.11% [58.34, 65.88] | 54.88% |
| Old day, never-taught pairs | **77.54%** | 50.00% | 62.89% | 55.27% |
| Final day after new learning, never-taught pairs | **75.39%** [72.35, 78.44] | 50.20% | 64.65% [60.73, 68.56] | 54.49% |

At old end, `H_W−H_N` was **+27.54 pp** [24.33, 30.74] and `H_W−REF_W` was **+15.43 pp** [10.72, 20.14]. Native `REF_W−REF_Z` was itself positive, **+7.23 pp** [1.75, 12.70]: REF already has limited causal transfer, so the head does not create it from nothing. At final day after the new stream, `H_W_L−H_N_L` on old never-taught pairs remained **+25.20 pp** [19.86, 30.53]. The head's new writes changed its old held-out choice by **−2.15 pp** [−4.91, +0.61] versus its same-prestate new-write sham, so interference in thresholded choice is unresolved; new sensory exposure without head writes had exactly zero measured effect (`S−R`).

The new-learning guard passed. On the **directly taught** cyclic cohort at new-cohort end, `H_W_L` scored **100%**, its matched `H_W_S` **47.40%**, and their paired contrast was **+52.60 pp** [46.71, 58.50]. The `H_N_L` head also scored 100% there. Native REF learned the cohort too: `REF_W_L−REF_W_S` was **+28.65 pp** [21.10, 36.19]. Thus the head's old-result survival was observed while its *same shared weights* acquired new examples; it was not an eight-symbol potential table protected by excluding new symbols. The disjoint new-symbol schedule may nevertheless make interference easier than an overlapping or contradictory stream.

## What this establishes and what it does not

This is stronger than V9.10A's offline graph fit and V9.9C's offline ridge probe in one specific respect: **the head updates causally, one actual byte record at a time, and its held-out answers come from the resulting online state**. It shows that the existing FE0/PN/KC interface and feedback stream suffice for an engineered, fixed-size shared learner to reuse taught information on unseen pair combinations and retain much of that advantage through this delay/new-learning schedule. It does **not** localize REF's native bottleneck uniquely to writer or reader: the added head changes both the update and readout. The no-write head is an exact zero baseline by design; the native no-write branch is a separate, imperfect baseline.

Nor does 77.54% establish a detailed eight-level ordering. The prespecified oracle **one-bit-per-symbol comparator can reach 75%** on these held-out pairs; the head's 95% interval includes 75%. A simple class-like feature rule learned from teaching remains a possible explanation. Exact raw or canonical pair lookup can only tie (50%) because neither orientation of any scored pair was taught; an analytic win-count rule from taught labels can solve all eight (100%), but it is not installed. The frozen KC encoder and byte predictor are substantial unchanged parent assets, so 41,416 bytes is the *added learned head*, not the complete fly size. Static weights have no intrinsic day-scale decay; delay-only retention is partly a property of this engineered comparator, while new-write survival is observed under one disjoint cohort. The 100% new-cohort score concerns **taught pairs** and is not cyclic generalization.

The next meaningful challenge is to freeze this architecture and test a second relation family with **withheld combinations and first-response scoring**, preferably one that a scalar symbol potential cannot represent. Before such a run, establish that its KC pair codes make the family learnable by a fixed online head, then predeclare the exposure and new-learning controls. A broad B/banking/topology search is not justified by this result alone.

## Audit and compact bundle

Engineering world 9905 passed exact one-record equivalence against the inherited feed, same-prestate training/query KC-code equality, write-flag inertness, clone/read-only, clock, predictor, and symbol-exposure checks. Eight pilot worlds (1851–1858) were excluded from confirmation and did not change the head rule. All 64 locked confirmation worlds (1901–1964) completed in **483.5 seconds**. Across confirmation worlds, the maximum KC-code difference between contemporaneous native W/Z arms and maximum recorded clock error were both zero. Canonical byte-order margin tables were hashed before hidden-rank scoring. An [independent audit](results/AUDIT.json) recomputed every scored pair and world summary and replayed one world through the unchanged V9.9C runner, matching the native REF old-end scores. No raw fly dataset, parent checkpoint, prior archive, cache, or per-record trajectory is copied into this bundle.
