# Fixed visible-context → Full151: four-cohort persistence

**Final status: complete and audited (2026-09-27).** This is an E1 test of directly taught byte-to-answer associations under continued learning. It keeps the qualified fixed visible-context encoder, native Full151 checkpoint, four output stores, answer-byte teaching, and prompted one-byte output unchanged. It does not test E3 transfer, arithmetic understanding, teacher-free learning, or 80-key capacity.

## Question and design

Can the old 16 `a+b=` byte cues retain their learned output after four new cohorts of four cues each? All 32 effective last-four-visible-byte keys are distinct. Each cue is taught in 12 interleaved presentations. The learner emits a byte at `=` before seeing the answer byte; that byte then routes native Full151 teaching. After old learning and a simulated 24-hour rest, the *same old-trained state* is forked into `WWWW` (new-cohort writes enabled) and `NNNN` (identical bytes, teacher bytes, and clock, but new writes disabled). Four new cohorts run consecutively, followed by another simulated 24-hour rest. Each cohort also has a no-write shadow cloned from the corresponding `WWWW` prestate. Read-only probes use model copies.

The sealed confirmation bank contained exactly 64 fresh worlds, 29701–29764. Technical world 29600 was excluded from inference. The predeclared primary estimand was the **paired, world-level final old-cue emitted-byte accuracy difference, `WWWW − NNNN`**. The protocol's 95% interval is a Student-t interval across worlds, never across the 16 cues. A claim of average write cost below 5 percentage points additionally required the interval lower bound above −5 points and final `WWWW` old accuracy interval lower bound above 80%.

## Result

| Measure | `WWWW` | `NNNN` / control | Interpretation |
| --- | ---: | ---: | --- |
| Old cues after old teaching and first 24 h | 1024/1024, 100% | `OLD_N`: 256/1024, 25% | Old learning gate passed. |
| Old cues after cohort 4, before final rest | 995/1024, 97.17% | 1024/1024, 100% | Immediate interference was larger. |
| **Old cues after final 24 h** | **1016/1024, 99.22%** | **1024/1024, 100%** | Primary paired difference **−0.78 percentage points**, 95% CI **[−1.30, −0.26]**. |
| New cohorts at their teaching end | 256/256 each, 100% | Same-prestate no-write shadows: 54, 59, 64, 76 of 256 | Every new-learning gate passed. |
| New cohorts after final 24 h | 246, 253, 254, 256 of 256 | — | High final recall for all four new cohorts. |

The final old-cue contrast was negative in **8 of 64 worlds** (one of 16 old cues lost in each), zero in 56, and positive in none. Its world-level estimate was −0.0078125 with 95% CI [−0.0130165, −0.0026085]. Thus the prespecified assay is sensitive, and additional native writing caused a **small, detectable loss of old output accuracy under this workload**. The separately reported correct-class-minus-strongest-wrong-class margin declined by −0.253 [−0.282, −0.223]. The −5-point operational cost criterion passed: the primary interval lower bound is above −0.05 and the final writing-arm old accuracy interval is [98.70%, 99.74%]. This is evidence of strong *functional persistence at 32 keys*, not literal zero forgetting.

Old writing-arm accuracy over the four cohort-end probes was 99.22%, 97.95%, 98.05%, and 97.17%; it returned to 99.22% after the final simulated 24-hour rest without further teaching. That recovery and the margin loss can reflect different fast/slow state contributions, but this experiment does not identify a specific internal mechanism. The eight final errors fell on `1+0=` (five) and `3+3=` (three); that concentration is descriptive, not an outcome-selected follow-up test.

## Integrity and audit

Before confirmation, the complete technical world ran once (32.41 s, 960 branch events, 448 read-only probe rows, 32 distinct technical KC supports) and passed the independent technical receipt audit and five tamper controls. The source lock sealed the design, runner, auditor, parent encoder/runner/checkpoint, technical world, and 64-world roster. Confirmation ran once with `caffeinate`; all 64 receipts were atomically committed, and the process exited 0. The receipt auditor independently regenerated every fixture and checked source hashes, event/probe rosters, answer bytes, native write ledgers, timestamps, branch digests, and output argmax values. [The final audit](results/AUDIT.json) records `pass=true` and per-receipt hashes. A second direct calculation from the raw probe rows, without importing the frozen auditor, reproduced the primary interval, raw counts, new-cohort scores, and error identities.

The frozen auditor had one post-run **serialization-only** defect: its reduction produced four `numpy.bool_` gate values, which `json.dumps` rejected after all receipts had been verified. The frozen scientific runner and auditor were left unchanged; [a narrow finalization erratum](audit_finalize_erratum.py) repeated every receipt check, called the unchanged reduction, converted exactly those four booleans to Python `bool`, and wrote [the summary](results/SUMMARY.json) and final audit. No trajectory was rerun and no outcome, gate, threshold, or world roster changed. The confirm receipts consumed about 17 MB; 64 learner lives totaled 2042 s (34.0 min), with maximum reported process RSS 397 MB. The four-hour and 100 MB caps were not approached.

## Scope of the conclusion

The result establishes a working, fixed **byte address → native Full151 memory → prompted emitted answer byte** bridge that retains almost all of 16 old arbitrary labels while learning 16 more under this specific four-cohort chronology. The small write-associated loss matters even though the operational persistence gate passed. The fixed encoder is effectively an exact four-visible-byte address; spacing and fixed-prefix variants in the parent bridge map to that same address by construction. It cannot by itself support unseen-key E3 transfer. The present 32-key workload does not certify 80 keys, open-ended text, self-generated answer timing, or learning without an arriving answer byte. It also cannot assign V9.5's larger loss to KC overlap: the models, cue sets, and workloads differ, and pairwise KC overlap here is not literally zero. Any later capacity or generalization question must be treated as a separate objective, not as an extension of this confirmation bank.
