# V69 / Iteration 14 — Action feasibility before action selection

**V69 is complete. Neither action family qualified for selector fitting or a runtime pilot.** Routing conflicting slow writes into the existing fast trace preserved some transient information but lost its advantage over deletion by the next day. Spatial redistribution showed small positive effects, including under privileged selection, but they were below the locked practical threshold. Its trajectory-dependent transfer-volume mismatch also prevents a clean claim of superiority over the intended matched control. These are different reasons for withholding progression, not proof that every possible routing policy is impossible.

The reference remains **iteration5/bi**, instantiated by the unchanged **5,177-KC iteration10 model** and frozen iteration8 reader. There were **zero selector fits, zero physiology/reader fits and no prospective runtime pilot**. All **335 startup checks and 131 final audit checks passed**. These audit counts do not mean the learning hypotheses passed.

## How the proposals changed after checking actual assets

The final [protocol](PROTOCOL.md) combines a bounded action comparison with a conditional action-value analysis. We retained temporal and spatial routing, fixed the primary loads at48 and96, and added explicit target/old-memory safety constraints, transport limits and dose checks. We removed delay-and-promote, recruitment changes and the proposed scalar utility weight.

Several concrete corrections mattered:

| Proposal premise | Verified fact and consequence |
|---|---|
| V68 established an accessible ownership signal | It decoded episode categories on development histories. Biological access and action utility were not established. |
| V68 F was slow_L1/slow_L2 | The actual F was `||dx*slow||2/(||dx||2+epsilon)`; E matched S1 feature27. |
| More retained mass than the scalar proves selection rather than dose caused failure | V68 did not exactly match realized temporal/spatial dose. The comparison limits a simple total-dose account but does not isolate all allocation effects. |
| Mean231 KCs represent each memory's direct write support | The231.32 figure is a final omit-one-history footprint, including changes in later learning. Own-cue anatomical alpha support averages 103.56. Neither is an independent memory-capacity estimate. |
| Overlap had almost no variance, around0.44–0.54 | Footprint Jaccard actually ranges0.157–0.864. The distinct `cue_overlap` used in the cited low correlation ranges0.0039–0.3780. The proposed recruitment rationale mixed variables. |
| Lambda 30.667 is an observed exchange rate | It divides mean new gain by mean focal damage; multiplying a sum of12–96 old losses adds an uncalibrated load dependence. No biological exchange rate was measured. |
| Fast routing can become consolidation by itself | The existing parent has no fast-to-slow promotion mechanism. Fast tau is 3,487.58s, half-life 40.29min; day survival is 1.74e-11. |

The supplied permutation null had no accompanying reproducible seed/script/conditioning specification and was not accepted as evidence. This audit used existing arrays and deterministic encoding only—no new learner simulation or fit. Its scripts/tables are preserved in the referenced `v69_design` assets. Recruitment mechanisms were deferred, not permanently refuted.

## Executed experiment

We reconstructed all 16 V67 histories at loads12/24/48/96 and checked their stored parent capacities. Each load supplied one deterministically chosen old association, counterbalanced across age quartiles. No selection depended on subsequent damage or retention. Four new reserved pairs per stream supplied NEW cases; these 128 cue patterns were globally disjoint from all 27,384 prior cues, including V68 validation. The histories remain known development histories, not independent animals or fresh confirmation of a final learner.

Each checkpoint supplied NEW, REVERSE, STABLE and ANOMALY, for **256 assigned cases**. The anomaly was one reversed A/B bout followed by the original contingency. Each case ran 12 bouts, with neutral probes after bouts 1/6/12 at +300s and +24h. NEW's primary endpoint was bout 6; durable reversal used bout 12. For reversal the deliberately revised focal was excluded from collateral damage, avoiding a penalty for the intended change. All other old associations were scored, including unsuccessful ones. Reversal/anomaly first-bout states were exactly identical within each arm.

Eleven arms were run: PARENT, exposure-matched NO_WRITE, DROP, FAST, uniform-drop and uniform-fast controls, SPACE, and four spatial-location shams. The complete assay produced **764,544 pair-outcome rows**.

- **DROP** zeroed the currently conflicting slow increment. It is a reference actuator, not an exact replay of V68's conditional gate.
- **FAST** added that increment to alpha-fast instead. It conserves each coordinate's raw total alpha increment before the existing decay. It adds no promotion process.
- **SPACE** moved conflict mass to currently active, nonconflicting recipients with the same sign, side and exact normalized alpha teaching-route row. The recipient cap was 2× its original increment. Unmovable mass stayed at the donor; no zero-teacher coordinate was recruited. Total signed slow mass within each group was conserved.
- **Uniform controls** matched the instantaneous fraction on their own current states. **Spatial shams** used the current state's requested transfer volume but randomized donor/recipient locations under the same route/sign/cap constraints. Thus “blind” refers to location selection, not ignorance of the requested volume. Missing feasible partitions were counted rather than hidden.

These are interventions in an effective model using real anatomical constraints, not evidence that a biological circuit implements those computations. Learned numeric state remained 165,688 bytes. The executable action wrapper also caches a fixed derived route-group index of 41,416 bytes plus a fixed randomization seed; that overhead is not presented as compression or free memory.

## Raw action results: transient preservation versus durable learning

The following are mean effects for one new association after six bouts and 24h. New gain and positive old damage are measured relative to the same-exposure NO_WRITE branch. Each new-choice percentage has 16 target pairs; these are not whole-capacity curves or a learner trained from zero with the intervention.

| Prior load | Action | New margin gain (Hz) | New joint choices | Mean positive old damage (Hz) |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 48 | PARENT | 4.629 | 87.50% | 0.14243 |
| 48 | DROP | 2.738 | 68.75% | 0.06936 |
| 48 | FAST | 2.738 | 68.75% | 0.06936 |
| 48 | SPACE | 4.538 | 87.50% | 0.11626 |
| 96 | PARENT | 4.794 | 68.75% | 0.16149 |
| 96 | DROP | 2.708 | 56.25% | 0.07641 |
| 96 | FAST | 2.708 | 56.25% | 0.07641 |
| 96 | SPACE | 4.863 | 68.75% | 0.14487 |

FAST reduced mean old damage, but its new gain fell by **1.891Hz [1.531,2.249]** at load48 and **2.086Hz [1.768,2.436]** at load96. This is the acquisition/retention tradeoff the action was supposed to avoid. The target-choice differences versus parent were −18.75 and−12.50 percentage points; their16-stream intervals include zero at the upper endpoint, so those percentage differences alone are not claimed as decisive significance tests.

FAST did preserve more transient target gain than DROP: at +300s the mean NEW gain was 16.127 versus 15.490Hz at load48, and 16.615 versus 15.842Hz at load96. Across **all** recorded +24h cases/bouts, the maximum FAST–DROP target-gain difference in absolute value was only **1.23e-9Hz**. In this parent, sending information to fast state does not create durable storage. It would be incorrect to infer that every policy involving fast state is excluded; no delayed-promotion policy was tested.

For day/dose12 reversal, PARENT succeeded on 11/16 assigned targets at each primary load, FAST/DROP on 7/16, and SPACE on 11/16. These are block interventions from the loaded parent, not V68's different reversal sample or a new prospective capacity result. All cases, delays and doses—including stable and anomalous inputs—are in the final tables.

SPACE's raw mean old-damage reduction was **0.02617Hz [0.01708,0.03696]** at load48 and **0.01662Hz [0.00896,0.02538]** at load96. Target choices were unchanged; new gain changed by −0.0912Hz [−0.1597,−0.0265] and+0.0693Hz [−0.0007,+0.1513]. These are useful positive observations. They do not by themselves show that the action safely helps every memory or beats a correctly matched amount of redistribution.

## Privileged selection: small spatial benefit, no qualifying family

The finite-menu oracle could choose PARENT or one primitive for each block after seeing its effects. It had to preserve target actual/reader margins within0.25Hz at both delays, preserve every parent-correct target and old joint choice, and avoid any old margin loss worse than0.25Hz. Only then could it select a reduction in day collateral damage. A fallback to PARENT remained in the denominator. The same oracle selections were applied to the controls; the oracle did not choose a different subset to disadvantage them.

| Family | Prior load | Primitive selected | Mean old-damage reduction | Fraction of parent damage |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| FAST | 48 | 0/16 | 0 | 0% |
| FAST | 96 | 0/16 | 0 | 0% |
| SPACE | 48 | 8/16 | **0.00902Hz [0.00374,0.01538]** | **6.33%** |
| SPACE | 96 | 6/16 | **0.00434Hz [0.00146,0.00759]** | **2.69%** |

The locked screen required at least0.02Hz **and**20% of parent damage at **both** loads48 and96, with a positive paired interval and adequate coverage. That means0.02849Hz and0.03230Hz here. These are declared engineering relevance thresholds, not biological constants. SPACE's intervals are positive but its effects remain well below those thresholds. FAST has no safe beneficial choice under this finite-menu definition. No classifier can turn this particular oracle ceiling into a large safe improvement within the tested block action set.

## Control limitation: instantaneous equality did not ensure equal cumulative allocation

The protocol anticipated that trajectories could diverge and made a 1% block-budget check mandatory. It failed:

| Primary load | FAST vs uniform-fast: slow-dose mismatch | SPACE vs location shams: total slow-dose mismatch | SPACE vs shams: transferred-volume mismatch |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 48 | 3.64% | <1e-8% | **10.96%** |
| 96 | 3.37% | <1e-8% | **9.75%** |

The combined alpha dose for FAST was essentially equal; its division across lifetimes was not. SPACE preserved total slow dose, but the amount of mass moved between coordinates differed as each arm's state changed. This is not missing destination capacity: mean feasible requested-volume coverage for the shams was 99.94% and 99.68% at the primary loads.

Numerically, the SPACE oracle also beat these shams by 0.00957 and 0.00424Hz, with positive intervals. **That does not pass the matched-control claim**, because the amount of redistribution was not sufficiently matched. We did not retrospectively rescale states, tune the shams, relax the 1% threshold or introduce a replacement arm. The correct disposition is a small spatial signal with an unresolved allocation-amount comparison. The practical-effect gate failed independently, so neither family qualified even if the comparison were set aside.

## Stopping, verification and implications

The action stage stopped at its declared boundary. **No compact feature search was performed, no action-value model was fit, no fresh runtime-validation bank was generated, and no new learning rule was promoted.** The spread-across24h schedule was conditional on a runtime pilot, so it was not run and is not claimed as passed. V69 restricts two specified primitives acting throughout a block; it does not prove an upper bound for arbitrary state-dependent temporal policies, other spatial allocations or other fly circuits.

The experimental distinction is clearer now. Merely changing lifetime provides transient storage in this model. Spatial allocation has some capacity to reduce collateral damage while preserving average acquisition, but the safely selectable benefit is small under these constraints. The next justified step, if pursued, is to determine whether that spatial signal survives controls that prescribe the same transfer amount throughout a trajectory. A better ownership classifier, a new recruitment scheme or a new timescale is not licensed by these results. Even a cleaner positive allocation result would still require prospective learning and physiology checks.

All source/config/input locks and 124 frozen parent assets verified. The campaign saved global dose totals. A separately source-locked **audit replay** then recorded cumulative signed anatomical-group budgets and checked every replayed outcome against the original 764,544 rows. This was additional instrumentation on unchanged code and inputs, not a new action or a change of selection criteria. Maximum outcome replay discrepancy was **3.55e-15Hz**. Route/cap/support and raw-budget identities passed; no scientific source or gate changed after the experiment lock. Original outcomes and earlier V67/V68 release hashes remained unchanged.

Main campaign worker time summed to **182.6s**, analysis 14.0s, and the detailed route replay 175.7s. The campaign used two workers; maximum reported per-worker RSS was **466MB**. These are measured worker totals, not total elapsed assistant time or combined system-memory measurements. The large synapse mother table was not loaded, and no data was downloaded.
