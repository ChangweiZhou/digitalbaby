Date: 2026-09-20. Primary project: `/Users/pencilbard/Downloads/second_try_copy`.

The proposed explanation needs substantial revision.** Independent recomputation reproduces +6.1198 percentage points across active cohorts, +8.4201 pp for the designated target and +5.7912 pp for the other cohorts. **Audit scope and reproducibility**

I read the memo, source implementation, current decisions and result records, then recomputed all 288 A6 centering checkpoints from 24 capture NPZ files. A separate NumPy implementation of the clipped native expression and two correctness gates was checked against the frozen native probe for native, LOBO-subtracted and same-block-subtracted states. It does not call A6's centering or transfer aggregation functions.

For K=8, the saved A4 sparse source rows were independently reconstructed and matched to A6's D, group means and retained weights. They permit recomputing all 21,504 source-group-destination cells, including exact nonlinear M/C Shapley effects. The maximum probe-margin discrepancy was 3.02e−14, correctness mismatches were zero, and maximum D reconstruction discrepancy was 3.96e−11. Source locks and cross-block anatomy/Q/F alignment passed. See `ACCEPTANCE_CHECKS.json`.

**Important archive limit:** K=2 captures retain aggregate D and group means, but not per-event source rows or per-group retained S. Consequently, K=2 LOBO and spectra are independently recomputed; its exact per-group self/foreign Shapley numbers are checked at the source/report level, not independently regenerated from raw source rows. Replaying those trajectories with a source-row capture would close this one provenance gap. No such replay was started here.

**1. Does the LOBO result reproduce without held-out-vector leakage? Yes.**

| Load | Native active accuracy | LOBO active accuracy | Active change | Target change | Other-cohort change | Positive blocks |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| K=2 forward | 89.4965% | 91.4063% | +1.9097 pp | +2.3438 pp | +1.4757 pp | 7/8 |
| K=2 reverse | 90.7118% | 92.4479% | +1.7361 pp | +1.6493 pp | +1.8229 pp | 7/8 |
| K=8 | 70.6163% | 76.7361% | **+6.1198 pp** | **+8.4201 pp** | **+5.7912 pp** | **8/8** |

The recomputation explicitly enumerates the seven other blocks; it does not use the runner's sum-minus-held-out shortcut. No held-out mean vector enters its own mean prediction. The frozen plan assigns 64 worlds to eight disjoint blocks. At each checkpoint, all active cohorts are included with fact-count weights. Each has 12 informed, currently relevant facts, and all blocks contribute 12 checkpoints; fact-weighted and block/checkpoint-weighted effects coincide here.

This does **not** make the correction an online local rule. Its total magnitude uses held-out retained-weight sums, and source membership, target exclusion, group/context identity and checkpoint identity are evaluator-defined. The donor vectors are out-of-block; the entire correction is not independent of held-out metadata. It is cross-validated diagnostic headroom on repeatedly studied development worlds. Overlapping donor sets also mean eight positive folds should not be treated as eight independent confirmations.

At K=8, same-block subtraction instead gives **−23.5786 pp active**, +19.7049 pp target and −29.7619 pp other cohorts. Target-only scoring would indeed give the wrong decision.

**2. What does the source→destination matrix actually show?**

The K=2 challenge values quoted by the memo match the stored records: M self effects +7.20 to +8.39, with foreign effects −0.138 to −1.478. But K=8 is different. The following is independently rebuilt from sparse source rows, averaging challenge roles/checkpoints/blocks; effects are signed-margin Shapley contributions, not percentage points.

| K=8 destination relative to a source | Pooled-mean M effect | Source-residual C effect | Total source S effect |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| The source's own cohort | +0.5340 | **+6.7970** | +7.3310 |
| Designated target, excluded from mean construction | −0.6230 | +0.3834 | −0.2396 |
| Another one of the six included source cohorts | +0.3891 | −0.5997 | −0.2106 |
| All seven foreign destinations averaged | **+0.2445** | **−0.4593** | −0.2147 |

Total source writes have useful self effects and foreign costs. That broad interpretation survives. The stronger interpretation “M carries the self signal plus harmful collateral, while C is mainly small random noise” does **not** carry over unchanged to K=8. Most source-specific self effect there lies in C. Pooled M helps the included source population on average and hurts the excluded target.

The source code explains why the categories change. A6 uses

`mu_g = mean(all non-target source events in group g)`

`M_cg(T) = retained_weight_sum_cg(T) * mu_g(T)`

`C_cg(T) = S_cg(T) - M_cg(T)`.

It does **not** compute a separate `mu_cg` for each source. At K=2 the pool has one foreign cohort; at K=8 it has seven. Source-specific structure moves into the residual as the pool broadens. Residuals can cancel in the sum while each contains substantial useful memory and collateral effects. This reconciles the small A5 aggregate C attribution with the large A6 source-level C effects; they are different contrasts, also evaluated on different nonlinear backgrounds.

For a fixed group and checkpoint, the *linear* M transfer matrix is exactly an outer product: destination sensitivity to `mu_g` times source retained weight. Its rank is at most one **by construction**. The four-group sum has rank at most four. Finding that rank would not independently establish a biological low-rank collateral mechanism. Nonlinear per-source Shapley values need not obey this rank bound or add across sources.

Thus `T = D + L + E` remains a possible descriptive model. Its components have not been identified as independent self memory, low-rank nuisance and random noise by A6.

**3. Does the behavioral metric resolve weak Euclidean alignment? Only partially, not in the strong form proposed.**

I used the source-derived derivative of signed margin at the native state, including its clipping mask. `W` gives equal weight to the currently scored facts, matching the behavioral population. I also compared exact finite state-removal effects to avoid relying solely on a local derivative.

| Load | Euclidean cosine: actual M vs LOBO M | Readout-metric cosine, all active facts | Cosine of exact finite-removal effects |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| K=2 forward | 0.2364 | 0.2833 | 0.3177 |
| K=2 reverse | 0.0368 | −0.0126 | 0.0158 |
| K=8 | **0.3587** | **0.3309** | **0.4045** |

K=8 target-only readout cosine is 0.4137; other-cohort cosine is 0.3168. There is no dramatic hidden alignment of the aggregate correction with the whole held-out M.

A complementary check projects every donor group into the *same held-out readout* before comparing donors. K=8 donor-pair cosines rise from 0.1144 Euclidean to 0.1843 functional on average. The strongest improvement is acquisition role 0, 0.1940→0.3586; challenge role 0 is only 0.0501→0.0662. Some functional sharing exists, but it is uneven and weak in the emphasized challenge regime.

The original singular spectra reproduce. K=8 group-average pairwise cosines range 0.0501–0.1940 and participation ranks 5.99–7.37. Those ranks are computed from only **eight block vectors**, so they are ranks relative to a maximum of eight, not an estimate that the 5,177-dimensional memory has only six or seven functional dimensions. Across K=2 reverse, some mean cosines are approximately zero or slightly negative, outside the memo's shorthand 0.05–0.19 range.

A positive behavioral effect does not require accurate prediction of the entire M, which also contains useful information. Nor does it prove that `mu = useful + stable nuisance + nuisance variation` is the unique explanation. That decomposition is not identifiable from the current observations alone.

**4. Broad margin improvement or a few threshold crossings? A mixed population shift with substantial losses.**

For K=8, across 9,216 repeated fact-checkpoint evaluations:

| Quantity | Result |
|---|---:|
| Mean / median signed-margin change | +1.0718 / +0.9825 |
| Margin improves / worsens | 5,094 / 4,084 (55.27% / 44.31%) |
| Wrong→correct / correct→wrong | 1,295 / 731 |
| Net correct gain | 564 = +6.1198 pp |
| 10th / 90th percentile margin change | −10.3304 / +12.3342 |
| Cohort×checkpoint cells with lower accuracy | 161 / 768 |
| Cohort histories with lower mean accuracy | 7 / 64 |

All 12 checkpoint-stage averages and all eight block averages improve. Averaging repeated observations within each fact first still gives about 56.60% of distinct block-facts a positive mean margin change. The gain is therefore not confined to a tiny handful of repeated probes. But it is also not broadly harmless denoising: many decisions are lost, and seven cohort histories decline, the worst by 7.6389 pp. These repeated observations are not independent statistical samples.

At K=2 forward, active accuracy rises while the mean margin changes by **−0.0161**; K=2 reverse gives +0.2754. Threshold redistribution therefore contributes materially, especially at low load. Mean margin, accuracy and tail harm must remain separate endpoints.

**5. Is the same functional nuisance present at K=2 and K=8? There is shared structure, but not an established common cause.**

LOBO improves accuracy already at K=2, so the phenomenon is not exclusive to high-load accumulation. Predictors estimated from K=2 and K=8 donor runs also have related directions: on the K=8 recipient's readout, forward-K2 versus K8 predictor cosine is **0.8055**, and reverse-K2 versus K8 is **0.7268**; ordinary state-space cosines are 0.6666 and 0.6093.

This comparison keeps the recipient's group weights and readout fixed and changes donor load. It measures direction similarity only. It is **not** a cross-load learning intervention or proof that a K2-calibrated correction can safely be deployed at K8. The donor worlds overlap across load conditions, the constituent source pools differ, and native states/clipping differ. These limitations are especially relevant because per-source M/C attribution changes with load.

**Two further corrections to the memo**

The quoted 5.738 / 5.740 / 6.034 / 6.035 nested-model values have the sign of **native minus mean-subtracted active margin**. They describe the contribution retained by keeping M. Actual active-margin changes upon subtraction are **−5.738 / −5.740 / −6.034 / −6.035**. They are not centering gains. Role adds little to this particular same-block summary, but that alone does not prove a context-only LOBO predictor preserves behavior or that an online learner can infer the context.

The proposed Rayleigh ratio is not yet an appropriate objective. Squaring `r_self^T v` rewards a large wrong-sign self effect. Squaring all foreign projections penalizes helpful foreign changes as well as harmful ones. Its full foreign readout matrix also assumes access to old memories and scoring information unavailable to a coordinate-local rule. Signed incoming noninferiority, explicit old-choice/tail costs and a local-information constraint would be needed before using such a surrogate. A2's repaired oracle/local-policy work already demonstrates that approximating an oracle is a separate unresolved problem.

**What can be answered about `x → u → w_slow → Rw` now?**

The existing archive supports the ends of the chain better than its middle. A6 retains encoded inputs, native states and group means; A4 retains actual slow-write/source matrices. A3 retains prototype cosine matrices, event norms, and selected exact/linear effects. It does not retain the full signed, coordinate-aligned raw `u` vectors needed to re-estimate cross-block context-conditioned drive means and their propagated effects at the same events.

Accordingly, I cannot honestly locate the earliest stage that creates the putative nuisance from these saved summaries. In particular, an ordinary mean or overlap in nonnegative x is not itself evidence of harmful interference; harm is relative to a task/readout. Finding a large effect only after projection also does not, by itself, prove the reader is the defective component. Norm comparisons across x/u/w are not a causal test.

**Recommended next step**

Keep FULL_151 as the reference. Do not turn the present pooled-M decomposition directly into a centering rule or reopen evolution on that premise.

The next bounded diagnostic should first remove the source-pooling ambiguity: compare source-specific, pooled and leave-source-out means under one common destination/readout population, reporting useful self effects, excluded-target effects and other-source effects separately. The saved K8 source rows are sufficient for that analysis. For the upstream question, capture coordinate-aligned `dx`, raw drive `u`, actual slow innovation and allowed pre-event local state at the already selected A3 windows, preserving the native trajectory and including K2 source rows. Apply one locked out-of-block prediction/effect analysis across those stages; context labels may stratify the audit but may not enter the learner.

Only if that identifies a predictable **signed collateral component while preserving self benefit** should one local transformation be tested in native continuous learning, with all-active choices, incoming learning and tail harm checked on fresh worlds. The present audit provides a narrower empirical target; it does not establish that a deployable persistent-learning core has been recovered.
