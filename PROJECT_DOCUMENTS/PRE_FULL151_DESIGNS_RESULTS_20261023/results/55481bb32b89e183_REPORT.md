# FULL_151 byte-bridge behavioral comparison

**Result:** BRIDGE_HARM. On 64 new worlds, the final held-out choice difference-in-differences `(BRIDGE_W−BRIDGE_N)−(FE0_W−FE0_N)` was -0.2188 (95% world-level Student-t interval -0.3319 to -0.1056). This is the fixed primary; no world was selected by outcome.

The bridge's own prior-native-write effect was +0.0156; FE0's was +0.2344. Final absolute held-out choices were BRIDGE_W 0.5417, BRIDGE_N 0.5260, FE0_W 0.7500, and FE0_N 0.5156. Immediate taught-pair W choices were BRIDGE 0.6510 and FE0 0.9427. The primary world signs were {'positive': 14, 'zero': 10, 'negative': 40}.

This was already an acquisition problem. At the old-teaching endpoint, BRIDGE_W scored 0.4896 on held-out pairs versus FE0_W 0.8125; their matched N arms scored 0.4375 and 0.5052. On taught pairs at that same point, BRIDGE_W scored 0.6510 versus FE0_W 0.9427. Thus the bridge did not merely lose a previously strong signal during the later cohort or delay. The absolute final bridge W score was only 0.5417, and its W−N interval includes zero. This architecture has not demonstrated E3 learning benefit despite the earlier E0 access pass. FE0 retains the narrow E3 benefit on this fresh roster.

The task presented two candidate byte orientations. A fixed policy read the inherited native value circuit and emitted `L` or `R` before feedback. It was not a learned action head, free-running byte production, or arithmetic. Held-out ordered pairs had no teaching in either orientation. The exact-pair recitation countermodel is at chance; a learned one-bit class per symbol can solve this narrow relation. W/N shared bytes, labels, predictor updates, clocks, and new-cohort writes; only old native writes differed. BRIDGE/FE0 shared those histories and differed only in the fixed sensory projection. The E0 bridge access result predates this run. The evidence level here is limited E3 within one synthetic relation if a positive native-write effect is present; otherwise report only the observed behavior. Nothing here certifies broad persistent learning or autonomous language output.

**Next decision:** Do not route W4 arithmetic or a larger learner through this bridge yet. The fixed gain-0.5 predictive projection changes both write-time and read-time native KC codes; this four-arm experiment identifies the resulting behavioral harm but cannot tell whether write/read mismatch, reduced task geometry, the chosen projection/gain, or online predictor adaptation caused it. A small fresh-world, prospective write-route × read-route diagnostic should separate those possibilities and measure trained/held-out KC overlap and reward-direction responses. Its endpoint should remain early taught and held-out choice, not KC separation alone. This is a front-end alignment problem to localize before changing Full151 memory or resuming action-head/evolution work.
