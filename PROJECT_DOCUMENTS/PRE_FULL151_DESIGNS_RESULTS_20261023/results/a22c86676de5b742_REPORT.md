# Step 7 — E3 emitted-byte bridge stopped at its frozen feasibility gate

**Status: `PILOT_QUALIFICATION_FAILED`; no confirmation worlds were run.** The new assay asked whether the Step-6 fading-pair front end plus two native Full151 output stores could emit a binary answer for a never-reinforced V9.9A relation instance after delayed and intervening learning. It did **not** test that E3 claim: the predeclared eight-world feasibility pilot missed its old-trained positive-control threshold. One excluded engineering world and all eight pilot worlds completed; the planned 64 confirmation worlds remain unexecuted.

## What was tested

The unchanged V9.8 E3 generator supplied six uppercase symbols divided into two hidden classes. Both orientations of six P–N edges were taught across 144 records; three edges, both orientations, were never taught. A 72-record cohort on disjoint symbols followed a simulated day later, with a final probe after another day. Every record kept the original 12-byte cue, constant `!` outcome, newline, and external Boolean teaching label. The Step-6 fixed fading-pair front end drove two copied Full151 value stores; the externally supplied label routed neutral teaching to the correct output channel and aversive teaching to the other. The model selected ASCII `0` or `1` from those stores at the fixed response point **before** outcome/feedback. W and N saw the same bytes, labels, and time; only W had old writes enabled. Both wrote during the new cohort. Prediction weights were frozen, as in Step 6. This was an external-teacher output bridge, not a reproduction of V9.9A's evaluator-read endpoint or a byte-derived-teacher test.

## Frozen qualification result

The excluded engineering world passed source and checkpoint locks, fixture/exposure checks, two-store birth equality, byte and teacher counts, W/N sensory parity, native clocks, read-only probe purity, and response-before-feedback assertions. Its runtime was 6.16 s. Eight prespecified pilot worlds then ran in 49.12 s total under `caffeinate`; none was replaced or rerun. The pilot was excluded from any confirmation estimate.

| Pilot guard, eight worlds | Observed | Frozen requirement | Result |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Old-end W output on 12 directly taught cues | **62/96 = 64.58%** | ≥70% | **Fail** |
| Old-end W−N on taught cues | +14/96 = +14.58 pp | ≥10 pp | Pass |
| New-end W output on six new taught cues | 37/48 = 77.08% | ≥65% | Pass |
| New-end N output on six new taught cues | 37/48 = 77.08% | ≥65% | Pass |

Old-end N was 48/96 = 50%, the deterministic `0` tie baseline on balanced labels. W's old-trained accuracy was 29/48 for target `0` and 33/48 for target `1`; this was not merely failure of one output byte. The new-cohort guards show that the teacher router and native writes can support a different six-cue task in both branches. They do not rescue the missed old-trained gate. Pilot held-out outputs were recorded for receipt completeness but were **not inspected for the go/no-go decision or interpreted as E3 evidence**.

An exploratory **read-only** address census on engineering-world taught cues found a minimum of 52 active-KC support differences between different-label cues and median 114; same-label pairs had median 122. A preceding record changed the same cue's support by median 0, maximum 4. This does not establish a behavioral distance threshold or identify the cause of the pilot failure. It suggests the tested code mainly distinguishes individual pairs, rather than grouping pairs by the answer class. The stronger 12-cue workload, two-channel opponent teaching, and representation may interact; the present receipts do not separate them.

## Interpretation and next decision

The frozen continuation rule prohibits the 64-world run after this failed pilot. Therefore there is **no new E3 emitted-output estimate**, no conclusion that the model cannot transfer, and no valid comparison with V9.9A's +17.19 pp evaluator-read effect. The narrow conclusion is that this specific integrated route did not establish sufficiently reliable output on the **already taught** old relation cues in eight qualification worlds. A targeted next diagnostic should hold the V9.9A relation/output task fixed and distinguish input-address effects from the two-channel opponent-teaching policy before another E3 confirmation is attempted. Any changed interface requires a new design and fresh worlds; this source-locked Step 7 is not to be edited or restarted.

The independent [audit](results/AUDIT.json) checked the source and parent locks, pilot/world rosters, original generator and exposure hashes, response argmax, teaching targets, write and clock ledgers, and all pilot gate arithmetic. It rejected six intentional receipt alterations (cue, teacher, output, writes, source digest, and roster). See [design](DESIGN.md), [technical qualification](results/TECHNICAL.json), [pilot gate](results/PILOT.json), and the eight compact [pilot receipts](results/pilot). No scientific failure receipt was emitted; the stop was the prescribed feasibility decision.
