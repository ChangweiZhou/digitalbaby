# Step 12 — when does the fading-pair route lose held-out choice?

**Result (27 September 2026): the final choice deficit was already present immediately after old teaching.** In 32 fresh paired worlds, FE0's old-end W−N choice advantage on three never-reinforced symbol pairs was **+23.96 percentage points** (world-level 95% interval **+10.23 to +37.69**). FE0+fading pairs gave **+3.13 points** (**−11.94 to +18.19**). The paired difference, pair minus FE0, was **−20.83 points** (**−39.84 to −1.83**). Both routes learned old taught cues well. This is a localization diagnostic, not a new generalization or output-autonomy claim.

## Frozen comparison and exposure boundary

The [design](DESIGN.md) held the V9.8 relation generator, V9.2 parent, single native Full151 value store, old/new teaching streams and clocks, and relative-value scoring fixed. Only the label-free input front end differed: FE0 or the Step 6 fixed FE0+fading-pair code. Each world taught 12 old ordered cues over 144 records; three cross-class symbol edges in both directions (six cue bytestrings) were never reinforced. W enabled old native writes, while N saw the same bytes and teacher bits with those writes disabled. Both states then had a day of rest and were each forked into ON/OFF branches for the same 72 new-cohort records; only ON allowed new native writes. The new symbols were disjoint from the old symbols. A strict or whitespace-canonicalized pair-key table has no held-out answer; fixed frequency and position cues are balanced, while a one-bit class rule inferred from old examples could solve this narrowly synthetic E3 task.

All probes used discarded clones and supplied no feedback. At birth, old-teaching end, one day later, new-cohort end, and final day, the evaluator compared native values for the held-out orientations. The learner did **not** emit a byte. Old and new *taught* pairs were measured as positive controls. The original [Step 11 result](../FULL151_BRIDGE_STEP11_INPUT_ATTRIBUTION_20260927/REPORT.md) is background; none of its worlds were rerun or pooled into the new intervals.

The pair-minus-FE0 old-teaching effect was **−26.04 pp** [−42.96, −9.12] in the final OFF branches and **−32.29 pp** [−49.15, −15.44] in the final ON branches. The pair route's no-old-write branch began near chance at birth (53.13%), was 51.04% at old end, and remained 53.13% at final ON. Its final `N_ON−N_OFF` difference was **−1.04 pp** [−10.44, +8.36]. The old-teaching effect's final `ON−OFF` change was **−1.04 pp** [−13.81, +11.72]. Thus this run does **not** support later new writing as the source of pair's missing choice advantage or of Step 11's unusual 65.63% N baseline. That earlier high baseline did not recur in these new worlds; its particular cause remains unresolved.

The choice metric signs each of three native value margins per world. On the continuous scale, pair's old-end W−N mean margin was **+0.0857** [−0.0849, +0.2562], versus FE0 **+0.0937** [+0.0564, +0.1310]. The pair route's very broad interval and 49/96 positive per-pair W−N changes (FE0: 67/96) caution against saying it learned *no* shared information. It did not turn that information into reliable correct relative choices. These pair counts and all intervals are descriptive; pairs within a world are not independent sample units. The multiple stage/source intervals localize a mechanism and are not separately multiplicity-adjusted confirmation claims.

Positive controls show native writes remained effective. Immediately after old teaching, W correctly ranked **89.58%** of directly taught old pairs for both inputs, versus FE0 N 49.48% and pair N 45.83%. At the end of the new cohort, ON branches scored FE0 **83.33%/92.71%** (old W/N origins) and pair **88.54%/97.92%** on the *new taught* pairs; their OFF counterparts were materially lower. The missing held-out choice signal is therefore not a blanket failure to write or retain taught associations.

No `FAILURE.json` exists. Science learner runtime totaled about **210 seconds**; the result directory is about **768 KB**.

The immediate Step 12 finding redirects the next design decision away from passive persistence or later-write protection as the *first* explanation. FE0's shared input continues to support limited held-out relative choice, whereas the current pair-enriched input mainly supports directly taught discrimination. This is compatible with pattern-separation versus feature-sharing as a hypothesis, but neither KC displacement nor a universal incompatibility has been causally established. Claude's proposed raw 1:1 parallel-bank sum can already be calculated exploratorily from Step 11 same-world margins and does not preserve FE0's held-out advantage; it should not be launched as a supposedly ready fix. Any later combined route needs a separately frozen readout, memory-capacity control, and a genuinely common architectural claim across the crowded-fact and relation tasks. No further architecture or parameter search was started here.
