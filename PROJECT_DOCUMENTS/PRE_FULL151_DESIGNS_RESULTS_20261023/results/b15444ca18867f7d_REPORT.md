# FE0 retention under the causal byte protocol — report

**Date:** 25 September 2026.
**Design:** `DESIGN.md`, fixed before the run; its hash is in `RESULTS.json`.
**Scale:** 16 new worlds × 2 arms, 27 s in total.
**Status:** an engineering screen. It is not a full-life confirmation, and it changes nothing in the S1–S5 roadmap.

## Result

**Under the current `CAUSAL_DT` protocol (dose 30/14 s), FE0's byte-cue associations from the first block survive 24 hours, 12 bouts of re-teaching of 12 neighbouring pairs, and a second 24 hours.** Both pre-specified conditions hold:

| | Difference A − B in paired choice (16 worlds, 95% CI) | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Gate: T1, end of the first block | +0.125 [+0.094, +0.156] | POS |
| **Primary: T3, after rest, challenge 0 and rest** | **+0.097 [+0.059, +0.135]** | **POS** |

- **Per world:** D3 is ≥ 0 in all 16 worlds, and exactly 0 in two of them.
- **Arms:** A has writing on from the start; B had writing off during the first block and on from challenge 0.
- **Readout:** both cues of each of the 36 untouched old pairs are read from copies of the learner, with no feedback.

## What the data say, and what they do not

- **The first 24 hours cost nothing measurable.** A's choice goes from 0.634 to 0.646 and D from +0.125 to +0.139. A's fast memory decays to about zero over this rest (norm 71.8 → ≈ 0), while its slow memory is unchanged (91.8 → 91.1). The association read at T2 is therefore held in slow memory.
- **There is a partial loss after challenge 0 and the second rest.** A's choice falls from 0.646 to 0.585 and D from +0.139 to +0.097. The design places challenge 0 and the second 24 hours in the same interval, so it cannot separate their effects. The only evidence on decay alone is the first 24 hours, which showed no loss.
- **The effect is specific to trained cues.** On pairs never shown, neither arm differs from chance, and neither do the arms from each other.
- **Re-teaching 12 pairs did not help the other 36.** After B learned the 12 challenge-0 pairs (its slow-memory norm went from 0 to 82.6), its choice on the 36 untouched pairs stayed at chance (0.488).
- **"T1" is the end of the block, not immediately after each pair.** The probed pairs were last trained between about 0 and 26 hours before T1. T1 is therefore lower than v8's bout-by-bout probe for FE0 (0.775–0.804).
- **The size is modest.** After two days, A chooses correctly on 58.5% of old pairs. This screens FE0 only, with one block, a teaching dose that is not calibrated to the old `event(30)`, and the frozen raw readout. It says nothing about TRACE88 or text.

## Checks

All pass in every world:
- **Accounting:** 864 records; 13 byte commits, 1 teaching event and 1 gap per record; 2 rests of 86,400 s. All clocks agree at 315,360 s.
- **Inputs:** the two arms get identical pre-outcome codes on every record.
- **B's first block:** B's fast and slow memory are exactly zero at T1.
  - PC: A's memory is non-zero at T1, and B's slow memory becomes non-zero after challenge 0.
- **Probe set:** exactly 36 pairs, disjoint from challenge 0's 12 targets. Their truth equals the delivery in the first block.
- **Probes are pure:** the learner's digest is unchanged by every probe set.
  - NC: a probe that commits on the real learner changes the digest.
