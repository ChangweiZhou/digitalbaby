# MiniFly V9.4 — new-byte writing and older byte memory

## Result

**The locked V9.4 primary gate found an immediate write-specific cost to old
ordered-byte choices.** After a 24 h old-memory interval, 32 fresh worlds
received 12 bouts of new ordered-byte associations. From each identical
pre-challenge state, `NEW_WRITE` and `NEW_NO_WRITE` saw the same bytes,
reward bits, and clock. The only intervention was whether the inherited
Full_151 value pathway made plastic writes. At new bout 12, old-pair choice
was **70.83% with writes versus 82.29% without writes**, a paired
**−11.46 percentage-point effect [95% t CI −21.86, −1.06]** over the 32
generated worlds. The precommitted label is
`WRITE_SPECIFIC_OLD_HARM`. Both positive controls passed: the old set was
learned before challenge (76.04% choice, +27.08 points against old
write-off), and the new set was learned by bout 12 (88.54% choice,
+37.50 points against same-stream new write-off).

This finding is **short term in deterministic choice**. At +24 h after the
new block, old choice was 76.04% in both challenge arms; the paired
difference was 0.00 points [−3.05, +3.05]. A smaller analog margin deficit
remained (−0.0060 Hz [−0.0111, −0.0009], secondary). The experiment
therefore does not demonstrate lasting erasure or protection of old
behavior under repeated subsequent learning.

## What the causal contrast says

The old pre-challenge choice was 76.04%. At new bout 12, `NEW_WRITE`
was 70.83%, a **−5.21-point change from its own baseline**, while matched
`NEW_NO_WRITE` was 82.29%, a **+6.25-point change**. A time-only branch
remained at 76.04%. Thus the −11.46-point primary contrast combines the
cost of active new writing with a transient benefit of seeing the new-byte
stream without value writes. It is the *total causal effect of the plastic
write flag under this fixed input schedule*, not a measured synaptic
erasure amount. The no-write-versus-time-only difference vanished by
+24 h.

Of 96 old reversed pairs, 73 had a correct margin immediately before
challenge. By bout 12, **16 of those 73** became incorrect or tied under
new writing, versus **0** under matched write-off. Some previously
incorrect pairs improved, so this transition count is not the net choice
effect. At +24 h, only one initially correct pair remained lost in the
write arm and none in write-off. The worst old-pair write-minus-write-off
margin averaged −0.0618 Hz [−0.0833, −0.0403] at bout 12.

We did not resolve whether the effect tracks the symbol shared by old and
new cue sets. Exploratory old-pair choice effects were −9.38 points for the two pairs
containing that symbol and −15.63 points for the unshared pair, with both
intervals crossing zero. Pre-challenge cross-set KC-code Jaccard overlap
averaged 0.699. This measures representation overlap; it does not assign
a causal site of interference.

The prespecified evaluator-side noise calculation assumes independent
Gaussian noise on the two option values. At 0.05 Hz SD, it gives an
immediate old-choice effect of −4.37 points [−8.60, −0.15] and a
+24 h effect of −1.83 points [−3.32, −0.35]. These are sensitivity
calculations, not fly physiology or observed noisy behavior.

## Robustness and byte-prediction coexistence

The formal result uses the protocol's two-sided 95% t interval over whole
generated worlds. It is **boundary-sensitive**: 14 worlds had a negative
old-choice effect, 14 had zero, and four had a positive effect; an
exploratory exhaustive two-sided world sign-flip calculation gives
`p=0.05127`. It is not a design-based randomization test; symmetry of
world effects was not established.
Leaving out either world 320 or 325 makes the t interval cross zero.
The locked label stands, but the result should not be described as a
large or method-independent effect. Worlds vary their generated byte
symbols while retaining one fixed fly/model seed, so these intervals do
not represent variation across flies.

Predictor and native value changes remained on separate routes. The
predictor state was bit-identical between new-write and new-write-off after
every record. Four prespecified sentinel worlds were scored on frozen,
no-reset 20,000-byte continuations from the common pre-challenge state
and post-challenge state. Doyle full-stream BPB was roughly
2.814–2.816 before and 2.819–2.822 after the challenge, still below
the locked train-only Markov-2 value 3.001. Stevenson was roughly
2.728–2.730 before and 2.735–2.736 after, still below 2.926;
content-byte checks also passed. The small drift is from reading the
synthetic stream, not the value-write flag. This still does not show
predictor features teaching the native value circuit.

## Assay integrity and limits

The independent design and code audits, the superseded technical
index-900 lock-comparison incident, and the corrected index-901 smoke
are documented in [AUDIT.md](AUDIT.md). No scientific world ran before
the source-matched 901 gate passed. All **32/32** science receipts passed
source, deterministic schedule, exact prestate, raw-byte count, clock,
predictor-route, probe-purity, and reward-balance checks. The current
source-lock digest is
`a0d5f02458fd51993d1552538d1a0bc8f2112f7259a89d43af0737e32d7db732`.
Independent post-run reviewers recomputed the positive controls, primary
interval, transition counts and locked status from the per-world receipts.

This task trains six old and six new ordered cues, each 12 times, with one
shared byte but no repeated old ordered pair. The model gets an external
reward bit after a constant `!` outcome byte. It does not test autonomous
reward inference, untrained-sequence generalization, higher load,
multiple new cohorts, or the original PN-cue A11 B/HASH8 comparison.
Its +24 h post-challenge probe has no intervening learning. Those are
limits on both the harm finding and the apparent recovery.

## Next decision

V9.4 identifies a transient write-versus-exposure effect and leaves the
persistent-core question open. The next A11-like byte experiment should
test whether this cost **accumulates or persists** as several fresh
byte-conditioned cohorts arrive. Keep the current byte checkpoint,
the matched write-off control and a fixed old-target set. Make old choice,
old-pair loss and new learning simultaneous endpoints; probe after each
cohort and after rest. A bounded K8/L1 versus K8/L4 comparison is a
candidate scope only after `K` and `L` are defined for byte-conditioned
cohorts; add K16 only under a separately frozen scope. Measure runtime and
precision before choosing sample size; do not inherit the old PN-cue A11
sample size or infer a new topology from this V9.4 result alone.
