# MiniFly V9.9B — frozen REF on a second, graded relation

**Prospectively locked design, 26 September 2026.** This is a bounded second-family E3 qualification of the unchanged V9.7 `REF` native value learner on the same V9.2 parent checkpoint and byte interface used in V9.9A. No topology, bank, rule, dose, decoder, or model parameter is selected by pilot outcomes. Engineering world 9902, feasibility worlds 1401–1412, and independent confirmation worlds 1501–1564 are disjoint from V9.8/V9.9A and from one another. The pilot may trigger or stop the confirmation according to the rule below; it is never included in the confirmation estimate.

## Claim and exposure ledger

In each world, randomly assign eight distinct uppercase bytes to hidden ranks 1–8. Present two orientations of 20 unordered comparisons, four times each (160 old records). The outcome/teaching label is aversive when the higher-ranked symbol appears first and neutral when the lower-ranked symbol appears first. Use the V9.8 12-byte frame: nine constant space bytes, two symbol bytes, one trailing space, followed by outcome byte `!` and newline. Presentation order is randomized separately by bout. Both orientations of every taught comparison have identical frequency; every ranked symbol participates in exactly five taught comparisons and appears equally often in the two byte positions. Old outcomes are globally balanced.

The eight **never-taught** undirected rank pairs are fixed *before symbol randomization*:

`(1,3), (3,5), (5,7), (7,2), (2,4), (4,6), (6,8), (8,1)`.

Neither orientation of these pairs occurs in old teaching, new-cohort teaching, or any other feedback event. Their first probes are read-only and occur before feedback on them. The later cohort uses three other randomly selected uppercase symbols, the unchanged V9.8 balanced cyclic-label generator, and 72 records. It receives native writes in `L` but not in `S`/`R`.

An exact raw-string or canonicalized pair-key table has no taught answer for the held-out pairs (0.5 with tie scoring). Symbol identity, occurrence count, byte position, and sensory stream are matched between `W` and `N`; class assignment is randomized across worlds. A scalar learned from labeled old comparisons can solve all eight held-out pairs: each symbol's taught high-rank win count is ordered correctly on those eight pairs. This is graded feature reuse, **not** evidence that the fly computes an explicit transitive closure. Exhaustive enumeration of all 2^8 assignments to one binary category per symbol gives a maximum held-out choice of 0.75 for a binary class comparator (ties count 0.5). The generator verifies the exact-table, win-count, transitive-order, and binary-class baselines before science. These countermodels are analytic controls, never installed in the learner.

## Causal state and schedule

`W` and `N` begin as exact copies of the frozen parent, receive the same bytes, labels, and clock times, and learn the same engineered byte predictor. Only `W` permits **old native value writes**; `N` disables them. Scored choices use the inherited native value circuit on read-only clones. After old training, measure taught and held-out comparisons at `old_end`, wait 24 h and measure held-out pairs at `old_day`. Fork both states at the exact same `old_day` prestate into `R` (no new bytes), `S` (new bytes and teaching calls, no new native writes), and `L` (identical new bytes with native writes). Measure held-out and new-cohort pairs at `new_end` for S/L; wait another 24 h; measure held-out and new-cohort pairs in R/S/L and taught old pairs in L at the common final clock. New-cohort probes are first responses on *trained new pairs*, and are a guard that genuine later learning occurred; they do not count as E3 transfer.

`W−N` on held-out pairs isolates the effect of prior native teaching. `S−R` describes sensory/nonplastic change; `L−S` describes the effect of later native write flags under the same new stream. The final `W_L−N_L` comparison remains valid even if one or both secondary component contrasts are unresolved. The predictor digests must match for W/N after each common sensory history and for S/L after the new cohort. All probes must leave live state unchanged.

## Stages, gates, and interpretation

1. **Engineering preflight (9902):** prove fixture exposure, balanced counts, analytic countermodel scores (exact 0.5, binary maximum 0.75, win-count/oracle 1.0), nonidentical code for each probed opposite orientation, read-only probes, same prestates, clocks, write flags, predictor digests, and finite measures. Lock this design, runner, parent sources, and checkpoint by SHA-256 before any feasibility world.
2. **Feasibility pilot (1401–1412):** run all 12 frozen worlds once. Proceed to confirmation only if old-end mean taught `W` choice is at least 0.60, old-end held-out mean `W` choice exceeds 0.50, old-end held-out mean paired `W−N` exceeds zero, and both W and N have new-end mean new-pair `L−S` choice above +0.10. Technical failures stop the run. This pilot is a cost/assay gate, has no inferential status, and cannot be used to alter dose or task. A stopped pilot is reported as a negative/inconclusive qualification, not erased.
3. **Confirmation (1501–1564, only after pilot gate):** run exactly 64 untouched worlds without optional stopping. The sole confirmatory statistic is the world-paired final held-out choice difference `W_L−N_L`, with a two-sided 95% Student-*t* interval. Call it positive only if its lower bound exceeds zero; otherwise report negative/unresolved as appropriate. Also report the absolute final `W_L` world-level interval: its lower bound must exceed 0.5 for useful above-chance behavior. To make the *stronger* claim of graded performance beyond a one-bit category comparator, its lower bound must exceed 0.75. These are hierarchical interpretation guards, not extra routes to rescue a failed primary. Trained-old acquisition and new-cohort `L−S` learning are mandatory reported guards. Margins, R/S/L differences, world sign counts and intermediate probes are descriptive only; no secondary cell can replace the primary.

If taught pairs fail, first examine feedback dose and native writing; if opposite orientations share an indistinguishable byte code, examine the input interface; if taught learning succeeds but immediate held-out reuse fails, examine shared representation/readout; if immediate reuse survives but the final probe fails, use R/S/L to distinguish passage of time from new writes. These diagnoses precede any new eligibility, shared/private bank, topology, or evolution candidate. Passing V9.9A plus a genuinely graded V9.9B supports a **bounded** persistent learning core on two synthetic relation families, not general reasoning, natural-language competence, or a minimality theorem.
