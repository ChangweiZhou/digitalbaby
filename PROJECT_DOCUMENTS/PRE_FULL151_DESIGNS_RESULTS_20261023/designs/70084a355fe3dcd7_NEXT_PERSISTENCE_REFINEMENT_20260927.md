# Visible-context → Full151: next-stage persistence refinement

**Status: review note, not a frozen or runnable protocol. No experiment was started by this note.** The [completed bridge report](REPORT.md) remains unchanged. Its 32-world result establishes reliable, directly taught, prompted one-byte output for 16 `a+b=` cues through a fixed last-four-visible-byte address and native Full151 writing. Canonical, spacing and fixed-`Q` forms have the same effective address; their E2 scores do not demonstrate retrieval across changed internal codes. The learned-`h` bridge remains unqualified.

## Corrections to the proposed interpretation

- The excluded technical world's 16 content-route KC supports have **zero 16-way intersection**, not zero pairwise overlap. Each support has 184 of 5,177 KCs; 119 of 120 cue pairs overlap, by 0–63 KCs. The global minimum pairwise Hamming distance is 242. The **mean of the 16 per-cue nearest-neighbor distances** is 279.75 (the source of “280”). These are birth-state geometry diagnostics, not a causal analysis of forgetting.
- The 16-cue behavioral pass rules out a hard inability of this Full151/four-store/teaching configuration to learn *these* directly taught associations. It does not prove all earlier failures were caused only by input overlap, nor prove that Full151 has ample capacity at higher loads.
- The existing V9.5 experiment correctly compares `WWWW` with `NNNN` **after both branches share an old-trained prestate**. Its final old-choice effect was about −11.3 percentage points. Saved V9.5 overlap varies little, so it does not identify overlap as the cause. Under the new address, either a small or large write effect would describe that new workload; neither result alone would prove why V9.5 behaved differently. In particular, content-route pairwise KC overlap is low but not zero.
- A 16-old + four-by-16-new design would jump from 16 to 80 effective keys while changing the tested cue distribution. A failed old-set score could then reflect capacity, old/new interference, or both. The approximate two-hour runtime is not qualified, and a confidence interval lying inside ±5 percentage points is an equivalence claim that needs a separate precision rationale. The more relevant one-sided operational claim is that the write cost is **not worse than −5 points**, contingent on a justified interval and competence gates; otherwise report `UNRESOLVED`.
- A CONTENT-versus-PAIR retention difference would not isolate KC overlap: the routes produce different old acquisition, write states, and query geometry, and PAIR already had uneven cue-level recall at 16 facts. PAIR is therefore not a positive control for the causal persistence contrast. V9.5's FE0 result is useful historical context, not a same-assay mechanistic control.

## Recommended next experiment: functional persistence at controlled load

Keep the fixed visible-context input, native Full151 checkpoint, four output stores, answer-byte teacher, `=`-prompted first-byte output, and 12 exposures per cue. Do not add a learned encoder, pair route, free output, or an E3 task to this experiment. This is a functional E1 persistence test, not an anatomical or overlap-mechanism attribution test.

1. Predeclare 16 old directly taught keys, then four sequential new cohorts of **four keys each** (32 keys total). All displayed records retain the same `a+b=` byte format. Choose new digit pairs outside the old roster and balance arbitrary answer labels `0`–`3` within each four-key cohort. Verify all 32 **effective last-four-visible-byte addresses** are distinct; a mere distinct full-line string or a prefix is insufficient. Seal the entire roster, labels, schedules, and source before science. Do not choose worlds based on learned outcomes or measured KC separation.
2. Teach the old set with writes on, score its first read-only output, and advance the simulated clock 24 h. Check old acquisition and the old prechallenge baseline. From that **identical old-trained state**, fork `WWWW` (writes on for all four new cohorts) and `NNNN` (the same bytes, teacher bytes, calls and clock, with new native writes off). Probe old and newly taught sets after each cohort; advance a further simulated 24 h after cohort 4 and probe before feedback.
3. Primary estimand: across sealed worlds, the paired final old-set emitted-byte accuracy difference `WWWW − NNNN`. This isolates the total effect of permitting the **new** native writes under this workload. Report the continuous correct-class-minus-best-competing-class margin as a separate supporting readout. A same-`WWWW`-prestate no-write shadow of each new cohort can verify that cohort's acquisition; it is not an additional discovery endpoint. `CLOCK_ONLY`, `WWWN`, and `NNNW` are unnecessary for the primary functional decision and should be added only if their separate questions and resource cost are justified before source freeze.
4. Interpretation gates: old teaching must beat an old no-write control; the old prechallenge and `NNNN` final scores must remain above the 25% four-byte-choice floor; each new cohort must be acquired in the writing history (preferably against its same-prestate no-write shadow). If these fail, report the full data but mark the persistence contrast insensitive. Analyze whole-world paired outcomes, never 16 probes as independent replicates. Choose the world count and any −5-point noninferiority claim from a **pre-run precision and resource plan**; do not assume V9.5's variance or extend the bank after looking at outcomes.
5. Before running science, qualify exact write/output-before-teacher order, identical fork states, read-only probes, unique effective keys, write-call ledgers, clock/flush behavior, independent receipt audit, and realistic full-life wall/RSS/disk in the final logging mode. The 24 h delays are simulated, not wall-clock sleeps. The main histories contain 192 old + 192 new teaching records, before control/shadow copies, so the earlier 16-key runtime cannot simply be multiplied by five.

**Decision boundary.** Strong final old performance with a sufficiently precise small `WWWW − NNNN` cost would establish only tested-load persistence of the engineered byte→Full151 bridge. A negative contrast, with learning and floor gates passing, would establish write-associated old-memory harm under that bridge and justify a later Full151 memory/teaching change while keeping the input fixed. Neither outcome identifies the cause of V9.5's loss or promotes a persistent learning core/E3 claim. A separate controlled-overlap manipulation would be required for a causal overlap claim; it is deliberately outside this next experiment.

This 32-key lifetime is intentionally not V9.5's 80-key proposal. If its write effect is small, that result does **not** certify 80-key capacity or four 16-key cohorts. Capacity at 80 keys would be a separate decision, not an automatic enlargement of the persistence bank.
