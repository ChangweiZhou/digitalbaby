## 1. What this round can and cannot establish

| Claim | Licensed by | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **Usable gain** | A1 | The complete strategy (front end + frozen learner + declared readout) predicts delivered consequences better than FE0 on the actual stream. |
| **Identity-dependent gain** | C_id | The candidate's discrimination advantage over FE0 shrinks by C_id when no cue identity ever recurs. Held identical meanwhile: the delivered-outcome sequence (times, sides, runs), the schedule, the wrappers and the consequence bytes. Any advantage from outcome timing, cross-record consequence traces, partner-in-trial effects or schedule structure is present on both sides and cancels. |
| **Identity part dominates** | B | The identity-dependent part exceeds the part of the advantage that survives identity removal (N). |
| **Retention** | R | Delayed (≥ 24 h) returns of pairs whose consequence has not changed are predicted better than by FE0. |
| **Sensitivity** | S_unp | The same difference-in-differences with the unpaired control. That control keeps cue identities and their presentation times, destroys contingency, and changes the outcome sequence. It is reported and sets the wording (§9.5); it does not gate. |

**Withdrawn.** "Write-dependent" is no longer claimed, for the reasons in §R. FEAS-1 does not say whether an identity-dependent advantage lives in the front end's state, in the learner's writes, or in their interaction.

**Not established:**
- **Own-cue vs partner-cue learning.** In R2 every trial pairs one aversive and one neutral cue. Learning a partner's recurring identity is also identity-dependent. Separating the two needs independent consequences (S2-ID at R8; roadmap §3.2a).
- **FK's own confound.** Removing key recurrence also changes front-end state that depends on repetition, such as FE1's habituation to repeated keys and FE-NI's self-cancellation. N therefore measures the candidate's non-identity abilities under a no-repetition regime, which may differ from its non-identity abilities in the intact stream. This is why UNP, whose confound is different, is run as a sensitivity analysis.

## 3. Interfaces and causality

| Component | Receives | Never receives |
|---|---|---|
| **Front end** | (byte, time) for every byte, in arrival order | The detector event; record boundaries; identity, role, key position, wrapper id; tags; truth |
| **Innate detector** (fixed; part of the animal) | Each arriving byte | Anything else |
| **Learner** (frozen) | The detector event; the front end's output at the detector moment | Truth, identity, role, wrapper id, tags |
| **Evaluator only** | Delivered and committed truth, identities, roles, tags, schedule, stream condition | — |

- **Detector event.** It fires when 0xF1 (US on) or 0xF0 (US off) arrives, so once per record, neutral records included. This is BYTE-CORE-1's S0 convention (§3.3, §4.2, amendment A1), not a fly finding. It is shared identically by all arms.
- **Read moment.** The front end is advanced to the detector byte's arrival time and read before that byte is integrated. The frozen adapter locates this position by scanning the record: lookahead in code, not in information. **Q2** requires a byte-by-byte driver to reproduce the adapter bit for bit in every arm. The driver commits the snapshot from before the byte once the detector fires, then teaches, then feeds the byte.
- **Read purity.** `read()` computes its output from the current state and modifies nothing: no caches, no counters. **Q3** inserts extra reads at random times and requires bit-identical later outputs and final state. NI-READLOCKED must fail.

## 4. Worlds and stream conditions

### 4.1 Namespaces

| Namespace | Block | Indices | Use |
|---|---|---|---|
| `FEAS1-ENG` | `Q` | 0–1 | Technical fixtures |
| `FEAS1-CAL` | `K` | 0–7 | Readout and NLMS-rate fitting; checks H2–H6 |
| `FEAS1-DEV` | `D` | 0–15, plus 16–31 (extension) | Development (selection) |
| `FEAS1-CON` | `V` | 0 … n_V − 1 | Confirmation; generated only after `CONFIRM_PLAN.json` is hashed |

Worlds come from the frozen `create_world(ns, block, index, "R2", "massed")`, seeded `BYTE-CORE-1|<ns>|<block>|<index>`.

**Every intact life has:**
- 3,648 scored consequence events;
- 13 day checkpoints (12 with relevant-pair probes, 320 in total);
- 40 delayed returns of unchanged pairs (erratum §13; fixed by the schedule).

### 4.2 World identity

1. **No seed or world reuse.** Q12 checks that every FEAS-1 world hash differs from every other FEAS-1 world hash and from every BYTE-CORE-1 world hash in its job files.
2. **Keys are unique within a world.** The frozen `audit_world` checks this, and it is extended to every key used in an FK life (§4.3).
3. **Cross-world key coincidences are counted and reported, not failed.** About 1.5 are expected by chance with BYTE-CORE-1 (36⁶ ≈ 2.2 × 10⁹ keys; ~396 per world). Every life starts from re-initialized state, and nothing crosses lives.

### 4.3 Stream conditions

**FK (fresh keys), exactly.**
- The j-th presented record of a life, in life order, uses key k_j instead of its cue's key.
- k_j is drawn from a world-seeded stream (`…|fk|…`) over the key alphabet, rejecting any key already used in that world: original keys, neighbours, or earlier fresh keys. No key therefore recurs within an FK life.
- Everything else is identical to the intact life:
  - event list and times;
  - sides;
  - delivered consequences, hence the consequence-byte sequence;
  - tags;
  - the wrapper index w, computed from the **original** cue's presentation count;
  - XOR masks (applied to k_j);
  - the newline.
- Probes in FK lives also use fresh keys. They are recorded and excluded from every decision quantity.
- Reserved bytes cannot occur in any rendered FK context (amendment A1's argument; checked in Q4).

**UNP.** The frozen `unpaired_world(w)` permutes whole trials' deliveries across trials.

**What each condition keeps and changes:**

| | I | FK | UNP |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cue identities recur | yes | **no** | yes |
| Cue→consequence contingency | yes | none (nothing recurs) | **destroyed** |
| Delivered-outcome sequence (time, side, runs) | — | **identical to I** | **changed** |
| Consequence-byte sequence seen by the front end | — | identical to I | changed |
| Presentation times, schedule, tags | — | identical | identical |
| Wrappers | — | identical | identical |
| Within-trial pairing (one aversive per trial) | yes | identical | kept |
| Key-repetition-dependent front-end state | yes | **changed** (FK's confound) | identical |
| What survives in the condition | — | Anything not needing identity recurrence: outcome timing, cross-record traces, partner-in-trial effects | Anything not needing contingency, with a changed outcome series |

## 5. Learner, adapter, driver

**The frozen learner and adapter, per record:**
1. The front end is fed the context.
2. At the detector moment the code is taken (§3) and encoded live with `encode_sparse`, and v is recorded.
3. Teaching is one 30 s event with punishment = [0xF1].
4. The remaining bytes are fed, then the 135 s gap.

**FEAS driver.**
- It is a copy of the frozen `run_life` that takes a learner factory, an FK key hook and an external recording wrapper. No frozen module is patched.
- **Q1** requires it, with FK off, to reproduce the frozen `run_life` bit for bit for intact and UNP lives.

## 6. Front ends

Every front end:
- receives only (byte, time);
- keeps all state in counted arrays;
- has no learned parameters;
- has a pure `read()`;
- is identical across worlds.

The receptor table **R** (256 × 88) is frozen: 6 channels per byte, amplitudes U[0.4, 1.0], seed `BYTE-CORE-1|RECEPTOR|v1`.

| Arm | Role | Definition | State | Basis (label) |
|---|---|---|---:|---|
| **FE0** | Reference; frozen `bytecore.FE0` | p ← p·e^(−Δt/10 s) + R[b]; `read()` = p/max p | 88 floats | Hallem & Carlson 2006 (R) |
| **FE1** | Candidate; frozen `bytecore.FE1` | Input R[b]⊙(1 − h). Per byte, h ← clip(h + 0.3(1 − h)⊙R[b], 0, 1). h decays with τh = 1,800 s. p as FE0 | 176 floats | Short-term odorant-selective habituation (Das et al. 2011, C+) |
| **FE-NI** | Candidate; new | FE0 integration plus an exposure-driven negative image m (below) | 176 floats | Long-term habituation via glomerulus-selective potentiated inhibition (Das et al. 2011, C+); negative-image proposal (Ramaswami 2014, T) |
| **FE-LAG** | Engineering benchmark; frozen `bytecore.FELAG`. Identical rules; never adopted (C8) | V51 lag code of the last 12 bytes; signed 8-contact adaptor; tanh; rectified; normalized | 12 bytes | Delay lines; not biological |

**FE-NI.**
- State: p (as FE0) and m, with m₀ = 0.
- `advance(t)`: p decays; m never changes, so there is no decay in silence.
- `feed(b, t)`, in order:
  1. `advance(t)`;
  2. p̂ = p / max p (zero if p = 0);
  3. m ← m + (p̂ − m)/1,792;
  4. p ← p + R[b].
- `read()`, pure: recompute p̂ from the current p, compute o = max(p̂ − m, 0), and return o / max o, or zero if max o = 0.
- Empty reads are counted by the external recording wrapper, never inside `read()`.
- N_m = 1,792 = 128 records × 14 bytes.
  - 12 massed records of one pair make up 9.0% of m.
  - Convergence reaches 95% after ≈ 383 records, which is within block 0 (576 records).
- **Expected weakness:** a late separator is under-cancelled (it is present in 2 of 14 samples).
- **Contrast with FE1, tested through R:** FE1's habituation recovers during rests; m does not.

**Fixtures (never candidates):**

| Fixture | Definition | Used in |
|---|---|---|
| **XR** | FE0 plus q, which decays with τ = 600 s. When a reserved byte arrives, q += R[b] after the read. `read()` = normalize(p + q), pure. It carries earlier consequences into later reads (weight ≈ 0.76 for the previous one) and deliberately uses the reserved-byte class. Its advantage cannot require identity recurrence. | H5 (CAL) |
| **FE-TAIL4** | Frozen oracle: FE0 fed only key bytes 2–5. Its advantage over FE0 (+0.159 AUC in CON, erratum PH-5) comes from how it codes each key, which is useless when no key recurs. | H6 (CAL) |
| **NI-READLOCKED** | v2's FE-NI, which updated m inside `read()` | Q3 (must fail) |

**Not candidates, and not dismissed:** FE1-NI, which inherits FE1's retention cost (erratum §13); the read-locked and output-driven negative images; a sparser receptor table; slot-geometry kernels; FE-SURP.

## 7. Declared readout

1. **Score:** s = −v.
2. **Model:** p(aversive) = σ(a·s + c).
3. **Fit data:** all scored events of the arm's 8 intact CAL lives, pooled. Every life has 3,648 events, so worlds are weighted equally.
4. **Fit:**
   - maximize the mean Bernoulli log-likelihood against the delivered byte;
   - the problem is convex, so it is solved by Newton's method (IRLS) from (0, 0) until max |gradient| < 10⁻¹⁰;
   - probabilities are clipped to [10⁻¹², 1 − 10⁻¹²] inside the objective only;
   - one function serves every arm.
5. **Freeze:** the fitted values go to `READOUT.json`, which is hashed before any DEV world is generated.
6. **Inheritance:** FK and UNP arms use their intact parent's (a, c). Their η is reported, never used to decide anything: under those streams the readout is mismatched.
7. **Use:** every η uses the frozen (a, c) with clip ε = 10⁻³, with no recalibration anywhere.
8. **No online correction.**

The CODE-NLMS learning rate (reference only) is chosen in the same phase over {0.1, 0.3, 1, 2}.

## 8. Outcomes

### 8.1 η (BYTE-CORE-1 §5.2)

- Per scored item: p̃(y) = clip(p if aversive, else 1 − p; ε, 1 − ε). Bits saved s = log₂ p̃(y) + 1; relevant information i = 1.
- η = Σs / Σi, per world.
- In-stream truth is the **delivered** byte, anomalies included.
- A Δη of 0.02 is 2 bits per 100 bits of relevant consequence information. It is not 2 percentage points of accuracy.

### 8.2 Decision quantities (per world, paired vs FE0 on identical worlds)

AUC is computed over all in-stream scored events of the life (3,648).

| ID | Definition | Currency |
|---|---|---|
| **A1** | η(c, I) − η(FE0, I) | η (the owner's metric) |
| **C_id** | [AUC(c, I) − AUC(FE0, I)] − [AUC(c, FK) − AUC(FE0, FK)] | AUC |
| **N** | AUC(c, FK) − AUC(FE0, FK) | AUC |
| **B** | C_id − N | AUC |
| **R** | η(c, I) − η(FE0, I) over the 40 delayed returns of unchanged pairs per life (tags `stable`, `react`; ≥ 24 h since the previous presentation) | η |
| **S_unp** (sensitivity, not a gate) | [AUC(c, I) − AUC(FE0, I)] − [AUC(c, UNP) − AUC(FE0, UNP)] | AUC |

- **Why AUC for C_id, N, B and S_unp:** FK and UNP lives run under readouts fitted on intact lives, so their η mixes learning with readout mismatch (erratum §2 caveat). AUC is readout-free.
- **Why this R** (erratum §13):
  - It is the owner's in-stream η restricted to delayed demand for unchanged pairs.
  - It excludes revisions, anomalies and misleading reminders, which measure flexibility; those remain in A1.
  - The earlier 24-h day-probe measure showed FE1 gaining while delayed returns showed it losing.
  - Tags are used by the evaluator only.

### 8.3 Reported, with no decision role

| ID | Quantity |
|---|---|
| R1 | Intact in-stream AUC; native paired accuracy |
| R2 | Retention profile: η and AUC by time since the previous presentation (< 1 h, 1–24 h, 24 h–7 d, ≥ 7 d) and by tag. 24-h day-probe η and AUC. Final 7-day probe η and AUC |
| R3 | For W1–W13 and each day checkpoint: η, observed aversive frequency, unconditional mean p, AUC, n |
| R4 | Offset share (learned minus no-learning copy below 0), per window |

| R9 | Resources |

## 9. Decision rules

**Statistics.**
- The unit is the world, and arms are paired on identical worlds.
- Every quantity is summarized by the mean and SD of per-world differences, with a 95% two-sided t interval (df = n − 1).
- "Lower" and "upper" mean the interval bounds.
- Development is selection, so there is no multiplicity adjustment. Confirmation tests one frozen candidate.
- **Every classification below is a decision tree: the first matching branch wins, so classes are exclusive and exhaustive.**

### 9.1 Development (n = 16), for each candidate c ∈ {FE1, FE-NI}

| Step | Condition | Status |
|---|---|---|
| D1 | A1 upper < 0.02, or C_id upper < 0 | **FUTILE** |
| D2 | A1 mean ≥ 0.02 and A1 lower > 0 and C_id lower > 0, and R upper < 0 | **TRADE_OFF_IN_DEVELOPMENT** (to the owner; not confirmed automatically) |
| D3 | A1 mean ≥ 0.02 and A1 lower > 0 and C_id lower > 0, and R mean ≥ 0 and B mean > 0 | **SELECTABLE** |
| D4 | Otherwise | **INCONCLUSIVE** |

FE-LAG is classified by the same tree and reported as an engineering benchmark; it never enters selection.

### 9.2 Extension (still development)

- **Runs only if** no candidate is SELECTABLE at n = 16 and at least one is INCONCLUSIVE.
- **Covers** FE0 and every INCONCLUSIVE candidate, under I, FK and UNP, on DEV 16–31.
- **Afterwards,** the tree is re-applied at n = 32; anything still INCONCLUSIVE becomes **NOT_SELECTED**.
- There is no second extension.

### 9.3 Selection

- Among SELECTABLE candidates, choose the one with the **largest A1 lower bound**. A tie goes to the larger C_id lower bound, then to the smaller state.
- **Reported as:** "usable gain supported (estimate meets the screening line 0.02); identity-dependent gain supported under the fresh-key control; retention estimate not negative". It is never reported as an established gain of any size.
- `SELECTION.json` is hashed.
- If nothing is selected, the result is `NO_CANDIDATE_SELECTED` (§9.6).

### 9.4 Confirmation plan (fixed before worlds exist)

**Sample size.**
- n_V is the smallest n ≥ 32 giving ≥ 95% power for each of A1, C_id and B to have a lower bound > 0, capped at 64.
- The formula is n ≥ ((t₀.₉₇₅,ₙ₋₁ + t₀.₉₅,ₙ₋₁)·SD/Δ_plan)², iterated in n.
- SD is the development per-world SD. Δ_plan is half the development mean.
- By the union bound, joint power is ≥ 0.85.
- If the required n exceeds 64, the result is `NOT_CONFIRMABLE_THIS_ROUND` and nothing runs.
- **Worked example (A1):** SD 0.0143 and Δ_plan 0.0127 give n ≈ 18, so n_V = 32.

**Reachability of `IMPROVES`.**
- Compute the power, at n_V, for R's lower bound to exceed 0. The planning value is half the development mean of R, or 0 if that mean is ≤ 0.
- **If the power is ≥ 0.80,** confirmation runs.
- **Otherwise** `CONFIRM_PLAN.json` is written with status `AWAITING_OWNER` and the runner stops. Confirmation could then settle the gain and bound R, but spending it is the owner's decision.
- For example, with SD_R = 0.05 and n_V = 64, the development mean of R must be ≳ 0.036.

**Plan record.** `CONFIRM_PLAN.json` holds the candidate, n_V, the power for each quantity, the readout hash and the six arms (candidate and FE0 under I, FK and UNP). It is hashed before any CON world is generated.

### 9.5 Confirmation classes (95% CIs; one candidate)

| Step | Condition | Class | Consequence |
|---|---|---|---|
| C1 | A1 lower ≤ 0 | **`NOT_CONFIRMED`** | Not adopted |
| C2 | C_id lower ≤ 0 | **`GAIN_NOT_IDENTITY_DEPENDENT`** | Not adopted |
| C3 | R upper < 0 | **`TRADE_OFF`** | Owner decision; not a default |
| C4 | B lower ≤ 0 | **`GAIN_IDENTITY_NOT_DOMINANT`** | Owner decision; not a default |
| C5 | R lower ≤ 0 | **`GAIN_RETENTION_UNRESOLVED`** | Owner decision. The report states that the data allow a retention change anywhere from R lower to R upper |
| C6 | Otherwise | **`IMPROVES`** | The default changes automatically. The S2 bridge must still be passed |

**Pre-registered sensitivity wording (from S_unp).**
- If S_unp lower > 0: "identity dependence supported under both the fresh-key and the unpaired control".
- Otherwise: "identity dependence supported under the fresh-key control; the unpaired control, which also changes the outcome sequence, did not confirm it".

S_unp never changes the class.

**Also always reported:** each estimate against the screening line; A1's lower bound; N with its CI. There is no loss margin in any class.

### 9.6 Round-level readings

- **`NO_CANDIDATE_SELECTED`** means no biologically motivated candidate in this set met §9.1.
  - If FE-LAG **would have been** SELECTABLE, the report says a deployable byte/time front end can meet these rules and that the biologically motivated candidates fall short. Promoting an engineering front end is the owner's decision (C8).
  - If FE-LAG also fails, no tested deployable front end meets the rules.
- **`TRADE_OFF_IN_DEVELOPMENT`** and **`AWAITING_OWNER`** go to the owner with their evidence.

## 14. Limits of any result

- **Complete strategies.** No locus (front end vs learner) is claimed (§1).
- **Own-cue vs partner-cue learning is not separated.**
- **FK changes repetition-dependent front-end state.** UNP has a different confound and is reported as sensitivity; neither control alone isolates identity learning perfectly.
- **R is imprecise** (40 events per life), and automatic adoption is correspondingly hard to reach. This is stated in advance.
- **Scope:** R2/F3, frozen FULL_151, this world family, biologically motivated candidates.
- **Not S2 readiness.**
