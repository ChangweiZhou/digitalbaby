# Four-visible-byte E3 assay qualification

**Status before execution: frozen only after source lock.** This is one bounded assay-qualification run. It tests whether the known FE0→native Full151→emitted two-option choice signal survives a cue wrapper that also activates the fixed four-visible-byte CONTENT encoder. It does not test a new core mechanism, combine two inputs in one learner, or promote the content bridge to E3.

## Task and exposure

Use V9.8's `make_e3(world)` generator unchanged for symbols, hidden P/N classes, training/withheld edges, labels, 144 old records, 72 disjoint-new-symbol records, 12 old exposures per ordered cue, and scheduling. Transform **every** 12-byte cue from `9 spaces + XY + space` to `## + 7 spaces + XY + space`, preserving the positions of X and Y. `#` is constant, carries no world or label information and is never used as a symbol. At choice/teaching time the CONTENT encoder's last four nonspace bytes are `##XY`; it may use its inherited FE0 fallback before that point, but it must not fall back at the native write or scored value. The transformed six held-out ordered pairs are absent from all teaching. Do not modify this wrapper after learning outcomes are seen.

Six of nine old P–N edges are taught in both orientations; three edges are held out in both orientations. The stream then advances a simulated 24 h, teaches a disjoint new-symbol cohort and advances another simulated 24 h. No held-out choice receives feedback. An exact or canonicalized pair-key table cannot choose withheld orientations above the order-balanced 50% baseline; a one-bit class rule learned from old teaching can solve this narrow E3 family.

## Architecture and arms

Within each world, FE0-W, FE0-N, CONTENT-W and CONTENT-N begin from the same V9.2 checkpoint and see identical bytes, outcome `!`, labels, clocks and supplied alternatives. Each has one native Full151 valence store. W permits native old writes, N has old native writes off; both have the same new writes on. The engineered predictor updates identically. A fixed, label-blind organ receives `12 cue bytes | 12 cue bytes ?`, retains option 1's native value and emits `L` or `R` after option 2; the scoring program reads only that emitted byte. Both option orders are probed on discarded copies. The organ and independent reference must use the **same input route as the trained store**. No route is available only to a future mechanism candidate.

## Freeze, qualification and inference

Source lock seals this design, runner, adapter, independent auditor, frozen parent sources/checkpoint, route names, byte wrapper, output rule, rosters, intervals, gates and resource caps. Technical world **30300** runs the full chronology but is excluded from inference and may not set thresholds. It checks exact exposure and four-byte keys; route-preserving clone/state; equal W/N sensory/predictor/clock paths; write counts; output-before-feedback; unmutated probes; and matched-time option values against an independent same-route reference. Tampered teacher/route/write/probe receipts must be rejected. No old trajectory is rerun.

Pilot worlds **30301–30316** probe **only taught** old/new pairs. For *both* input routes, require W old-trained choice ≥80% at old end, W final old-trained ≥70%, final trained W−N ≥10 percentage points, and W and N new-trained choice ≥65% at new-cohort end. The pilot is excluded from confirmation and may not compute held-out outputs. A failing gate stops the program; the wrapper, model and thresholds are not retuned.

Confirmation worlds **30401–30464** are fresh and fixed in advance. Recheck the same trained gates. The **sole confirmatory primary** is the 64-world paired FE0 final held-out emitted-choice W−N effect; require its two-sided 95% Student-t interval lower bound >0 (nonzero world variance) and absolute FE0 W final held-out choice ≥60%. The scientific unit is a world, not the six ordered choices within it. CONTENT's held-out W, N, W−N and interval are prespecified **descriptive** diagnostics; no requirement that its W or N accuracy be exactly 50% and no inference of zero from a nonsignificant interval. Save old-end held-out results to distinguish immediate absence from later loss. Every new-cohort taught score and both correct-side scores are reported.

If FE0 fails on the newly wrapped fixture, verdict is `WRAPPED_E3_ASSAY_NOT_QUALIFIED`; this does not overturn the original Step 10 result or imply Full151 has no generalization. If FE0 passes with guards, verdict is `WRAPPED_E3_ASSAY_QUALIFIED`. CONTENT's result cannot establish a two-route integrated system. No optional world extension, replacement, wrapper search, or completed-trajectory rerun is permitted. An execution/scientific error writes `FAILURE.json` and stops without automatic restart.

## Why this matters for the next core

The completed CONTENT four-output 16-key and 32-key persistence runs remain the separately validated E1 safety benchmark. This run supplies a modest E3 benchmark accessible on a four-visible-byte byte format. Future candidate core mechanisms may change Full151 internals, but in each benchmark the candidate and unmodified baseline must receive the same input route(s), output rule, teaching budget, timing and worlds. A candidate's E3 gain cannot be credited to a private second channel. Separate benchmark wins cannot be called one integrated model until a later same-architecture test passes. Teacher-free learning, arbitrary output and arithmetic understanding are not claims of this qualification.

## Resources and integrity

The predeclared confirmation cap is four wall hours and the persistent results cap is 100 MB. A full technical life measures wall time, RSS and receipt size; if projected confirmation exceeds a cap, stop before pilot. All per-world receipts are atomic and never overwritten. An independent auditor regenerates the source fixture and wrapper, validates every event/probe roster and value-to-output computation, and reduces world-level effects without replaying the learner. Run the active runner under `caffeinate`; leave it attached until normal exit or terminal failure.
