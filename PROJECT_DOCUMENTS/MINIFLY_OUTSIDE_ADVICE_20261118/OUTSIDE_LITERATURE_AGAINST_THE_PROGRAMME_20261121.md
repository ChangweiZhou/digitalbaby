**How stored copies should be combined, and what guarantee covers it.** The report: "the stored copies of the mixer weights should be combined as a linear mixture, updated by Bayes' rule with fixed share to the uniform past or by mixing past posteriors (MPP)" (Executive summary). It leaves one point open: "One point remains to be confirmed in the code: that the copies' predictions are mixed in probability space and not in logit space." (Addendum, item 2). I read the code: they are mixed as probabilities (`kern_books.c`, line 159 as committed: each share becomes (1 − s) × share + s / sets; the answer is the sum of share × probability; the shares are then multiplied by the probabilities and normalised). So plan 3 is already the linear pool. What it runs is fixed share to the uniform vector over the sets present, with s = 1/65,536, a growing pool, and the living set as one member. Mixing past posteriors was not run. The guarantee for the rule as run: "Fixed share to the start vector pays about ln n + ln(1/α) nats at every switch, whether or not the regime is new" (Prompt 1, Q1), against the copies as they behaved. With our numbers (mine: 121 sets, α = 1/65,536) that is 15.9 nats a switch, or 0.00014 nats a byte on a test of 111,540 bytes. The mixture still stands 0.03 to 0.10 above the own stage there (`PLAN_4.md`, section 1). By the bounds the two rules differ only in the nats paid at a switch. The report says as much of a smaller pool: "the choice of copies matters more than the rule (inference)" (Prompt 1, Q1). Two consequences (mine). First, on any stretch of L bytes the mixture cannot be worse than the living set, or than any one bookmark, by more than ln(n/α) + L·ln(1/(1 − α)) nats: about 18 nats on that test. Predictions B1 of plan 3 and V3 of plan 10 (0.002 nats a byte) therefore test the program, not the idea, and should not be counted as findings. The result files on disk agree with the bound: in 408 pairs of plain answer and mixture (ten result files of plan 3 and one of plan 8, as they stood; read for this check only), the mixture is never above the plain answer by more than 0.00009 nats a byte. Second, the remainder is not in the rule; it is in copies that do not learn (plan 8) and in the counts.

**Copies of the low-order counts with each bookmark, against counts in two memories.** The report asks for the first: "the order 0 to 2 counts should be snapshotted with each copy, because the bounds compare only against the experts as they actually behaved, and the answer of forget-me-not (FMN) to drift in shared state is to copy whole model states" (Executive summary). Plan 5 did the second. They differ in what they can give back. A copy restores at once what A's short contexts held. A bounded memory must meet each context again a few times, and the lifetime memory beside it stays the pool of all kinds. The report does not agree with itself here: its conclusion is to "keep short-order statistics shared but bounded, rather than partitioned" (Conclusion), which is plan 5's form and not the copy. Our figures do not decide it. Plan 5 measured the loss on streams read late (cut by 0.66) and the cost of a life (22 to 41% lower). It did not measure how much of the loss on return still sits in the counts once they have two memories. That one figure decides whether copies of counts are worth a new kernel (experiment 3). They are not cheap: a bookmark with counts of its own no longer hears the living set's messages, and hearing the same messages is what lets `books.py` answer for 120 sets from one pass over the cells.

**Why the detector over-split.** The report: "The programme's detector lacks both of the remedies that this literature uses against over-splitting: a persistence prior on regime identity, and a merge step." (Prompt 6, Q1). The right test "charges a fresh model its own adaptive code from empty plus the prior cost of a switch, and it scores the block against the Bayesian *mixture* of existing regimes, not against each regime alone" (Q2). Set against the code of plan 2 (`kern_det.c`): the fresh cell was already charged its own adaptive code from empty (line 30). The belief already has a share for switching (s = 1/W, line 24), which is a kind of persistence. What is absent: any cost of a birth; the mixture as the thing to beat (a birth needs only to beat the best single cell, line 32); any merging; and anything shared between regimes. The report's own sentence then applies: "if it used a sequential code and still won, the sub-regimes of source code are real at order 0" (Q2). So its first experiment under prompt 6 is in part what was run. A cost of birth of the size it names (about 2(⌈log n⌉+1) bits a segment, some 33 nats here) is small beside the thousand nats or so that a fresh cell pays to learn a block of 4,096 bytes (mine, by the report's own formula for that cost). I would not rebuild the detector. What the report offers in its place is selection without births (experiment 6), and what plans 3 and 8 already are: "The soft forms, with shared statistics, are the ones that did not hurt on small data." (Prompt 6, Q5).

**1. A source whose rules change and return, with an exact reference (c; also b and e).** *The report's rule* (Prompt 16, Experiments for one day, third row): "Random order-2 Markov chains over 16 symbols, switching every 2,000 bytes without labels; compare CM with an oracle Dirichlet-1/2 model reset at each switch". *Counts against the system:* "CM's excess over the oracle is more than 0.05 nats/byte in the second 1,000 bytes of each segment, and does not shrink". *Where it fits:* a builder like `kinds6.py`; then singles of `mem.py`, `cellmix.py` and `awake.py` (the plain system and those of plans 5, 7 and 8), which return the loss byte by byte. *Mine:* a second source in which the chains are drawn from a pool of four and come back, with a second reference that keeps one table for each chain and is told which is present; that gives the ceiling for (b). How the chains are drawn is not given and must be fixed in the plan. *Why first:* nothing tonight measures revising, and here the truth is known. The bounded kinds alone cannot follow at this pace at order 2 (a context is met some eight times a segment and halves only at 32); whether the senders of the last byte counted carry it is what the run would show. *Cost:* streams of 1.5 million bytes; some ten singles of 10 to 60 CPU-seconds. It measures a changed count, not a revised fact.

**2. Probes inside a stage (a, b; a measurement).** *The report's rule:* the three quantities of Prompt 18, Q6, quoted in section 1; and (Prompt 18, third row) "A six-stream life with frozen probes of earlier streams scored every 64 KB". *Counts against the system:* "A post-switch rise larger than the end-of-life forgetting (a stability gap), or no recovery on returns". *Where it fits:* `lives.py`, the lives of plan 1. The tests are already answered in forked copies after each body; answer them also after every 65,536 bytes, with nothing changing, over the first 16,000 bytes of each test. *What it decides:* whether A is lost within the first blocks of B (then the change itself is the place to act) or steadily (then only separate sets can hold it); and whether one bookmark every 65,536 bytes is the right pace, which plan 3 chose between two values. *Cost:* about 120 probe points in a life; 11.5 million bytes answered without learning; some 3 CPU-minutes more for the small system, and 720 forked copies.

**6. **8. ## 7. Questions only the owner can settle, which the report sharpens

1. **Recall by exact match, and the rule against attention.** The project's rule excludes "an attention-equivalent content-addressed read" (the research-loop skill, section B). The report's authors argue: "an exact match model equals a degenerate hard attention whose score is 1 if and only if the last k bytes are equal, but so does every hashed context table. The ruling therefore hinges on whether the rule bans learned soft similarity, or any key-based lookup." (Prompt 12, Q4). The recalling senders are in every system of tonight's line and gained 0.009 to 0.124 nats (roadmap, section 19). Which of the two does the rule ban?
2. **Is a trust "local"?** One trust is shared by every cell of a kind and level. The room for the unmet is shared in the same way, and a map from a cell's state to a probability would be (experiment 7). The report classes such things together, "Pooled across contexts: SEE, PAQ StateMaps, APMs, and the programme's learned escape" (Prompt 9, Q7), and admits them because they "each learn from their own error" (Prompt 3, Q1). The constraint as our prompts state it: "Each unit learns only from what reaches it." Does a number shared by all cells of a kind count as one unit's own?
3. **Stored copies and the size of the learner.** The report's way to (a) and (b) is "more weight vectors", and for the counts to "copy whole model states". A bookmark is 26,792 numbers; a life of 8 million bytes takes up to 120 of them (`PLAN_3.md`); each answers every byte. The project counts clones and checkpoints in a learner's size. What bound on stored copies, and on the history kept for recall, should a learner meant to live long be held to?
4. **What counts as showing (c) and (d).** The report's tests are all scored from probabilities, and one objective it cites for continual learners "is simply average log loss under a compute constraint" (Prompt 5, Strongest counter-argument). The project's contract asks for the system's own complete answer with its own end mark. Are experiments 1 and 4 acceptable as measurements of (c) and (d) at development level, or only as diagnostics beside tests of the project's own kind?
5. **Where (d) is to be sought, and at what length of life.** The report finds no way inside tables of counts to answer what was never met, beyond smoothing; and its record has programs of counts alone behind those with a part taught by backpropagation at 100 MB and at 1 GB. The programme's strongest result on (d) came from the relay system teaching the counting system (98.8%; sums; one line). After the steer away from addition, is that pairing to be carried to general streams now, or is (d) to wait until (a), (b), (c) and (e) hold? And is a learner of this family meant for lives of that length?
