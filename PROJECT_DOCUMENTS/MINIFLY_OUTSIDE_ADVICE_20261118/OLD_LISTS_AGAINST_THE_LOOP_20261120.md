## 5. Five items that bear on what is done next, ranked

Each is an entry for the audit of a design (section 2). The ranking is mine.

1. **Holding cells that read no other holding cell (track M).** *The lists:* the scan (section 1) and the audit memo (section 2.2) both point to the earlier learner's cell, `MOD97_LOCAL_RULE_20261106/exp11_cell.py` (same ref): "512 units, each with its own gate; no unit reads another's state", so that the trace at each connection is exact. Review section 9.23: with it that learner came to "hold an operand across twelve distractor bytes" (add mod 97 at 12 letters, three seeds, from the birth that had never needed to hold: 90 / 78 / 63 with the trace against 21 / 32 / 5 without credit through time). *Why it bears:* in R §12 every holding cell reads 16 other holding cells one byte late, so a cell's own trace is not the whole of its credit and the rest has to cross between cells; that crossing is what R §12.4 leaves open, and without it the gap texts stand at 28% and 45%. *Smallest experiment:* holding cells whose gate and candidate read the lines only; ordinary cells that read them at the same byte; the mismatch relayed over two steps as in R §10; no crossing. Texts `m1` and `g1` beside R §12.2, then `g2` beside 8%, at equal numbers of strengths. *Against it:* the arm stays near 28% and 45% at equal lines, or holds one digit and leaves `g2` where it is. *To be carried with it:* that learner was born from development by back-propagation through time, used the sign rule, batches and replay of stored episodes, and its gates read a ten-byte window through a learned layer, not the bare lines; and review 9.23 says of it "adding the cell made forgetting far worse", which bears on keeping with holding cells (NOT_RUN in R §16). The two remedies now running for the same problem are different ones: longer runs, and a signal passed over two connections.
2. **Rehearsal from a store of lines as they arrived (track K).** *The lists:* S1 and S9 (a fast store, replayed); the scan, section 1, on the earlier learner: "final held-composition accuracy for self-generated targets was 32.08 / 24.88 / 31.58% across seeds 73129/93683/116089, versus 62.46 / 57.00 / 65.42% with stored observed targets"; the audit memo's first rank. *Why it bears:* both forms of rehearsal made by the system that failed in R §9 were lines the system generated. *Smallest experiment:* with the 128 new cells, the old line in 32 drawn from N lines the system kept as they arrived (N of 32, 256, 2,048), beside the program's fresh lines (67.0%); never-shown sums scored; the store's bytes counted. *Against it:* addition stays near 4.4% at every N, or sums in the store are kept and never-shown ones are not. *The lists disagree here:* the 50plus summary has "replay archives/reservoirs as the default solution" under "DEPRIORITIZED engineering families", the screen of 7 October stops the replay add-ons as they stood (its row is in Chinese; the paraphrase is mine), the survey grades replay "High", and the scan reports the measurement quoted.
3. **Before another mechanism for keeping: how much of the first task is still in the cells in between (track K).** *The lists:* F10, quoted in its row; the scan's reserve "Silent memory and false reinstatement", with "Misleading reminders create false retrievals"; the 50plus summary's condition for opening latent memory: "dormant return is poor while a few renewed exposures rapidly restore performance, suggesting hidden information rather than complete deletion". *Why it bears:* R §11 has addition at 1.8% after subtraction, back at 70% after 4,000 to 6,000 lines against 60,000 to 70,000 at first, and at 44.6% when the answering cell's old strengths alone are put back. In my reading the summary's condition is met. *Smallest experiment:* after subtraction, addition taught again with the cells in between held still and the answering cell alone learning: lines to 70% and the level reached, beside R §11; subtraction scored afterwards. *Against the reading:* the answering cell alone does not bring addition much beyond 44.6%; then what was lost is lost in the cells in between and K1 is aimed at the right place.
4. **Score what a way of keeping costs in reuse (track K; a check on K1, not a mechanism).** *The lists:* the 50plus summary on stores divided by an address: "HASH8 strong trained-pair memory but poor V9.8 transfer" and "It appears to trade away relational geometry"; the audit memo's falsifier for opposing traces: "No improvement over matched two-store, or selector needs future task/phase truth." *Why it bears:* in R §11 writing subtraction into new cells leaves it at 75.4% against 85.8% without them. *Smallest experiment:* in each arm of K1, subtraction from birth beside subtraction after addition, lines to 70% and the level at the end. *Against the concern:* the arms that keep addition learn subtraction as fast and as far as the plain system does after addition.
5. **A gate on what a holding cell gives out (track M).** *The lists:* S2, "selective output gating"; review section 9.17 on an earlier learner: "Separate write and read gates | 87–98% vs 57–82% tied, 20–58% always open | The PBWM gating structure helps hold a cue across distractors; capacity is confounded, as Codex notes"; `exp11_cell.py` carries such an option. *Why it bears:* the loop's cell has one gate and is read by every reader at every byte, the letters included, and two digits across letters stand at 8%. *Smallest experiment:* the cell of R §12 with a second gate on what it gives out, on `g2`, at equal numbers of strengths. *Against it:* no gain over five seeds, or a gain that goes when the one-gate arm is given the same numbers. The survey's rule for the gate, reward alone, is not what is proposed: see section 4, (b).

## 6. What the lists do not help with

- **Language at the level of a small GPT.** No list has a text of language, a loss in nats, or anything on scale in cells and readings. The survey's tests are sized for "a 60,000-parameter network". One sentence of the scan bears on line L1 and no more: "use a predictive probability distribution and an appropriate categorical loss".
- **How a connection's strength is decided.** RM section 1 records Mr. Xu's words: "The connection strength (ideally can involve many cells) should be jointly decided by the cells together." No list has a strength held in parts by both ends, a right of either end to withdraw, or a connection of more than two cells. The nearest are the catalogue's note on sign (`w=s*a`, a fixed sign and a strength not below zero), which is the one-sign arm of R §10 (70.7% against 84.2%), and the 50plus summary's remark that "true event-level structural rewiring is largely untested", which the loop has since measured in one form (R §10: 42.7% with 32 lines, 80.8% with 64).
- **Where a new task is written, settled by the cells.** The lists name the problem (the 50plus summary: "M9 should regulate how much plasticity is available, while M1 decides where it goes") and give no rule that does it without a program. The two fly records on retention are, in the review's words, "marked partial on local sufficiency and neither is a replacement for replay".
- **The loop's main result.** Nothing in the lists predicts that the order of the digits, cells that add and place value form by learning (R §5). The catalogue's fixed computations would lay such things down, and the catalogue says so itself (F16).
- **Speed and cost.** Nothing beyond the catalogue's rule that every store and signal be counted.

## 7. Limits

1. The mapping is a reading, by the outside session's helper. Another reader would place some rows otherwise, most of all S4 and S8 on the arm `fixed`, F01 on line L1, F08 on rule Q, F11 on the new cells, and H4 on K1; the counts of section 4 move with those choices.
2. A resemblance to a circuit is not evidence that a mechanism will help. The catalogue says as much of its own records: "No source examined establishes that any of these modules, alone, yields systematic arithmetic-rule generalization from raw bytes under the project’s local lifetime-learning restriction."
3. Nothing here was run. Every figure of the loop is the report's and is development level, repeated by no one. Every figure of the earlier learners is quoted from the review or the scan and belongs to other learners, texts and rules; none is carried over to the loop.
4. The sources behind the lists were not checked by me. The survey's list of works cited includes an unrelated page ("Google Sports Data"), and the survey and the catalogue give different journals for Felsenberg et al. (2018); I did not check which is right.
5. The older lists were skimmed, not read; the 70 rows of the old ledger were not set against the loop one by one.
6. Section 2's rule stands over section 5: these are entries for an audit of designs, and the lists themselves authorise no experiment.
