# Errata to the report of the small case, found on 20 November 2026

These are faults of mine in `loop_small_case`, found after the report's sections were written. Each is stated with what it touches and what it does not. The report's text is left as it was written; this file and the plans named here carry the corrections.

## 1. The score of subtraction is mostly not a score of lines never shown

Found by an independent helper on 20 November while it checked the keeping line's files; confirmed by me from `cells2.py`.

`cells2.py` holds out 500 ordered pairs (a, b) for each operator and shows 8,000 others. For subtraction, `text()` writes the larger number first whatever the order of the pair. A held-out pair (a, b) and the shown pair (b, a) therefore write the same line, and about four held-out subtraction lines in five are lines that training also writes: 399, 404 and 420 of 500 on the seeds 73129, 93683 and 116089 (recomputed from the program's own split). Addition is not touched: an addition line is written in the order of its pair, and no held-out addition line is written in training.

*What this touches.* Every figure for subtraction that this report and the lines built on `cells2.py` call "never-shown": sections 11 and 16 of `REPORT.md` and `PLAN_5.md`, `PLAN_13.md` here; the subtraction columns of the lines `nano/keep`, `nano/replay`, `nano/sparse` and `nano/joint` where they use this scoring. Such a figure is a score on a mixture of about four fifths shown lines and one fifth never-shown ones. For a system that answers shown and never-shown lines alike it changes little; for a system with a part that holds shown lines exactly (cells that count) it can flatter.

*What this does not touch.* Every figure for addition, and with it every statement about how much of addition is kept through subtraction, which is what the keeping track's criterion is stated in.

*How large it is, where it has been measured* (added later on 20 November, as the lines report). The line `nano/replay` redid all 70 of its runs with a clean score beside the old one (the never-shown pairs whose line no shown pair writes): for the relay system, with and without replay, the old score is above the clean one by 0.4 to 0.5 points at the median of fifteen seeds (single runs from 4.0 below to 6.0 above), so for that system it overstated nothing that can be measured, and every reading of that line stands on the clean score. The line `nano/keep`, first file back: its final candidate has 85.1 on the 101 clean pairs against 86.8 on all 500. The line `nano/keep` then redid all 110 of its runs: the fault moved no arm by more than about five points and no order between arms (plain 83.7 and 82.3 on the clean score against 86.0 and 85.8; the program's choice 68.8 and 74.7 against 74.0 and 75.4). For cells that count (`nano/sparse`) the first figure is 62.5 clean beside 63.8 with at most three delays; with four delays it is not yet reported.

*What is owed.* A score of subtraction on the held-out pairs whose written line no shown pair writes (about 80 to 100 of the 500), beside the present one, in any further run; asked of the lines on 20 November.

## 3. The never-shown sums were scored in a situation the systems never met while reading: an empty past

Found by me late on 20 November, while I looked at how sure the answering cell of the counting system is where it errs (`nano/table/sure.py`): lines met for the first time during the reading were answered better than the never-shown lines at the end of it, though both are lines the system had not met.

`cells2.py`'s score gives each held-out line alone: the delay lines that reach back before the start of the line hold line ends. A system that reads a stream of lines is never in that situation after its first line: the bytes before a line are always the end of the line before it. The score therefore asked two things at once, a sum never shown and a past never met. Every line built on `cells2.py` took over the form of the score.

*Measured* (`past.py` and `past_life.py` here, `nano/table/past_cells.py`; results in the `res` folders as `past_*.json`, the printed lines in `res/past_output.txt` and `nano/table/res/past_cells_output.txt`). No system is changed and nothing is learned differently; the held-out pairs, the seeds and the systems are the reported ones. "After a read line": the bytes before each held-out line are those of a line drawn by chance from the lines the system has read (mean of five draws; the draws differ by under one point). Never-shown additions, whole answer, %:

| System | Lines read | Empty past (as scored so far), five reported seeds | After a read line, the same seeds |
|---|---|---|---|
| the relay system of `cells2.py`, plain | 40,000 | 68.8, 64.2, 66.2, 67.2, 62.8; median **66.2** | 82.9, 82.3, 81.2, 86.5, 83.2; median **82.9** |
| the same | 400,000 | 81.8, 81.8, 86.4, 84.8, 87.6; median **84.8** | 95.0, 95.0, 97.0, 95.5, 96.1; median **95.5** |

(The counting system's figures are last readings; that line reports the mean of the last three, 77.5 and 91.8.) By place, the relay system at 400,000 lines after a read line: hundreds 98.2 to 98.9, tens 96.7 to 98.1, units 100.

The life of two tasks (the relay system, addition 400,000 lines, then subtraction 200,000 lines; seeds 73129, 93683, 116089): never-shown additions after subtraction 2.0, 1.6, 1.8 with an empty past and 2.0, 1.8, 1.8 after a read line. Subtraction at the end of the life, on the clean score of erratum 1: 79.2, 85.4, 90.0 with an empty past and 91.1, 90.4, 92.8 after a read line.

*Why* (my account, from the programs; not tested further). In the relay system the strengths of a cell in between for "a line end at a delay that reaches before the line" were set by chance at birth and have been used once, at the first line of the life; they do not fade. At the scoring each cell receives one to three such strengths in place of the ones it has lived with. In the counting system the kinds whose delays reach before the line are silent at the scoring, since they never met line ends there, and the trusts were learned with them speaking.

*What this touches.* Every figure for never-shown sums in `REPORT.md` and the plans here, and in every line that scores as `cells2.py` does (`nano/keep`, `nano/keep2`, `nano/replay`, `nano/joint`, `nano/multi`, `nano/kinds`, `nano/sparse`, `nano/ahead2`, `nano/life3`, `nano/merge`, `nano/search`, `nano/unite`; and, not yet measured, the texts with letters between the numbers of `hold.py`, `nano/cross2`, `nano/layered`, `nano/skip`). For the relay system the old score is 11 to 16 points too low at the two lengths measured; for the counting systems 1 to 3 points; for class kinds alone nothing. So:

1. **The relay system answers 95 to 97% of never-shown two-digit additions after 400,000 lines, not 82 to 88%.** The report's "84%" and the roadmap's "84 to 88%" understate it.
2. **Comparisons between the relay system and the counting systems made at the empty past are wrong in size, and one is wrong in direction.** At 40,000 lines the tables so far set the relay system's 66 against 77.5 for the counting cells without classes; after a read line it is 82.9 against 80.1. Classes (92.9) still stand ten points above the relay system at 40,000 lines; at 400,000 lines the relay system (95.5) stands above every counting system measured there (62 without classes and 79 to 81 with them at the empty past, on two seeds, by that line's report).
3. **Any test in which the relay system was joined to other cells, or kept by other cells, was made with the relay system handicapped at the scoring**: the three joinings that `nano/sparse` found refuted, the keeping by cells that join, and the keeping by replay. Their sizes are open again until they are scored after a read line.

*What this does not touch.* The forgetting (2% after the second task by either score). Every figure on prose. Any statement made about lines that were shown.

*What is owed.* The score after a read line beside the old one in every further run on sums (asked of the lines on 20 November); the final candidates of the ended lines scored again; the texts with letters between the numbers.
