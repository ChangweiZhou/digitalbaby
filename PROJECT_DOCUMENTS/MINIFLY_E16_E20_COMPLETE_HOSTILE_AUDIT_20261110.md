# MiniFly E16–E20 hostile audit

10 November 2026

## Disposition

The strongest recent results are narrower than the project narrative. Replay improves a particular allocation policy on the windowed learner. Longer credit improves acquisition in a windowless learner. E19 has a credible mathematical equivalence check and a useful negative result against its backprop reference. Each result survives within its own protocol. The evidence is still insufficient to advance a persistent architecture that learns, retains, revises, and acts from the same continuing state.

**Hold the E20A full roster at admission.** Its regular qualifier cannot validate the gapped prompt it is supposed to test. The primary gate also combines cases with different memory demands, scores the final suffix, and allows a score-time Store in only one comparison arm. Fixing the qualifier alone would leave the main inference unresolved. **Keep E16-M4, the corrected-panel repeats, and E18 open.** Their available artifacts have different completion states. **Close E19’s registered comparison as a miss.** Preserve the positive deep-credit result and remove the causal attribution of the entire shortfall to sign feedback. ## 1. E20 can answer the wrong question even if it runs

### Critical. The gapped qualification path is broken

`E16_COMPGAP=6` inserts up to six letters after each operator. `qualify()` still asserts a ten-byte prompt and parses both operators and all operands at fixed offsets. For `c46+SE10-75=`, the length assertion fails. Removing it leaves a parser that attempts `int(b'SE')`. The panel-disjointness check and `shortcut_ceilings()` in that path also assume the old layout. `life()` avoids the shortcut parser failure by routing it to the no-gap panel. That explains how the dry lives can produce receipts while the normal qualifier remains incompatible. The dry archive contains no matched-pipeline M dry receipt. This is a confirmed source defect with a direct admission consequence. [Prompt renderer](https://github.com/ChangweiZhou/digitalbaby/blob/04034a8a61d81eb94fabf88e046407ba8b9317c0/MOD97_LOCAL_RULE_20261106/exp16_integrated.py#L216-L231) · [Qualifier](https://github.com/ChangweiZhou/digitalbaby/blob/04034a8a61d81eb94fabf88e046407ba8b9317c0/MOD97_LOCAL_RULE_20261106/exp16_integrated.py#L551-L581) · [Life path](https://github.com/ChangweiZhou/digitalbaby/blob/04034a8a61d81eb94fabf88e046407ba8b9317c0/MOD97_LOCAL_RULE_20261106/exp16_integrated.py#L403-L420)

Admission requires a forced nonzero-gap fixture, a round trip against the structured operands, an actual gapped disjointness check, and both full-output and suffix counterexamples. Record the qualified panel hashes and settings alongside the dry-run receipt.

### High. The registered gate does not identify memory-dependent composition

At the first answer byte, a ten-byte window still sees both first-operand digits when the total gap is zero or one, one digit when the total is two, and neither digit when the total is three or greater. Under the nominal independent uniform 0–6 gap draws these strata have probabilities 3/49, 3/49, and 43/49. These are theoretical fractions. The actual panel needs an item-level visibility census. The score then pools the strata and compares only the last two answer bytes and EOS. `final` can count a correct suffix after an incorrect intermediate or separator. `exact` already checks the whole generated answer and EOS. The reported shortcut ceiling is taken from the no-gap panel and consists of a few selected heuristics. It is neither a measured gapped-window ceiling nor a bound on every available suffix shortcut. [Visibility and hypotheses](https://github.com/ChangweiZhou/digitalbaby/blob/04034a8a61d81eb94fabf88e046407ba8b9317c0/OPUS_TASK_E20_COMPOSITION_FROM_MEMORY_20261110.md#L17-L40) · [Scorer and shortcut code](https://github.com/ChangweiZhou/digitalbaby/blob/04034a8a61d81eb94fabf88e046407ba8b9317c0/MOD97_LOCAL_RULE_20261106/exp16_integrated.py#L299-L369)

Use held twins with byte-identical visible suffixes at `=` and distinct hidden operands, with both the intermediate and final answers differing. Require both first autonomous worked answers, including EOS, to be correct. A deterministic suffix-only predictor can earn at most one row per twin and zero both-correct twins. Score the same weights with native state intact and with state suppressed, keeping the Store off in both. Add a hidden-history swap or matched irrelevant-history control, because zeroing an internal representation can itself be an out-of-distribution perturbation. Include a same-length control that leaves the relevant operands visible, for example with leading distractors, to separate loss of visibility from generic long-prompt difficulty. Keep the existing registered metric separate if it has already been frozen. P20.1 combines an absolute floor with a mandatory 15-point no-gap penalty. A strong gapped score can miss that prediction because the penalty is small. The existence and cost of the capability deserve separate verdicts.

## 2. E16’s split correction solved one leak and left a second one

**High. The strict single-operation panel still permits equivalent supervision.** The repaired composition split excludes all taught operand triples from its held and within panels. In `strict_panels()`, standalone teaching is checked in both orientations for commutative operations, but the substep table is built and queried in only one orientation. A held `add(a,b)` survives when the direct reverse is absent but `add(b,a)` was a supervised step in a taught composition. For minimum and maximum, equivalent numeric pairs can also cross the mod-89 birth, mod-97 life, and mod-73 life because their answers are invariant to the modulus for shared operands. That cross-wrapper route matters when claiming a never-taught semantic relation. Preserve the ordered-byte endpoint, then build the stronger endpoint by canonicalizing each relevant supervision source against an explicit equivalence policy. Keep distinct modulus identities for genuinely modulus-dependent arithmetic. Audit cross-modulus accidental answer matches as potential shortcuts.
