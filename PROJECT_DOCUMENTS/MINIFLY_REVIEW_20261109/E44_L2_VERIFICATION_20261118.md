## 1. What was verified, and from which commit

**Terms.** A *visit* is 256 updates of learning on one *family*, the four operations addition, subtraction, maximum and minimum under one modulus. A *life* is a sequence of visits in a *world*, a seeded generator that fixes the families, their items and the order of the visits. In each update the network answers 16 *asked* items and then learns from 48 *taught* items. A *reduction item* is an asked item of addition or subtraction whose answer needs the reduction by the modulus. A *birth* is the network trained beforehand in experiment E41 by back-propagation, the exact gradient, from which a life starts; a birth is identified by its *seed*. An *arm* is the rule of the life: `naive` learns the 48 taught items of the stream; `replay` keeps a *store* of 55,209 past taught items, the size of one copy of the weights, and takes 24 of the 48 items of each update from it; `naive-wd0` and `replay-wd0` are the same arms with the weight decay of the optimizer set to 0 instead of 0.25. A *continuation* is a visit to the family of the visit before; a *return* is a visit to a family met before, after an *absence* of two or more visits. The *event file* of a life (`<life>.npz`) holds one row for each asked item; the *list file* (`<life>_list.npz`) holds the network's answers to a fixed list of 200 reduction items of each family at the end of every eighth visit and of each visit to the family; the *receipt* (`<life>.json`) records the settings, a digest of every visit's stream, the CPU time and the resumptions. A *resumption* continues an interrupted life from a *saved state* (`<life>_state_v<n>.pt`, the network, optimizer and counters after n visits). The *probe* is a branch of four visits over the families 52 to 55, which no life meets, started from a saved state. D1 to D15 are the definitions of the roster note, the file at the repository root whose name ends in `_TASK_E44_L2_ROSTER_FIRST_LONG_LIVES_20261117.md` (revision 2); the *instructions* are `PROJECT_DOCUMENTS/MINIFLY_REVIEW_20261109/COORDINATION_20261117/WORKER_E44_L2_FIRST_LONG_LIVES.md`. A *90% interval* is the note's D11: the mean of the per-world values plus or minus t(0.95, n − 1) × sd / √n, where n is the number of worlds; a difference is *higher* or *lower* when the interval excludes zero and *not distinguished* otherwise. The reviewer's three *expectations* (i), (ii) and (iii) are those the note records under the predictions.

## 3. Findings

Ordered by weight. Each states the report's text, the evidence, and the kind: an *error of fact*, an *overstatement* (a reading stated more strongly than the fixed rules and the figures allow), an *omission* (a required statement, table or limit that is missing), or a *matter of form*.

**1. Worlds that share a birth are counted as independent, and the report does not say so; several readings depend on it (omission).** The report lists the seeds (section 1: "seed 73129 for worlds 1, 4 and 7; seed 93683 for worlds 2, 5 and 8; seed 116089 for worlds 3 and 6") and says only that "Four worlds at 1,024 visits and eight at 256 give wide intervals". The eight worlds at 256 visits start from three birth files, and the four long worlds from three (worlds 1 and 4 share one). Within a seed the worlds differ, but the learner at the start does not. The note's D11 counts worlds, and its readings stand as the rules fix them. When the worlds of one seed are averaged into one value (three values, t with 2 degrees of freedom; not the note's definition; part C), the following readings no longer exclude zero: `naive-wd0` − `naive` in late level at 256 visits, −0.1103 [−0.3063, +0.0857] (the note's interval −0.0948 [−0.1657, −0.0238], *lower*; PL2.4); `replay-wd0` − `replay` in late level, −0.1614 [−0.4323, +0.1094] (−0.1431 [−0.2455, −0.0408], *lower*; PL2.9); `naive-wd0` − `naive` in kept at return after 33 visits or more, +0.0273 [−0.0404, +0.0949] (+0.0307 [+0.0033, +0.0581], *higher*; PL2.5); `replay-wd0` − `replay` in score trend and in trend of learning; and D13 for `naive` at 256 visits, whose trend of learning becomes +0.0701 [−0.0682, +0.2085], so that "still learning" would become "stuck". The readings that survive the averaging are: `replay` higher than `naive` in kept at return in every class at both lengths; `naive-wd0` higher than `naive` in kept at return after 5 to 32 visits (+0.0716 [+0.0107, +0.1325]); `naive-wd0` lower than `naive` in trend of learning (−0.1260 [−0.2198, −0.0323]); and both long arms still learning at 1,024 visits (trend of learning +0.0998 [+0.0837, +0.1159] and +0.1111 [+0.0618, +0.1603]). Two of the readings that change also rest on few items. The reading behind PL2.5 rests on 18 right answers of 471 items under `naive-wd0` against 4 under `naive`, over 121 returns in eight worlds (world 3 has 3 such returns and 13 items). ## 6. What the report may be read as showing, and what not

All statements hold up to the number of visits named, at learning rate 0.001, weight decay 0.25 (or 0), visits of 256 updates, a store of the size of the weights and a replay share of one half. Each is over four worlds with three births at 1,024 visits, or eight worlds with three births at 256 visits. The level of evidence is E2.

**Does a conventional learner keep learning over a long life, and does it learn new families faster as it ages?** Up to 1,024 visits, in each of the four long worlds and under both arms, the level a family reaches on its first visit rises with the number of families met before it. The trend of learning (D7) is +0.0822 to +0.1108 under `naive` and +0.0911 to +0.1446 under `replay`, and both arms are therefore *still learning* by D13. This reading survives counting the shared birth once. The score on returns over the second half of the life shows no trend that the interval can distinguish (`naive` +0.0147 [−0.0163, +0.0457]). The probe gives the same answer with fixed families. The first-visit level of families 52 to 55 is higher after 1,024 visits than after 64 in all four worlds: under `naive` by +0.3988 [+0.2275, +0.5702], from a mean of 0.427 to 0.826; under `replay` by +0.4761 [+0.3957, +0.5565], from 0.267 to 0.743. It is also higher after 1,024 visits than after 256 under both arms. "Faster" here means a higher level after one visit of 256 updates. The probe cannot separate the age of the network from the number of families it has met or from the growth of its weights. It rests on 24 branches, one per arm, world and age, from four worlds and three births. At 256 visits the evidence is weaker: `naive`'s trend of learning has a lower bound of +0.0032, and `replay`'s is not distinguished from zero. The report may not be read as showing that learning continues beyond 1,024 visits, or at another rate or visit length.

**What does a store of the size of the network add?** To what is kept, a large amount. At returns after 5 or more visits `replay` answers 0.537 of reduction items right on the first update at 1,024 visits, against 0.011 under `naive`. The difference is +0.5260 [+0.4340, +0.6181] at 1,024 visits and +0.4637 [+0.4189, +0.5084] at 256, positive in every world, and it survives counting the shared birth once. To the late level, nothing that the intervals distinguish: +0.0469 [−0.0112, +0.1050] at 1,024 visits (three of four worlds positive) and +0.0146 [−0.0175, +0.0466] at 256. This is a failure to distinguish and not a finding of equality, since the interval at 1,024 admits a gain of up to 0.105. The store lowers the first-visit level of a new family, by −0.1283 [−0.2013, −0.0552] in the median at 1,024 visits and −0.2163 [−0.2652, −0.1673] at 256. In the probe the store's deficit is distinguished after 64 visits (−0.1603 [−0.2941, −0.0265]) and not after 256 or 1,024. Part of this deficit may come from the visit length, which was calibrated for `naive` (finding 7).

**What does the absence of weight decay change?** It was read only up to 256 visits, in eight worlds with three births. Without decay the late level is lower: `naive-wd0` − `naive` −0.0948 [−0.1657, −0.0238]; `replay-wd0` − `replay` −0.1431 [−0.2455, −0.0408]. The trend of learning is lower: −0.1185 [−0.1607, −0.0763] and −0.0994 [−0.1540, −0.0449]. The first of these four readings and the two under `replay-wd0` are not distinguished when each birth is counted once (finding 1); the trend of learning under `naive-wd0` is. Without the store, kept at return is higher without decay, but remains small: after 5 to 32 visits +0.0750 [+0.0447, +0.1053], with 0.085 against 0.017 kept after 5 or more. With the store, no difference in kept at return is distinguished. The weights grow at every eighth visit in all 16 lives without decay, from a born norm of 52.8 to 126.3 (`naive-wd0`) and 122.9 (`replay-wd0`) after 256 visits, while with decay the norm of the recurrent matrix stays near its born value. The report may not be read as showing what the absence of decay does over 1,024 visits.

**What is kept of a family at a return?** Without the store almost nothing, after any absence of two or more visits: 0.000 to 0.027 in every cell of D15 at 1,024 visits. The most common answer is the right answer under the modulus of the family taught in the visit before (0.499 of 12,683 answers at returns, against a chance line of 0.007; 0.469 to 0.519 by class of absence). With the store, about half (0.537 after 5 or more visits), and answers under the last family's modulus fall to 0.013. Kept at return under `replay` is higher at returns to families visited more often (0.093 to 0.714 after 2 to 4 visits). The rise persists within each half of the life, but the store holds more items of a family met more often, and these figures do not separate the two (finding 4). Under `naive`, the level over the first 16 updates of a return is higher after many earlier visits in the first half of the life, and hardly so in the second half (0.118 against 0.123). The report may not be read as showing that a memory becomes more lasting by repetition alone under any arm.
