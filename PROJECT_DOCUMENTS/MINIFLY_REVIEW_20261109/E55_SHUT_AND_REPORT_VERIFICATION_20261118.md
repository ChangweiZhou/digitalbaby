On seed 73129, with the plain forward pass and with the update gates of the cells in reserve shut by a bias of 10 and their rows allowed to change only away from the directions in use, the gates stayed shut on every item of the first family that was measured, at 22 and at 50 positions (largest 1 − z 6.521e-5 and 1.37e-4; 5.4, 7.2), while learning opened them on the new task's items in three of the four runs. In the same runs the first family was partly kept in all four: up to 135, 34, 925 and 244 of its 31,684 answers changed, and the changes pass entirely through the cells in reserve, whose states of about 1e-3 on first-family items (growing with the position) are read by weights that the construction leaves free. The task on other bytes (`unrelated`) was learned under back-propagation from row 4,096 and was not learned within 16,384 updates under the sweep, although 126 cells opened. The task on the first family's own bytes (`conflict`) was learned under back-propagation from row 8,192, with 92.9 against 34.4 when the reserve is held shut, while 0.77% of the first family's answers changed; under the sweep no gate opened and the task was not learned. On items of 50 positions the first family was largely lost in that last run (36 to 65 of 300 right against 282), with the gates still shut.

The runs do not show that the first family is kept: in the terms of decision 14 its statement (the first family's answers kept while a task on other bytes was learned) is not met by any run of this arm, and the arm's one learned task on the first family's bytes was learned with the first family partly kept. They do not show that a gate held by a bias keeps the first family's answers, because the gate is not the route by which they change. They do not show anything beyond one seed, two tasks, one panel of each length and 16,384 updates; and the four acts of the program remain, with the record of the bytes in use and the single use of the keyed forward pass as parts of them, so that requirement A is not met.
