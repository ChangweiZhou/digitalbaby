## 1. What was verified, and from which commits

**Terms.** A *visit* is 256 updates of learning on one *family* (the four operations add, sub, max and min under one modulus). A *life* is a sequence of visits in a *world*, a seeded generator that fixes the families, their items and the order of the visits. In each update the network answers 16 *asked* items and then learns from 48 *taught* items. A *reduction item* is an asked item of add or sub whose answer needs the reduction by the modulus. A *birth* is the network trained beforehand in experiment E41, under one learning rule, from which a life starts. *Back-propagation* is the exact gradient. *The sweep* is the project's rule that passes the error backward by the signs of the weights. An *arm* is a birth together with the rule of the life: `naive` and `replay` start from the birth made by back-propagation and learn by back-propagation; `sweep` and `sweep+replay` start from the birth made by the sweep and learn by the sweep. The *store* is a buffer of 55,209 past taught items, the size of one copy of the weights; an arm with the store (`replay`, `sweep+replay`) learns 24 of the 48 items of each update from it (the share ρ = 0.5). The *event file* of a life (`<life>.npz`) holds one row for each asked item: its update, visit, task, whether the answer was right, the generated bytes, and counters. The *list file* (`<life>_list.npz`) holds the network's answers to a fixed list of 200 reduction items for each family, at the end of every eighth visit and of each visit to the family. The *receipt* (`<life>.json`) records the settings, a digest of every visit's stream, the CPU time and the resumptions. A *resumption* is the continuation of an interrupted life from a saved *state* (`<life>_state_v<n>.pt`, the network, optimizer and counters after n visits). D1 to D15 are the definitions of the roster note, the file at the repository root whose name ends in `_TASK_E44_L2_ROSTER_FIRST_LONG_LIVES_20261117.md` (revision 2); the *instructions* are `PROJECT_DOCUMENTS/MINIFLY_REVIEW_20261109/COORDINATION_20261117/WORKER_E44_L2_SWEEP_LONG_LIVES.md`. A *90% interval* is the roster note's D11: the mean of the four per-world values plus or minus t(0.95, 3 degrees of freedom) × sd / √4, that is the mean ± 1.1767 standard deviations; a difference is *higher* or *lower* when the interval excludes zero and *not distinguished* otherwise.

## 3. Findings

Ordered by weight. Each states the report's text, the evidence, and the kind: an *error of fact*, an *overstatement* (a reading stated more strongly than the fixed rules and the figures allow), an *omission* (a required statement or limit that is missing), or a *matter of form*.

**1. The readings are stated as differences between rules, but they are differences between two learners, each a birth together with its rule (overstatement).** The report says: "over 1,024 visits, the sweep is **behind** back-propagation in late level" (section 5.2). Its only limit on this is: "**Births.** The two rules start from different births, each made under its own rule, so a difference at early ages partly reflects the births" (section 7). The arms `sweep` and `naive` differ in two things at once: the rule of the life and the birth file. The sweep's births are 1.70 to 2.70 points lower in accuracy R than back-propagation's (the receipts of E41: 91.95, 92.55 and 90.90 against 94.65, 94.25 and 93.00). Before visit 0 they answer 0.9195, 0.9255 and 0.9090 of the birth family's 2,000 items, against 0.9465, 0.9425 and 0.9300 (part C). The reviewer's file `E44_GATE_FOR_THE_SWEEP_20261117.md`, section 7, states that at this visit length the instrument "compares two learners, each a birth together with its rule ... it cannot assign a difference between them to the rule of the life". Nothing in these lives confines the effect of the birth to early ages. The data and dose of the two births are the same (equal data digests, 40,960 updates each; part A), which removes one source of difference but not the difference of the birth. The fixed sentences are the instructions' and may be kept, but the report should say that "the sweep" means the arm `sweep` (the sweep's birth with the sweep). It should say that no difference is assigned to the rule alone, and the limit should not be restricted to early ages.

**2. The reading "behind" depends on treating worlds 1 and 4 as independent, and the report does not say so (omission).** The report says: "There are four worlds and three birth seeds, and worlds 1 and 4 share a seed. The 90% intervals over four worlds are wide" (section 7). Worlds 1 and 4 start from the same birth file under each rule, so they replicate the world and not the learner. The roster note's interval, over four worlds, gives −0.0663 [−0.1195, −0.0131], *lower*. With worlds 1 and 4 averaged into one value, as three births, the same difference is −0.0754 [−0.1539, +0.0032] (t with 2 degrees of freedom), *not distinguished* (part C). This second computation is not the roster note's definition and does not replace the reading the instructions fix. The report should still state that the reading "behind" is the only one of its main readings that excludes zero, and that it would not do so if the shared birth were counted once. The same is true of PS.2's outcome "Yes" (section 6).

**3. "Level", "the same", "not changed" and "not seen" are stated as findings where the intervals only fail to distinguish a difference (overstatement).** The instructions fix the words "behind / level / ahead" and "steeper / the same / flatter" by D11's interval, so "level" and "the same" are the required words, and their use is correct. The report qualifies "stuck" ("means only that no trend was found at this precision", section 5.3) but gives no such qualification to these words. With the store, "the sweep is **level** in late level" rests on −0.0424 [−0.2061, +0.1213]: the interval allows the sweep to be 0.21 behind or 0.12 ahead. "Its score trend over the second half is **the same as** back-propagation's" rests, without the store, on +0.0357 [−0.0391, +0.1105]. That interval allows a steeper trend of up to 0.11 per unit of ln(visit + 1), and the sweep's trend is steeper in worlds 1, 2 and 4 (+0.0727, +0.0561, +0.0730; world 3 −0.0590). "Overtaking in time ... is **not seen** up to 1,024 visits" is the reading the instructions allow ("a steeper trend with a late level that is behind or level" is not found). It should carry the statement that a steeper trend of the size the interval admits is not excluded. Section 5.7's word "**not changed**" is the word that the reviewer's message of 17 November fixed for an interval of the change that includes zero (appendix; corrected by the reviewer, see finding 5), so the author used it as instructed; it is D11's "not distinguished" under another name. With the store it rests on +0.0002 [−0.1382, +0.1385], an interval 0.28 wide, and it reads as a positive finding of no change, which these figures cannot give.

## 4. The resumptions

The `.starts.jsonl` records, the logs and the receipts agree. Three lives were resumed twice, `sweep` of worlds 1 and 2 and `sweep+replay` of world 1, and the other five lives ran in one run each (part B).

| Life | Starts (UTC) | Resumed from | Run 1 reached | Run 2 reached | Visits computed twice |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `sweep`, world 1 | 21:31:20, 22:12:02, 00:38:28 | 64, 640 | visit 88 | visit 718 | 25 and 79 |
| `sweep`, world 2 | the same | 64, 640 | visit 88 | visit 720 | 25 and 81 |
| `sweep+replay`, world 1 | the same | 64, 640 | visit 87 | visit 715 | 24 and 76 |

The report's "about 24 visits after the first stop and about 78 after the second" agrees with this table. The stop times are not in the files, because the log lines carry no times. Adding each run's CPU seconds to its start gives stops at about 21:50 and 00:33 UTC. CPU time is less than wall time, so the second estimate is a lower bound, and the report's "about 00:37" is consistent with it.

**Which state was loaded.** Each receipt's `resumes` names the state file and its SHA-256. The script read the six states from the branch's history (committed at 55053f10 and 1305d67b by the author's saver while the lives ran). The SHA-256 of each equals the one the receipt records for its resumption (part G). The four states that are still committed (world 1's two lives, after 256 and 1,024 visits) equal the receipts' `states` entries.

**The rule for resuming.** The instructions require only that an interrupted life be continued with `life --resume` (section 8). Neither they nor the roster note require a digest of the state file to be checked before loading. Read as text, the runner's `Life.load` refuses a state whose recorded source digests differ from the present sources. It checks the loaded weights and optimizer against the digests stored in the state. Before the next visit, `run_life` regenerates the stream of every earlier visit and refuses to continue if one visit's digest differs from the one in the state. So the state is checked from its contents, and the continuation cannot draw from a different stream. The file's own SHA-256 is computed only after loading and is recorded. This verification establishes that the recorded value is that of the committed state.

**Is a resumed life the same as an uninterrupted one?** No uninterrupted run of the long lives exists, so this cannot be shown by comparing hashes. Two pieces of evidence bear on it. First, in each resumed life the visits computed twice (104, 106 and 100 log lines) print the same share right and the same mean loss in the interrupted run and in the resumed run, with no exception (part B). Second, every row of each event file, across both resumption points, has the counter `seen` (the number of taught items of the task so far), `blind`, `task`, `wrap`, `mirror` and `correct` equal to the regenerated stream. The `update` and `visit` columns have no gap or double (part B). Under `sweep+replay` the counter `replayed` never falls. On every row of all four worlds it is equal to the same counter under `replay`, so the store's draws are the same under both rules. Check 4 (section 5) shows that the runner's resumption is exact, hash for hash, in a short life with the store. Together these make a difference between a resumed and an uninterrupted long life unlikely. What they do not show is equality of the weights after a resumption: the log lines are rounded to three and four decimals, and no hash of a resumed long life can be set beside an uninterrupted one.
