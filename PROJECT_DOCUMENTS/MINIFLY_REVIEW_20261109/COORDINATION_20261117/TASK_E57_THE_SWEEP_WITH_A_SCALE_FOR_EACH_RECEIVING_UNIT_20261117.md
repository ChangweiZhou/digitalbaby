## Terms

- *The network* reads one byte at each position of an item. It has a table of 8 numbers for each byte (the *byte table*), a map `l1` from these to the 512 values of the byte's *code*, a recurrent layer of 256 cells, and a readout. Each cell has a reset gate, an update gate and a candidate state. The *input map* (768 by 512) takes the code to the three gates of each cell, and the *recurrent map* (768 by 256) takes the cells' previous states to them. The readout is a map `lm` from the cells' states and a map `l2` from the code to 512 units, and a map `l3` from these to the 257 output classes (the 256 byte values and the symbol that ends an item). A *linear map* is the multiplication by one of these six weight matrices followed by the addition of its bias.
- *The dense network* (`G` in E42) has no restriction. In *the network with limited connections* (`F10`) each cell reads its own state and those of 10 fixed cells of its neighbourhood, so that each row of the recurrent map keeps 11 of its 256 entries; the other entries are zero throughout. In *the network with a limited input* (`in16` in E49) each cell of `F10`, in addition, reads 16 of the 512 values of the code, so that each row of the input map keeps 16 entries. The entries that a matrix keeps are given by its *mask*.
- *Back-propagation* (rule B) carries the error from the output toward the input; where it crosses a linear map backward, it is multiplied by the transpose of the map's weight matrix W. *The sweep* (rule S) differs in the crossing only: the error is multiplied by the transpose of sign(W) times a matrix S of non-negative magnitudes, with sign zero outside the mask. The gradient of a map's own weights is the same product of the arriving error and the map's input under both rules, and the gates are differentiated exactly under both.
- *The scale* is the choice of S. In every run of E26 to E50 each entry of S is one number for the whole matrix, the mean of |W| over its kept entries; this is the scale `matrix`. With the scale `row`, S[i, j] is the mean of |W| over the kept entries of row i, at every one of the six maps. Row i of a matrix holds the incoming weights of one *receiving unit*: a unit of the code at `l1`; one gate of one cell at the input map and at the recurrent map, so that a cell has three rows and three scales in each of these two matrices; a readout unit at `l2` and at `lm`; an output class at `l3`. A unit that receives through two maps has one scale at each. With the scale `rowcells`, the input map and the recurrent map have the scale `row` and the other four maps the scale `matrix`. With the scale `rowcol`, at the input map and at the recurrent map S[i, j] is the mean of row i times the mean of column j divided by the mean of the matrix (all over kept entries), and the other four maps keep the scale `matrix`.
- *Weight decay* is the optimizer's multiplication of a weight by 1 − 0.001 × 0.25 at every update, before the step of the gradient. In the runs of E42 and of E49 it acts on every tensor but the byte table. Other values were run in the dense network only: E46 ran births with a weight decay of 0.05 and of 0 on those tensors, and E38 and E43 ran continuations without it. In E50 `l1` takes no part and is not changed. *The code is not decayed* means that the weights and the bias of `l1` are, in addition, exempt from it; everything else is unchanged.
- *The first family* is four operations on two numbers below 89 (addition and subtraction modulo 89, the larger and the smaller of the two), each written as a line of bytes with 0 to 12 capital letters that carry no information between the operands. Half of the pairs of operands of each operation are taught. Every 256 updates the network is scored; the scores of one such point are a *scored row*. Score A is the percentage of right answers on 600 untaught pairs of addition and subtraction written with 12 letters. Score R is the percentage on 2,000 untaught pairs of addition and subtraction whose answer needs the reduction modulo 89, written without letters. Score T is the percentage of right answers on 600 taught pairs of addition and subtraction written with 12 letters (the entry `taught` of a scored row); "the taught pairs" in this brief means score T. A score *reaches 80* at the first of three consecutive scored rows at or above 80, and *updates to 80* is the number of updates at that scored row. A *level* is the mean over the last three scored rows.
- *The cosine* of a parameter tensor on a batch is the inner product of the sweep's gradient and the exact gradient of that tensor, divided by the product of their lengths; it is 1 when the two have the same direction. A *reading* is one entry of the list `cosines` of a receipt: the cosines and the two sizes taken before the first update or after a multiple of 2,048 updates (point 5 of the runner).
- A *receipt* is the file that a run or a check writes with its settings, its scored rows and the hashes of its sources. A *dry run* is a run made by a check to test the runner.
- E42, E49 and E50 are earlier experiments of the programme. A *runner* is the script that carries out the runs of one experiment. *The stream* is the sequence of batches of 64 taught items that a run is fed, one batch for each update; it is the same for every run of a seed. A *count* is the number of right answers on one of the sets of items on which the scores are taken, and the *batch digest* is the SHA-256 that a scored row records over all batches fed so far. `tensors_sha256` is the SHA-256 of the thirteen tensors of the network at the end of a run, and `start_tensors_sha256` the same before its first update. The *neighbourhood* of a cell is the 48 cells within three steps of it in each direction on a grid of 16 by 16 cells that closes on itself.

## The question

In E49, on seed 73129, the network with a limited input did not learn the first family under the sweep within 24,576 updates (A, R and T stayed below 5 at every scored row), while it learned it under back-propagation. E49's two other networks with a limited input failed under the sweep in the same way on that seed. The sweep also needs more updates than back-propagation in the network with limited connections, on each of E42's three seeds (1.36 to 1.89 times the updates to 80 on A). In every run the sweep has used one scale for a whole matrix. Every run of the network with a limited input has decayed the map that makes the code (`l1`); a weight decay of 0 was run at birth in the dense network only (E46). No other scale and no other decay of `l1` was tried in the network with a limited input. **In the network with a limited input, does the sweep learn the first family with a scale for each receiving unit; is a scale at the cells alone sufficient; and does it learn the family with the scale `matrix` when the code is not decayed? Does a scale for each receiving unit change the number of updates in the network with limited connections and in the dense network?**

The question belongs to Mr. Xu's requirement that learning be local, at the input as well (requirement C of the plan), and to his requirement on the number of updates (B1). Under the scale `matrix` the error that a unit sends back is multiplied by the mean over all weights of a matrix, a quantity that no single unit holds. Under the scale `row` it is multiplied by the mean of that unit's own incoming weights. The signs of the weights through which the error then travels are used under either scale; the scale does not change that.
