## Terms

- *The network* is that of E50's stage 3, the setting `C46-one` (`exp50_code.build("C46-one", 73129)`): 256 recurrent cells of the gated kind of E26; each cell reads 10 other cells, fixed for the run and drawn from the 48 cells around it on a grid of 16 by 16, and itself; the input is a fixed code of 512 values in which each of the 46 used bytes turns on 11 values of its own, and each cell reads 46 of the values, one of every used byte; the readout is dense (every one of the 512 readout units reads all 256 cells). The byte table and `l1` take no part.
- *The sweep* (rule S, `local=True`) sends the error back through each linear map by the signs of its weights times one scale for the map; *the exact gradient* (rule B, `local=False`) sends it back through the weights. The sweep is the working rule. It has three parts that a neuron does not have: the sign of the forward connection, one scale for a matrix, and the pass back over the item after its end (decision 38); every report that uses it names these three parts. The exact gradient is outside what a neuron has on any network, because a neuron does not know other neurons' weights (decision 38, which revises decision 31). On this network it is *a reference arm*: it shows what the same limit would give if the relayed signal were exact, and it is never called a candidate or "the local learner". *The dense reference* is back-propagation over the whole item on the dense network (decision 31); for seed 73129 it is E42's configuration `G` under the exact gradient (`$D:$M/exp45/e42/e42_G_B_73129.json`: 80 on R after 6,400 updates, on A after 5,120), and the report gives it beside the record.
- *The record* is the two runs of E50's stage 3, `$D:$M/exp45/e50/e50_C46-one_<S|B>_73129.json`, in which the error is passed back over the whole item at the rate 0.001. They are not repeated.
- *A position* is one byte of an item. An item is the start symbol, a byte that names the task, two digits (the first operand), the operation byte, 0 to 12 letters, two digits (the second operand), the sign `=`, and the answer of two digits with an end symbol. With k letters an item has 10 + k positions under teacher forcing (10 to 22). The loss is taken at the three *answer positions* only (7 + k, 8 + k and 9 + k, counted from 0). The two digits of the first operand are read at positions 2 and 3.
- *The state* of a cell at a position is its output there. *The error at a state* is the derivative of the loss with respect to it, as the rule in force computes it.
- *One step of relay* (see the next section) moves the error from the states of one position to the states of the position before, along every path at once. *A cell's own path* leads from a cell's state at a position to its own state at the position before, through the term `z * h(t-1)` and through the cell's reading of itself in the recurrent map (the diagonal entry of each of the three gate blocks of `rnn.weight_hh_l0`, which is inside the mask). *A crossing* leads from a cell's state at a position to the state, at the position before, of another cell that it reads (an entry of the recurrent map off the diagonal and inside the mask). A path back from an answer's error is a sequence of steps along own paths and crossings; its number of positions counts both, its number of crossings counts the second only.
- *Form (a), the limit in positions* (revisions 1 and 2): every path, a cell's own included, is cut K positions back. *K* is the largest number of positions over which an error is passed back; it is not the number of passes between cells. *Form (b), the limit in crossings* (revision 3, after the outside audit, its sections 3.1 and 3.7): a cell's own path is kept for the whole item, and only the paths with at most X crossings are kept. *X* is the largest number of crossings on a path along which an error is passed back. In both forms, under the sweep, a cell's reading of itself is sent back as in the record: by the sign of its weight times the recurrent map's one scale (`layer.sign_scale(m)`, whose mean is taken over the mask with its diagonal). *The whole item* is K of at least 21 in form (a) and X of at least 21 in form (b); either is then the record's update.
- A run is scored every 256 updates (a *scored row*). A threshold is *reached* at the first of three consecutive scored rows at or above it; the first single row at or above it is given in brackets. A *level* at an update is the mean of the three scored rows that end there. *A* and *R* are the shares right on never-taught pairs (A: 600 items of addition and subtraction with 12 letters; R: 2,000 items whose answer needs the reduction by the modulus, written with no letters); *T* is the share right on 600 taught items with 12 letters. These are E50's definitions and E50's scorer (`exp42_cells.panels`).

## What one step is in this network (from the source)

In `e50_sparse_code.forward` the state of cell i at position t is computed from the input at position t and from `(Wh * mask) h(t-1)`, the states at position t - 1 of the 10 cells that cell i reads and of cell i itself, and from the cell's own state through the term `z * h(t-1)`. There is no other path between cells. Backward, the error at the state of cell i at position t therefore goes to the states at position t - 1 of the cells that cell i reads (through the signs of its weights under the sweep, through the weights under the exact gradient) and to its own state at position t - 1, through its own reading of itself in the recurrent map (crossed like the others) and through the term `z * h(t-1)` (differentiated exactly under both rules). One step is thus a step back in position and a step between cells at once; the source does not allow one without the other. Two consequences are stated here and in the report. First, because the readout is dense, every cell receives an error directly at every answer position; relay is not needed for the error to reach a cell (before any relay the readout's error is non-zero at 256 of 256 cells at every answer position of the first batch, under both rules: the outside audit's section 3.2, recomputed in `e61_revision_3_check.py`, part 2.3), and what K limits is how far back in the item an answer's error can change what a cell did. Second, form (a) cuts the cell's own path together with the paths between cells. Form (b) separates the two: it keeps each cell's own path for the whole item and limits only the crossings; it is run here beside form (a).

## The question

Two questions, on the same network and beside the same record, and a third on the record's rate.

*Form (b), the limit in crossings.* Over how many crossings between cells must the error be passed for the network to learn the first family, when each cell keeps its own path for the whole item? With X = 0 each cell uses only the error that reaches it from the readout and the error carried back over its own path; no credit is passed between cells. With X = 1, 2 and 4 the credit for what a cell passed to another cell is passed back over at most that many crossings. In this network an error at one cell has reached every cell after four or five crossings, depending on the cell (`check_e61_brief.py`, part 4.2), so that X = 4 nearly covers the network. The record's trained networks depend wholly on what passes between cells: when each cell keeps its own path and receives from the other cells only the mean over a panel's items of what they send, both networks answer fewer than 1% of the items of R, A and T exactly (the outside audit's section 3.5, reproduced by running its script again). Credit for what a cell passed to another is therefore credit that the record's solution needs, and X withholds or grants it.

*Form (a), the limit in positions.* How far back in the item must an answer's error act when every path is cut? The record passes it over the whole item, which the principle does not allow as one global pass and allows as relay. The distances in an item say what K can reach: from the first answer position the second operand is 1 and 2 positions back, the operation byte 3 + k, the first operand 4 + k and 5 + k, the byte that names the task 6 + k. If the number of letters is uniform on 0 to 12, an answer's error reaches both digits of the first operand in no item for K of 4 or less, in 4 of 13 items for K = 8 and in 12 of 13 for K = 16. Whether a smaller K than the distance suffices is open, because the weights are the same at every position: what a cell learns from the second operand, two positions before the answer, it also applies to the first. Only K = 0 and K = 8 are run. K = 0 beside X = 0 isolates what a cell's own path over the item contributes. K = 8 is the one value that tests the open question (whether credit over short items teaches long ones): it reaches both digits of the first operand in 4 of 13 training items, in every item of R and in no item of A. The runs of K = 1, 2, 4 and 16 of revision 2 are removed: K of 4 or less reaches both digits of the first operand in no training item, and K = 16 reaches them in 12 of 13, so that these eight runs give the least for their hours (the outside audit's section 3.7).

The scored items do not have the distribution of the training items. The items of R are written with no letters, so that the first operand stands 4 and 5 positions before the first answer position and K = 5 reaches both of its digits in every item of R. The items of A and of T are written with 12 letters, so that the first operand stands 16 and 17 positions before the first answer position and only K of 17 or more reaches both digits; K = 16 reaches the second digit only. With K between 5 and 16, items of R's form are among the training items in which the error reaches the first operand (one training item in 13 has no letters), whereas the items of A and T are reached only through what the weights, which are the same at every position, carry over from shorter items. For this reason a limit is called sufficient here only if it meets the criterion on A as well as on R (decision 39 (i)). The report states beside every reading on R that its items have no letters, and beside every reading on A or T that theirs have 12.

*What each form keeps of the credit at the first operand.* The outside audit computed, on the record's start and trained weights, the share of the whole-item credit at the states of positions 2 and 3 that each form delivers (point 4 defines the share). For items with 12 letters it is 0 for every K of 8 or less; at K = 16 it is 0.49 (sweep) and 0.45 (exact gradient) at the start and 0.33 and 0.16 in the trained networks. Under form (b) it is 0.05 and 0.03 at X = 0, 0.97 and 0.95 at X = 4 at the start, and 0.04 and 0.02 at X = 0, 0.47 and 0.46 at X = 4 in the trained networks; for items with no letters, X = 4 delivers 0.97 and 0.93 in the trained networks (the audit's section 3.4; recomputed by a separate method in `e61_revision_3_check.py`, part 2.5). These figures describe gradients in networks trained with the whole item; they do not say what a learner trained under a limit would do.

*The rate (decision 39 (ii)).* Two runs of the whole item at the rate 0.003, one under each rule, say whether the record's comparison of the two rules, made at 0.001, holds at a second rate.

2. **The update with the error relayed over K positions (form (a)).** The reference form is the following; `n` is the object that `R50.build` returns and `U` is 256.

       def one_step(n, gi, hp, local, scale):          # the step of S50.forward for any rows: gi (..., 768), hp (..., 256)
           m = n.m
           gh = L42.rec(n.layer, m, hp, m.rnn.weight_hh_l0, m.rnn.bias_hh_l0, local, scale)
           i_r, i_z, i_n = gi.chunk(3, -1); h_r, h_z, h_n = gh.chunk(3, -1)
           r = T.sigmoid(i_r + h_r); z = T.sigmoid(i_z + h_z); c = T.tanh(i_n + r * h_n)
           return (1 - z) * c + z * hp

       def relayed_grads(n, x, y, mask, local, K):
           m, layer, iface = n.m, n.layer, n.iface
           z1 = n.table[x]; B, L = x.shape
           Wi, bi, Wh, bh = m.rnn.weight_ih_l0, m.rnn.bias_ih_l0, m.rnn.weight_hh_l0, m.rnn.bias_hh_l0
           gi = I49.masked(z1, Wi, bi, iface.mask_in.repeat(3, 1).float(), local)
           scale = layer.sign_scale(m) if local else None
           with T.no_grad():                           # (a) the states, without credit
               h = T.zeros(B, U); before = []
               for t in range(L):
                   before.append(h); h = one_step(n, gi[:, t], h, local, scale)
           Hp = T.stack(before, 1).requires_grad_()    # h(t-1) for every position, as a leaf
           Hs = one_step(n, gi, Hp, local, scale)      # (b) the step of every position at once, with credit for one step
           Hd = Hs.detach().requires_grad_()           # (c) the readout, and its error at every state
           z2 = T.tanh(lin(z1, m.l2, local) + lin(Hd, m.lm, local))
           loss = E.cross_entropy(lin(z2, m.l3, local)[mask], y[mask])
           for q in m.parameters(): q.grad = None
           loss.backward()                             # gradients of l2, lm and l3; Hd.grad is the error at the states
           d = Hd.grad; total = d.clone()
           for k in range(K):                          # (d) K steps of relay
               g, = T.autograd.grad(Hs, Hp, grad_outputs=d, retain_graph=True)
               d = T.zeros_like(g); d[:, :-1] = g[:, 1:]        # the error at h(t-1) is the error at the states of position t-1
               total = total + d
           gs = T.autograd.grad(Hs, [Wi, bi, Wh, bh], grad_outputs=total)       # (e) the cells' own weights
           for q, g in zip([Wi, bi, Wh, bh], gs): q.grad = g
           return float(loss.detach())

2b. **The update with at most X crossings between cells (form (b)).** The reference form is the outside audit's direct definition (its section 3.7): X + 1 copies of the states, equal in value; copy v takes a cell's own earlier state from itself and the other cells' earlier states from copy v + 1; the last copy takes the other cells' earlier states as constants; the readout reads copy 0; the loss is differentiated once. `own` applies the diagonal of the recurrent map (a cell's reading of itself) and is sent back by the same value as the crossing: under the exact gradient by the weight, under the sweep by the sign of the weight times the map's one scale. `ROWS = T.arange(3 * U)` and `COLS = T.arange(U).repeat(3)`, so that `Wh[ROWS, COLS]` is each cell's reading of itself in the three gates.

       class OwnSign(T.autograd.Function):         # forward: each cell's reading of itself; back: sign(d) * scale to h, the outer product to d
           @staticmethod
           def forward(ctx, h, d, scale):          # h (..., 256), d (768,)
               ctx.save_for_backward(h, d, scale)
               return T.cat([h, h, h], -1) * d
           @staticmethod
           def backward(ctx, g):
               h, d, scale = ctx.saved_tensors
               gh = (g * (d.sign() * scale)).unflatten(-1, (3, U)).sum(-2)
               gd = (g * T.cat([h, h, h], -1)).reshape(-1, 3 * U).sum(0)
               return gh, gd, None

       def own(h, d, local, scale):
           return OwnSign.apply(h, d, scale) if local else T.cat([h, h, h], -1) * d

       def gated(gi_t, gh, h_own):                 # the gates of S50.forward, with the cell's own earlier state in z * h(t-1)
           i_r, i_z, i_n = gi_t.chunk(3, -1); h_r, h_z, h_n = gh.chunk(3, -1)
           r = T.sigmoid(i_r + h_r); z = T.sigmoid(i_z + h_z); c = T.tanh(i_n + r * h_n)
           return (1 - z) * c + z * h_own

       def crossings_grads(n, x, y, mask, local, X):
           m, layer, iface = n.m, n.layer, n.iface
           z1 = n.table[x]; B, L = x.shape
           Wi, bi, Wh, bh = m.rnn.weight_ih_l0, m.rnn.bias_ih_l0, m.rnn.weight_hh_l0, m.rnn.bias_hh_l0
           gi = I49.masked(z1, Wi, bi, iface.mask_in.repeat(3, 1).float(), local)
           scale = layer.sign_scale(m) if local else None
           d = Wh[ROWS, COLS]                          # (768,): each cell's reading of itself
           hs = [T.zeros(B, U) for _ in range(X + 1)]; first = []
           for t in range(L):
               new = []
               for v in range(X + 1):
                   other = hs[v + 1] if v < X else hs[v].detach()
                   gh = L42.rec(layer, m, other, Wh, bh, local, scale) + own(hs[v], d, local, scale) - own(other, d, local, scale)
                   new.append(gated(gi[:, t], gh, hs[v]))
               hs = new; first.append(hs[0])
           Hs = T.stack(first, 1)                      # copy 0, which the readout reads
           z2 = T.tanh(lin(z1, m.l2, local) + lin(Hs, m.lm, local))
           loss = E.cross_entropy(lin(z2, m.l3, local)[mask], y[mask])
           for q in m.parameters(): q.grad = None
           loss.backward()                             # every weight's gradient over the paths with at most X crossings

## What it cannot show

- Anything beyond one seed, one family, half the pairs and this network; the other seeds follow only on the coordinator's word.
- The number of connections: every cell reads 10 cells here. The number of connections is E62's subject. With 256 cells, a cell that read about fifty cells drawn from all cells would reach nearly every cell in two steps (50² = 2,500 > 256), and one that read all 48 cells of its neighbourhood would reach every cell in three; Mr. Xu's example of several passes (1, 50, 50², 50³) cannot be reproduced at this width, and this brief does not claim it. In this network (10 of the 48 neighbours) an error at one cell has reached every cell after four or five crossings, depending on the cell; because the readout is dense, this number does not limit which cells receive an error.
- Relay as the only way an error reaches a cell, in either form: the readout is dense (see the section on what one step is). The test of relay between cells in a network in which only some cells are read is decision 40's, on E53's network when E53 is verified.
- Relay over several crossings as the only route to a cell. In this harness one crossing takes one byte, forward as well as backward: the first answer byte is read out at the position of `=`, one position after the last digit of the second operand, so that a cell more than one crossing from the cells that receive that digit cannot hold it yet. A network in which relay over several crossings is the only route to cells that the task needs requires several crossings for each byte, forward and backward; that is a new network with no record beside it (the outside audit's section 3.6). In form (b) every crossing is also a step back in position.
- What a learner trained under a limit would do, from the figures quoted in this brief: the shares and cosines of the outside audit's section 3.4 describe gradients in two networks trained with the whole item, and a cosine is not a learning curve. That the record's networks answer almost nothing when a cell receives only the mean of what the other cells send (its section 3.5) shows that the record's solution uses what passes between cells; it does not show that no solution exists without it. The cost of form (b) is a count until check 5 measures it. The predictions are guesses on one seed (the outside audit's section 6).
