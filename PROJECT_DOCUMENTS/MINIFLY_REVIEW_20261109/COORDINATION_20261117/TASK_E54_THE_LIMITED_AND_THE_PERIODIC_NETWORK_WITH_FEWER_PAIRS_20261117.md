## Terms

- *The dense network* (`G` in E42) is the network of the present size, 256 cells, each connected to every cell. *The limited network* (`F10`) gives each cell 10 fixed connections to cells of its neighbourhood on a 16 by 16 grid. *The periodic network* (`P8`) is a limited network whose cells update every 1, 2, 4 or 8 positions and whose connections are replaced during learning by E42's rule: at every 256th update (an *event*) a cell gives up each connection that is at least 512 updates old and whose strength is below theta times the mean strength of the cell's connections, and takes a new one at random in its neighbourhood (`replace` in `e42_cells.py`, lines 173 to 195). theta is 0.5, E42's value. All three are configurations of `exp42_cells.py`. *The present width* is this size (`h512` in E41's receipts); *the half width* is E41's dense network of 128 cells (`h256`).
- *Back-propagation* (rule B) is the exact gradient; *the sweep* (rule S) passes the error backward by the signs of the weights.
- The four operations are add, sub, max and min under modulus 89. An item is the code of its operation, the first operand, the sign of the operation, k capital letters that carry no information, the second operand and the equals sign; "at 12 letters" means k = 12. *The taught fraction* f is the share of the pairs of operands that are taught; the other pairs are *untaught*. *The stream* is the sequence of batches of 64 taught items. A *panel* is a fixed set of test items.
- Score A is the share right on untaught pairs of add and sub at 12 letters (300 items each); score R the share right on 1,000 untaught pairs of each of add and sub whose answer needs the reduction by the modulus, without letters; the *length test* is the share right on 300 untaught pairs of add at 40 letters.
- The scores are taken every 256 updates; one such set of scores is a *scored row*. *Updates to 80* on a score is the first of three consecutive scored rows at or above 80%. A *level* at an update is the mean of a score over the three scored rows that end at that update.
- Two *rises* are given for every curve of R. The *last rise* is the mean of R over the nine scored rows that end at the last update of the run, less the mean over the nine scored rows that end 8,192 updates earlier. The *rise before it* is that second mean less the mean over the nine scored rows that end 16,384 updates before the last update. A curve of R *has flattened* if both rises are smaller than 3 points in size. A curve with a rise of minus 3 points or less has not flattened, and the report says that it fell. (One span is not enough. Applied before the end of E41's twelve runs at the half width, at updates 20,480 to 36,864, a test on the last rise alone says "flattened" 30 times of 60, and in 6 of the 30 the level then rose by 5.4 to 11.5 points before the run ended; the test on both rises says it 15 times, and in none of them did the level rise by 5 points. The scatter of R from row to row is 2.0 to 4.0 points at a quarter of the pairs in E41, so a difference of two means of nine rows has a scatter of 0.9 to 1.9 points.)
- A *receipt* is the JSON file that a run writes, with its settings, its scored rows and its hashes. The *batch digest* is the SHA-256 over all batches presented so far, recorded at every scored row. A *dry run* is a run of fewer updates than the full length, made to test the runner.
- The report is *licensed* to make a statement if the condition fixed in this brief for that statement holds.

## The question

E42 compared the limited and the periodic network with the dense one at one taught fraction, a half. **With a quarter, with 15% and with a tenth of the pairs taught, do the limited and the periodic network generalise better than the dense one, as well, or worse, and is any of the three fractions enough for one of them where it is not enough for the dense network?**
