# MiniFly follow-up on project status and what would justify advancing the architecture

November 10, 2026

MiniFly has become a credible small-scale continual-learning project. It has demonstrated that rehearsal can preserve some learned rules and that deeper temporal credit can make a windowless model trainable. The architectural claim still needs one learner that acquires a useful rule, retains it through interference, revises it when circumstances change, and applies the revision to an untaught case. The latest verified cloud snapshot contains E19 Stage A and gradient checks. E18, E19 Stage B, and E16-M4 lacked completed reports in the branches checked for this assessment.

This note follows the [E16–E19 static source audit](MINIFLY_E16_E19_HOSTILE_SOURCE_AUDIT_20261110.md). It is an assessment and proposed decision framework. The findings below refer to archived experiments. The proposed test has not been run. ## What the evidence earns

| Area | What the experiments support | What remains open |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Associative memory | Earlier fly-derived systems could acquire and retrieve taught associations, sometimes under intervening learning or changed presentation. | Whether fly-derived indexing contributes to decisions more effectively than a simpler memory given the same information and budget. |
| Retention by replay | E16-M3 preserves hard-pair performance better at higher replay shares within the tested schedule. | How much is attributable to additional rehearsal, reduced fresh interference, or changed reservoir coverage. How the system handles stale evidence and rarely revisited skills. |
| Learning in a windowless network | E17 Phase 4 recovers single-operation acquisition with the first bank trainable. | Whether temporal reach alone causes the rescue, whether useful skills survive without rehearsal, and whether the architecture supports composition. |
| Forward temporal credit | E19’s exact-weight trace matches an independent autograd calculation on the tested architecture and objective. The backprop reference clears its Stage A feasibility gate. | Learning under the forward-only sign rule, effectiveness on a full life, and an advantage in measured memory, feedback, or compute. |
| Closed-loop behavior | Earlier consequence-driven agents improved on bounded action tasks. | Whether the learner uses a recent observation to choose between actions that would otherwise look identical, including on a held combination after a rule change. |

### Replay buys retention at an opportunity cost

E16-M3 is a real positive result for the implemented allocation policy. Hard-pair performance ended near 1% with zero replay and recovered as replay increased. But the optimizer runs for a fixed number of updates while the policy replaces fresh examples with replay. In a full batch, moving from 50% to 75% replay cuts fresh rows from 32 to 16. The drift clock advances by update, so the learner also receives fewer observations from each part of the stream, admits fewer fresh records, and changes its exposure to interference. Row share differs from supervised-token share because composition answers are longer. A higher share can be a good allocation at a fixed compute budget even if the mechanism has no special ability to protect a skill.

There are two distinct questions. For a deployment comparison, allow the scheduler to trade fresh observations for rehearsal and score the resulting policy per unit of cost. For causal attribution, hold the fresh observation sequence and its timing fixed, begin from the same acquired state, and compare old-example replay with a matched additional-update control. Record delivered observations, replay rows, supervised tokens by family and source, retained bytes, and actual compute. A flat final score on an old skill that continues to be replayed should be recorded as supported retention under rehearsal. A no-rehearsal interval tests the stronger claim.

The practical concern is revision. The existing runs retain old skills that are still correct. A learner facing a changed cue must update what it does now while preserving knowledge unrelated to that change. Uniform replay can reinforce obsolete answers. This behavior needs a direct test rather than an inference from the retention curve. ### E17 improves acquisition while exposing an architectural limit

The unfrozen Phase 4 arm acquires single operations on the new modulus. The project can retire the earlier conclusion that the first bank must be frozen. The short-trace comparison changes feedback scaling as well as temporal reach, so the rescue does not isolate either mechanism. The full-time sign arm uses a backward pass over the item and costs about as much CPU as matched backprop. Its original unrehearsed development performance falls from roughly 93–95% to 11–43% by the end. Calling the birth preserved can mean the system remains trainable, even though its original behavior has substantially degraded.

The most consequential result is the composition floor. Complete answers on individual hard E17 panels remain around or below 4%, including under matched backprop. This is a warning about the tested model and protocol. Improving its credit rule may still improve single-step learning, but the project needs a positive reference for composition before a composition failure can be pinned on the learner’s update rule. Report absolute complete-answer accuracy alongside suffix accuracy and ratios. On corrected E16 panels, 13/15 pass the family-average gate and only 2/15 pass the harder pair gate. These are different conclusions about the same learner. ### E19 separates a mathematical success from a learning claim

The exact-weight trace has an independently checked equivalence result. That is an engineering milestone with a defined boundary. The tested model uses fixed rates and a restricted architecture. Its backprop-trained Stage A hard means at 12 distractors are 69.0%, 70.5% and 69.3%. The registered Stage B relative cutoff corresponds to about 59–60% absolute. Report that absolute value if the gate passes, along with longer-delay performance. The Stage A versus sign-rule comparison uses different decay, so any gap needs a matched decay comparison before it can be attributed to the sign rule.

The project should keep separate verdicts for gradient equivalence, learning, and deployment. A forward computation for gradient traces does not itself demonstrate learning with the sign rule. A successful training run does not itself demonstrate incremental inference, online updates, bounded state under a continuous stream, or a resource advantage over ordinary backprop. The present scorer rebuilds state from the prefix for each generated byte. The cost ledger should count the implementation that produces the claimed result, including trace storage, optimizer state, replay, and state reconstruction. ## The project-level risk

Different systems are solving different parts of the problem. The fly-derived architecture contributes the historical memory evidence. The windowed network provides the strongest replay-backed composition evidence. The windowless network now acquires single operations with deeper credit. E19 tests a more tractable gradient formulation in another architecture. These are valid separate findings. Summing them would credit a single learner with capabilities that have never competed for the same parameters, observations, or memory budget.

The project should also distinguish three mechanisms in the multiple-timescale discussion. Fixed decay rates preserve activity across positions within a record. An inner loop would repeatedly transform held information. Slow weights would protect learned behavior across episodes. The current banks reset between records and establish no cross-record result by having several decay rates. Each proposal should be tested against the particular failure it addresses. An inner loop aimed at composition needs an inference compute control. Slow weights aimed at retention need a matched replay and memory control. ## A promotion test for one continuing learner

Use arithmetic as a diagnostic and give the promotion decision a small learn–revise–act task. The test should force the current state of the world to matter.

1. **Acquire.** Teach a stable action–consequence relation through a bounded experience stream. Establish an unrelated skill and score both before any change. Freeze the eligible comparisons and evaluation cases in advance.
2. **Revise.** Change the meaning of a familiar cue while keeping unrelated knowledge valid. Provide enough consequences that a learner could infer the change. Keep the same continuing learner state. Do not signal the regime with a task ID or a privileged boundary unless that is explicitly a reference arm.
3. **Act on a held combination.** Pair cases with the same visible decision cue but different relevant intervening observations. Correct actions must be opposite across the pair. Score the learner’s first autonomous action before feedback and require both sides to be correct for the paired endpoint. This rules out success from always choosing the latest response to the cue.
4. **Measure collateral damage.** Recheck the unrelated skill and report whether behavior still follows the obsolete rule. Score at fixed budgets of newly delivered consequences. Record replay, feedback bandwidth, state and optimizer memory, peak process memory, and actual CPU. Report both per-example and per-supervised-token costs when they diverge.

Use a recency table and an ordinary compact recurrent learner with the same observations and feedback. The table tests how much the task rewards recency. The recurrent control tests whether the claimed architectural addition earns its cost. Use an oracle to establish identifiability and a wrong-history or shuffled-cue control to show that the relevant observation actually drives success. Give matched arms the same scoring interface and log any privileged inputs used only by reference arms.

Treat the first qualified implementation as a small development check. Before allocating a larger roster, require that the oracle solves the task, the proposed paired endpoint penalizes the shortcuts, and at least one reference acquires and revises the rule under the declared interface. If a reference cannot solve the assay, diagnose the task or representation before interpreting a negative candidate result. If a candidate improves only the easy average, classify the gain there and keep the promotion decision open.

## What would change the decision

- **Advance the architecture** if the same learner improves first-action paired transfer over both controls at a matched budget, retains the unrelated skill, and retains the advantage when the observation and scorer are audited. If the benefit is assigned to a fly-derived component, ablate that component in the same architecture.
- **Keep a mechanism as a bounded tool** if it improves one component, such as replay allocation or online gradient calculation, while the integrated comparison is neutral. Preserve the narrower result and its cost.
- **Reconsider the candidate** if a simple control matches it, gains disappear after matching exposure, or the added mechanism buys accuracy only by exceeding the resource budget. That result should end the current promotion case and specify what evidence would reopen it.

Before any promotion claim, record the qualifying learning modes separately. Supervised development, backward temporal credit within an episode, forward traces with a vector error, and lifetime action-consequence feedback supply different information and computation. The boundary on what counts for the final learner is still an owner decision. A run can answer a narrower question while that boundary is open.

The source audit adds a practical prerequisite. The gate must bind the actual loss, panel, optimizer, checkpoint and replay policy to each receipt and reject mismatched pairs. Verify gradient and update coverage by byte role, particularly where the useful signal arrives later than the input.
