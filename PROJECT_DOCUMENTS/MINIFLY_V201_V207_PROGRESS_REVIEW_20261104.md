# MiniFly progress review · V201–V207

November 4, 2026

The recent gains are real. Several results arrived together, but different MiniFly implementations crossed different thresholds. The clearest advance is that we can now identify why some tasks failed, intervene on that mechanism, and recover performance in a controlled comparison.

## What moved

- **V206 recovered a memory association that the short window had lost.** With 40 or 64 extra bytes between the person and destination, the bounded anchor achieved about **95%** exact answers with its own newline, versus about **14%** for the matched split control. The gain appeared in all eight worlds. Both split arms wrote **360 rows per committed byte** on the long cases, and wrong-person answers fell from **14.36% to zero**. The useful change was which bytes the address retained. The experiment supplied write timing and placed the subject early enough for the anchor to retain it. [V206 report](../minifly_v206_causal_entity_binding_design_20261104/official/REPORT.md) · [Receipts](../minifly_v206_causal_entity_binding_design_20261104/official/RESULT_BUNDLE.zip)
- **V201 learned from consequences of its own actions.** Across **10,240 live choices**, MiniFly received sampled safety or harm without correct-action demonstrations. After reversal and learning on other cues, its final read-only choice score was **100%** on new renderings of known cues, versus **67.19%** when consequences were reassigned among actions on the same logged experience. During live reversal learning, safety was **63.70%**. The system explicitly saves one pending action, so the result validates using correctly paired consequences. Assigning a delayed consequence among several pending actions remains open. The nominal neutral gap did not advance learner state, so the retention evidence comes from intervening learning. [V201 result bundle](../minifly_v201_action_consequence_design_20261103/official/RESULT_BUNDLE.zip)
- **The parallel QA and pretraining results have distinct scopes.** V205 reached **99.5%** on a changed-prefix QA1 panel without gold-answer feedback. Its QA2 result with both people and objects renamed was **87.7%**, using supplied candidate operations and a learned selector. The **85.2%** fixed-channel score previously cited alongside it came from the **original** panel, where the learned selector scored **100%**. The both-renamed panel had no matched fixed-channel result. V207 showed that more ordered prose improved frozen prediction on held-out authors from **2.786 to 2.708 bits/byte**. Markov-3 scored better at **2.492**, and QA transfer stayed unopened because the development questions never called the pretrained predictor. ## Why the jump makes sense

**Waiting helps only if the earlier information is still available when it matters.** V204 waited for more bytes, but its write address could already have lost the person. V206 kept the earlier bytes accessible. V201 uses the same principle in a different setting. It keeps the chosen action and its active rows available until the consequence arrives. In both cases, a short local update can work because the architecture preserves what the update needs. V200 shows a limit of that principle. Holding query state helped unfamiliar prompts but hurt familiar ones, so its complete package still failed the advancement gate. ## Next comparisons

If learning in an environment remains the main line, confirm V201 on fresh worlds, then let two actions await consequences at once. That tests whether the learner can assign credit when the most recent action may be the wrong one. In parallel, V206 gives QA a clean comparison. Fix the address that now works and vary who decides when to write. The open question in both tracks is how MiniFly selects the right earlier state when several are available.
