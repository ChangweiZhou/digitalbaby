Fly Brain Learning Mechanisms: Evidence Map and Verification Synthesis
Executive summary
Download the full long-form Markdown report
The accompanying report contains 70 distinct proposed learning mechanisms, intentionally below the requested cap of 200. Each entry records the mechanism, mechanistic level, implicated brain region, molecular players, original experimental evidence, follow-up verification status, approximate first-proposal date, experimental methods, and representative primary or official-source links. Using the report's explicit evidence rubric, the catalogue contains 31 “verified,” 33 “partially verified,” 2 “disputed/revised,” and 4 “untested” mechanisms. Those labels are evidence-synthesis judgments rather than claims of scientific finality.
The dominant, best-verified account of associative learning in Drosophila is a compartmental mushroom-body learning architecture. Sparse sensory activity in Kenyon cells (KCs) is combined with reinforcement signals from anatomically defined dopaminergic neurons (DANs); KC activity and dopamine engage receptor–rutabaga–cAMP/PKA signaling; plasticity changes transmission from KCs to mushroom-body output neurons (MBONs); and the altered balance among MBON outputs biases learned approach or avoidance. Direct synaptic physiology, targeted neuronal activation/silencing, behavioral conditioning, molecular genetics, and serial-EM connectomics converge on this framework. Hige and colleagues provided direct evidence for long-lasting heterosynaptic plasticity at fly MB output synapses, while Aso and colleagues established the compartmental MBON architecture and its relationship to learned valence. [1]
The molecular foundation of that model reaches back much further. Classic dunce and rutabaga genetics established cAMP signaling as central to fly learning; the rutabaga product was subsequently identified as a Ca²⁺/calmodulin-responsive adenylyl cyclase. Modern work has filled in the physiological link between those genetics and synaptic storage: dopamine and KC activity interact nonlinearly in the MB, and direct electrophysiology published in 2024 showed that cAMP–PKA activation at KC output synapses can produce presynaptic long-term depression, resolving the otherwise counterintuitive fact that a pathway often associated with potentiation is required for an associative trace expressed as synaptic weakening. [2]
Dopamine is not simply “the reward transmitter.” Defined PAM neurons can provide appetitive reinforcement, whereas PPL1 channels prominently support aversive reinforcement, and different PAM populations distinguish aspects such as sweet taste and nutrient value. Dopamine also participates in forgetting: Berry and colleagues showed that ongoing DAN activity controls memory decay and distinguished dDA1/DopR1-biased acquisition from DAMB/Dop1R2-dependent forgetting. Later work identified a dopamine circuit capable of temporarily suppressing retrieval without destroying the long-term memory. [3]
Long-term persistence adds another layer. CREB-dependent transcription, protein-synthesis-dependent consolidation after spaced training, DPM–MB recurrent interactions, and the Orb2/CPEB system all contribute to memory lasting beyond the fast KC→MBON trace. Orb2 is particularly notable because behavioral genetics linking it to persistent memory was followed by evidence that amyloid-like Orb2 oligomers are required for persistence, and later structural work supported the concept of a functional neuronal amyloid. [4]
The field's modern picture is therefore multi-site and recurrent rather than a single-synapse Hebbian model. Serial-EM reconstructions reveal repeated KC–DAN–MBON compartments together with KC→DAN, MBON/DAN feedback, and other recurrent pathways; direct experiments show reciprocal KC→DAN signaling can be required for normal learning. Moreover, postsynaptic nicotinic-receptor plasticity in MBONs means that the historically strong presynaptic KC-output model is not the entire story. [5]
Research scope and methods
The literature search covered all years through September 18, 2026 and deliberately crossed discovery systems rather than relying on one bibliographic index. PubMed/NCBI was used for original biomedical papers and bibliographic verification; bioRxiv for recent preprints and preprint histories; arXiv for computational/connectome-constrained proposals; web-indexed Google Scholar author and citation records for citation chaining and discovery; publisher sites for original full-text records; and laboratory/institutional websites for publication-list cross-checking. Google Scholar results were used as discovery/citation triangulation rather than treated as a reproducible bibliographic database because its public web interface is not equivalent to a stable API. Relevant Scholar records surfaced, for example, work by Aso, Tomchik, Davidson, Cervantes-Sandoval, Huetteroth and Berry. [6]
Key laboratory/official-site checks included the Ronald Davis laboratory, whose program explicitly spans acquisition, consolidation, retrieval and active forgetting, as well as Berry-lab, Hige-lab and Brembs-lab materials. These lab pages were used primarily to locate and cross-check original publications, not as substitutes for the papers themselves. [7]
Representative search families included Drosophila learning memory mechanism mushroom body, Kenyon cell MBON dopamine plasticity, rutabaga cAMP learning, DopR1 DAMB forgetting, PAM reward PPL1 aversive, DPM consolidation, Orb2 long-term memory, Rac forgetting, postsynaptic plasticity nicotinic MBON, central complex visual pattern memory, operant self-learning PKC FoxP, olfactory habituation GABA, and date-focused 2025–2026 queries in bioRxiv and arXiv. The arXiv search captured, among other items, a 2025 connectome-informed analysis of how KC→MBON structural changes affect modeled learning capacity and a 2026 preprint proposing multisensory expansion of the memory engram. [8]
A mechanism was retained when it specified a causal or computational process—molecule, pathway, synaptic update rule, identified circuit operation, consolidation/forgetting process, or explicit learning computation—and had an identifiable Drosophila experiment or a fly-connectome-constrained model. Preference was given to claims tested with behavioral learning, loss-of-function, rescue, sufficiency, temporally restricted perturbation, physiological recording, or direct synaptic measurement. Descriptive expression differences, generic sensory physiology, mechanisms demonstrated only in other insects, and computational work that merely borrowed insect inspiration without making a biological fly-learning claim were excluded.
Verification was scored on four dimensions: independent replication (0–3), causal perturbation (0–3), behavioral relevance (0–2), and physiological/synaptic readout (0–2). In the report, “verified” generally requires a total around 8/10 or higher plus meaningful orthogonal or independent support. “Partially verified” denotes strong but narrower evidence, limited independent replication, or uncertain generality. “Disputed/revised” is reserved for an original formulation materially changed by later work. “Untested” is used mainly for recent preprint/computational proposals awaiting causal follow-up.

Several of these mechanisms are nested rather than competing explanations. For example, rutabaga coincidence detection, cAMP–PKA signaling, presynaptic LTD, and MBON reweighting describe molecular, synaptic, and circuit levels of the same causal chain. Conversely, Rac/DAMB forgetting and CREB/Orb2 consolidation operate on memory dynamics after acquisition rather than explaining the initial KC→MBON update.
Key revisions and emerging mechanisms
One of the clearest historical revisions concerns octopamine versus dopamine. Early work encouraged a convenient transmitter-level division in which octopamine mediated appetitive reinforcement and dopamine aversive reinforcement. Subsequent identified-neuron experiments showed that dopamine itself is essential and sufficient in major appetitive pathways, while octopamine can act upstream of or tune particular dopamine circuits. The 2026 PNAS study of larval learning extends that picture by describing selective octopaminergic modulation across MB dopaminergic channels; because it was preceded by a 2025 bioRxiv version and is very recent, the report classifies the specific mechanism as only partially verified despite publication in a peer-reviewed journal. [24]
A second revision concerns the site of the associative trace. The evidence for presynaptic KC-output depression is exceptionally strong, but a strict “all storage is presynaptic” interpretation is no longer adequate. Postsynaptic plasticity experiments show that nicotinic receptor composition and dynamics in MBONs can participate in induction and expression of associative memory, while bouton-resolved measurements show substantial heterogeneity in dopamine-dependent presynaptic plasticity. The likely modern interpretation is therefore a multi-component synapse embedded in a recurrent circuit, not a single uniform presynaptic scalar weight. [25]
A third conceptual revision is that forgetting need not equal erasure. Berry et al. established an active dopamine-dependent process that controls memory decay, but Sabandal, Berry and Davis subsequently identified a PPL1-α2α′2/DAMB mechanism that can temporarily suppress retrieval while leaving long-term memory capable of returning. Behavioral failure at one test time therefore cannot automatically be interpreted as disappearance of the stored trace. [26]
Electrical coupling is another less prominent but mechanistically interesting branch. APL and DPM neurons form heterotypic gap junctions involving different innexins; cell-specific knockdown selectively disrupted anesthesia-sensitive memory while sparing anesthesia-resistant memory in the original study. Separate work found that gap-junction networks in mushroom bodies contribute to visual learning. These findings are well supported within their original paradigms but have a smaller independent-replication base than cAMP/dopamine mechanisms, so they are classified as partially verified. [27]
The most consequential new peer-reviewed result located in the 2026 literature is the Gr43a internal sugar-sensor consolidation mechanism. Francés and colleagues reported that brain sugar-sensing neurons participate not only in fasted appetitive memory involving sucrose but also in consolidation of aversive memory in fed flies, indicating that an interoceptive metabolic circuit can be recruited by ostensibly non-food learning. The paper was published in Nature on March 25, 2026. Its causal evidence is strong, but independent replication cannot yet be expected on this timescale, so the report assigns “partially verified” rather than “verified.” [28]
Among preprints, Okray and colleagues' 2026 work is particularly notable because it proposes cross-modal engram expansion: combined color–odor learning recruits visually selective KCs into an olfactory memory representation, with DPM transmission required for enhancement and DopR1 signaling in APL implicated in a disinhibitory bridge. Because this was located as a 2026 arXiv preprint rather than a mature independently replicated mechanism, it remains explicitly marked untested in the catalogue. [29]
A 2025 arXiv study by Xie and Ocker instead asks a computational question: how does KC→MBON structural connectivity constrain learnability? Their connectome-informed simulations predict that MBONs with few KC inputs perform worse in odor classification and that ablation/pruning of developmentally mature KCs is particularly damaging. This is useful as a mechanistic hypothesis generator, but it remains an in silico prediction until corresponding biological perturbations test those specific structural predictions. [30]

The report also includes three Mermaid visualizations: a historical timeline from the first dunce genetics through the 2026 results; a mechanistic flowchart linking sensory coding, DAN teaching signals, rutabaga/cAMP, presynaptic and postsynaptic plasticity, MBON action selection, consolidation and forgetting; and a condensed verification chain showing how genetics, direct physiology, connectomics and newer receptor-plasticity work progressively changed the canonical MB model.
The connectomic context is especially important. Takemura and colleagues reconstructed all neurons and synaptic interactions in adult MB α-lobe compartments, and subsequent adult-MB reconstruction work expanded the picture of matched DAN/MBON compartments and recurrent pathways. These anatomical datasets strongly constrain learning models, but anatomy alone does not establish synaptic sign, plasticity direction, or learning causality; the highest-ranked mechanisms are therefore those for which connectomics is paired with manipulation and functional physiology. [31]
Direct physiology remains the most important discriminator among competing molecular models. The 2015 Hige study established long-term heterosynaptic plasticity at the MB output site; 2023 work showed dopamine-dependent presynaptic plasticity is heterogeneous among boutons; and 2024 Yamada–Davidson–Hige experiments used electrophysiology, pharmacology and optogenetics to show that cAMP–PKA activation can directly induce presynaptic LTD and that cyclic-nucleotide pathways can support bidirectional long-term plasticity. Together, these studies make the KC-output synapse one of the unusually well-characterized associative plasticity sites in an intact genetically accessible brain. [32]
The resulting synthesis is that fly learning should no longer be represented as a single equation such as “odor + dopamine → weaker KC synapse.” The better-supported 2026 formulation is sparse sensory eligibility + local neuromodulatory teaching + compartment-specific pre/postsynaptic plasticity + recurrent feedback + state-dependent consolidation/forgetting. The canonical dopamine–cAMP–KC→MBON rule remains the center of gravity, but the empirical trend is toward multiple local rules operating on different time scales and under different behavioral states. [33]
