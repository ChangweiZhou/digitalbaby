# Round 6 and V195 reconciliation

The recovered Round 6 source and the V195 preflight answer different questions. The recovered long-life learner has a 256-column fact head and scores the argmax once after a supplied query cue. Period and space following the answer are observed by the general predictor, but their fact-teach marker is zero. The fact head never learns or predicts a separate EOS. V195 trains the answer and then EOS in the same 257-column bag head. The answer-site and EOS-site prefixes share n-grams, so those updates interfere in the reconstruction.

The saved controlled preflight holds its own synthetic keys, presentation schedule and storage fixed. On the two trained wrappers it yields 16/16 and 16/16 first-byte recall without EOS training, versus 0/16 and 0/16 with EOS training. All 32 EOS-trained queries stopped before emitting a byte. One held wrapper was less affected (12/16 first bytes correct), illustrating that the interference depends on the prefix.

Other differences matter for a future replication. Round 6 uses 12 presentations per new or corrected association and scores one held template per key by alternating between two templates. V195 proposes four presentations and scores both held templates for each key. Round 6 uses digit labels and its own fixed hash. V195's clean fixture used 16 letter labels and a Blake2b hash. The recovered Round 6 large bank is float32, shape 65,536 by 256, or 64 MiB for the fact readout alone. The memo's 32 MiB float16 figure is a storage projection borrowed from a different experiment. The recorded large-bank commands do not switch dtype. V195 actually uses float16 for persistent weights.

The Round 6 percentages are therefore evidence for cued, byte-only retention and correction in its source setup. They give no own-EOS score. The V195 preflight is a negative result for its common EOS adapter, and no official V195 comparison of reopening or wrong-winner decay occurred. The later session event 1828 starts Round 7 variants and does not show completed results for them in this export.

The two Round 6 source files were recovered by static reconstruction of the recorded heredoc and literal edits. Both parse. The event manifest identifies the reconstruction steps. The export records completed Round 6 jobs and a publication command, but the complete redirected per-seed JSON is not included in the extracted artifact. No recovered model was executed in this reconciliation.

To bridge the discrepancy, first replay the source-matched byte-only parent with its original presentation count, hash and precision. Use the same keys and wrappers for a paired native-EOS comparison. Give the continuation site an address feature distinguishable from the query prefix, derived from the model's own emitted byte and the supplied query start. Teach EOS on actual boundary observations and still let the model choose from all 257 classes. Qualify first-byte preservation and own EOS before any correction comparison. Keep a dtype comparison separate from the EOS interface change.
