## What changes scientifically

The native learner can produce complete taught NEW answers under repetition. Clustering sometimes makes more cues correct at their individual last-teaching times. But at the final matched dose, the native W schedule contrast is only 3→4 of16 in one world and1→1 in the other. Both schedules give exactly the same held counts, with no OLD-prior causal advantage and no advantage over the declared countermodels. This does not rescue the addition objective or establish schedule equivalence.

Earlier OLD value writes reduce NEW acquisition relative to matched OLD-write inhibition at the final endpoint in both worlds. That is an observed conditional effect of this learning episode, not proof that all pretraining is harmful or that any arithmetic relationship was first acquired and then negatively transferred.

Retention is now a real measured problem in one fresh world: five initially correct OLD answers are absent at the final/delayed boundaries under both schedules. V156's15/15 retained subset must remain scoped to its earlier states and shorter/different continuations. Conversely, the other V157 world began with no correct OLD answers, so it supplies no initially-acquired retention denominator. The new result is not universal forgetting.

The sampled current-write controls establish some local write damage. They do not separate time, sensory evolution and competing teaching over every lost item's entire subsequent history. Do not infer that simply adding replay, consolidation or availability gating will solve the task. Last-teaching-to-final loss also has recency and time confounds.
